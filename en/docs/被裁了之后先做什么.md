# What to do first after being laid off

What has to be done after a layoff or a dismissal is spread across section 19, section 7 and several other sections. It is put in order of time below. Each step only says what to do. The details, the numbers and the reasoning are all in the item pointed at just before the brackets. If you have only been underpaid and are still working, go straight to item 2 in section 7 (if your wages are owed to you, complain to labour inspection first).

## The same day, before you sign anything

1. If the company hands you a form saying 「resignation for personal reasons」, do not sign it. Sign it and the economic compensation and the unemployment insurance benefit usually both go with it. See item 7 in section 19 (do not sign 「resignation for personal reasons」).
2. If the company says 「sign first so the paperwork moves along, the compensation comes separately」, ask for the compensation to be written into the agreement on the spot. If they will not write it down, do not sign. Not signing on the day carries no legal consequence. See item 7 in section 19 (do not sign 「resignation for personal reasons」).
3. Turn on the recorder during the conversation. For a conversation you are party to, you do not need the other person's consent first. See item 41 in section 8 (turn on the recorder for phone calls and meetings that could turn).
4. Before you hand back the computer and the accounts, save your payslips, attendance records, employment contract, social insurance record and chat logs. See item 8 in section 19 (before you leave, save your payslips, attendance records, employment contract).
5. Save only the documents relating to your own employment. Do not take the company's source code, customer lists or technical documents away. See item 7 in section 11 (do not take source code, customer lists or technical documents away when you leave).
6. Hand over your account access. Do not delete the data and do not change the password, even if the company owes you money. See item 6 in section 11 (hand over your account access when you leave).

## The same day to the first few days: work out the money

1. Work out N first: one month's wage for each full year, and half a month for under six months. See item 4 in section 19 (work out N before a dismissal).
2. The monthly wage is worked out from the average over the 12 months before you leave. Bonuses, allowances and subsidies all count in. This is the part a company is most likely to cut. See item 4 in section 19 (work out N before a dismissal).
3. If the company did not give you 30 days' written notice, it has to pay one month's wage on top of N. Put that month down separately when you negotiate. See item 5 in section 19 (if the company dismisses you without giving 30 days' notice).
4. If the company terminated the contract unlawfully, the compensation is 2N. Ask legal aid or a lawyer whether it was unlawful before you decide whether to negotiate or go to arbitration. See item 6 in section 19 (the compensation is twice the economic compensation standard).
5. Annual leave you did not get through is settled off when you leave, worked out by how much of the year you have already done. See item 2 in section 19 (annual leave is 5, 10 or 15 days according to total length of service).
6. Add the unpaid overtime pay to the same figure. See item 1 in section 19 (overtime pay runs in three bands at 1.5, 2 and 3 times).

## The first week

1. Go and register as unemployed. The unemployment insurance benefit is counted from the day you register, so a month late means a month less. See item 1 in section 7 (if you have lost your job, apply online first for unemployment insurance benefit).
2. The registration is done at a public employment service office such as an employment service centre. It is free. See item 5 in section 7 (to find work, start with free public employment services).
3. Apply for the unemployment insurance benefit on the 「掌上 12333」 app or the electronic social insurance card mini-programme. You can only get it if you have paid in for a year and you did not resign of your own accord. See item 1 in section 7 (if you have lost your job, apply online first for unemployment insurance benefit).
4. While you are on the unemployment insurance benefit, the unemployment insurance pays your employee medical insurance. You do not have to enrol separately. See item 1 in section 7 (if you have lost your job, apply online first for unemployment insurance benefit).
5. If you cannot afford to sue, telephone 12348 and apply for legal aid. You no longer have to go back to your home district for a certificate of financial difficulty. See item 3 in section 7 (if you cannot afford to sue, apply for legal aid).
6. If you signed a non-compete, keep the original. The company has to pay the compensation monthly, and if it does not pay for 3 months for reasons of its own you can ask for it to be terminated. See item 12 in section 11 (if you have signed a non-compete).
7. Fix your daily routine: get up at the same time, put the job search into the diary at fixed times, and do not spend the whole day at home. See item 3 in section 29 (after losing your job, fix your daily routine).

## The first month

1. If wages or overtime pay are being withheld, telephone 12333 and complain to labour inspection first, then apply for labour arbitration. Neither route costs anything. See item 2 in section 7 (if your wages are owed to you, complain to labour inspection first).
2. If an unlicensed workshop employed you, go to labour inspection and arbitration all the same. Money owed by a foreman is chased to the main contractor. Only work done privately for a household or an individual goes to court. See item 22 in section 7 (if you are owed work pay, first find out who employed you).
3. The labour arbitration has to be started within 1 year of leaving. After that the other side only has to say 「too late」. See item 19 in section 8 (1 year for labour arbitration).
4. To find work, start with the public employment service offices. Job placement and career guidance are both free. Do not go to an agency that charges. See item 5 in section 7 (to find work, start with free public employment services).
5. Do not only send out resumes when you job hunt. Learn the technique, set a definite target, and ask people you know to pass your name on. See item 14 in section 7 (when job hunting, do not only send out resumes; use a system).
6. If you hear 「pay first, then start work」, 「train first, then a job is arranged」 or 「recruit people」, refuse on the spot. See item 15 in section 7 (do not sign a "training loan").
7. If you want to learn a skill, look up the local human resources and social security bureau's 「vocational training subsidy catalogue」 first. Do not pay for a course yourself. See item 13 in section 7 (while unemployed, go and claim the vocational training subsidy).
8. See whether you qualify for recognition as a person with difficulty finding work, for example on grounds of age or long-term unemployment. Once you have it, your own social insurance contributions carry a subsidy, and you can also queue for a public-interest post. See item 12 in section 7 (after registering as unemployed, seek recognition as a person with difficulty finding work).

## The months after that

1. Cover living costs out of the emergency fund first. Do not borrow, and do not sell investments at a loss. See item 27 in section 5 (save 3 to 6 months of living costs as an emergency fund).
2. In the first three months, put every irreversible big decision off. Selling your flat, investing the whole severance payment in one go, going into a partnership and standing guarantor for someone all count. See item 12 in section 29 (in the first three months after a shock, put every irreversible big decision off).
3. If the unemployment insurance benefit has run out and you still have no work, do not let the medical insurance lapse. You can pay for resident medical insurance yourself year by year, but after a lapse there is a 3-month period during which treatment is not reimbursed. See item 9 in section 7 (do not let the 400 yuan a year of resident medical insurance lapse).
4. A gap in the pension does not reset it to zero. It is counted by the years you have accumulated. Do not pay somebody to keep it going for you. See item 18 in section 7 (do not panic when social insurance contributions lapse).
5. If the household's income falls below the local means-tested income support line, apply for it. See item 7 in section 7 (if your income is below the local means-tested income support line, apply for means-tested income support).
6. If you cannot hold on and you want somebody to talk to, telephone 12356. It is free. See item 11 in section 29 (to have somebody to talk to, call 12356).
7. For money you have still not got back, watch the one-year limit counted from the day you left. See item 19 in section 8 (1 year for labour arbitration).
