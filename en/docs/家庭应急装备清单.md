# Household emergency kit checklist: what to buy, where to put it, how often to check it

This is the long-form version of item 26 in section 1 of the README (fit fire extinguishers, fire blankets, escape respirators and a first-aid kit). This list says what a household should keep on hand. It does not say how to use any of it. What to do in the moment in a fire, in cardiac arrest, with major bleeding, with a scald or with carbon monoxide poisoning is all in section 13. How to choose and how to fit smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms, see item 3 in section 1. Gas hoses and stoves, see item 4 in section 1.

This section works out the bill of fewer deaths and fewer injuries. It covers only harm caused by accidents, not illness. How much use the equipment itself is, the evidence is weak. The reason is in the last section, so read that paragraph before going any further.

## 2. The three fire items

**Fire extinguisher.** A handheld fire extinguisher is a product subject to mandatory national certification. A compliant cylinder has the CCC mark printed on it. The standard it is made to is GB 4351—2023 Handheld Fire Extinguishers. The kind that comes pre-pressurised (carbon dioxide extinguishers excepted) has a pressure gauge on the cylinder. The needle on the gauge has to sit inside the green zone. If the needle points into the red or into the yellow, this extinguisher can no longer be used. How many years before it is written off is worked out under the XF 95 standard: 6 years for water-based, 10 years for dry powder, 12 years for carbon dioxide. The period runs from the day it left the factory. The date of manufacture is stamped into the metal of the cylinder. For home use, buy ABC dry powder. Put one at the kitchen doorway and one in the entrance hall. Do not push it into the cabinet directly above the cooker. If there is a real fire, you cannot reach up there.

**Fire blanket.** In the official list it sits in the same item as the fire extinguisher. It has two uses: covering a pan of oil that is burning, or wrapping it round yourself to get out. One in a kitchen drawer is enough. It takes up no space and it does not go out of date. If the pan catches fire, turn off the heat first, then lay the blanket over it. Once you have laid it down, do not lift it again straight away.

**Escape respirator.** Its official name is a fire-filtering self-rescue breathing apparatus. The standard it is made to is GB 21976.7—2012 Building Fire Escape and Refuge Equipment, Part 7: Filtering Fire Self-rescue Breathing Apparatus. This is a mandatory national standard that is in force and valid. It took effect on 1 June 2013, and the review in 2023 concluded that it stays valid. When you buy one, look for this standard number, and look for the CCC mark as well. Buy as many as there are people in the house. Keep them by the head of the bed where you can reach them with your hand, and do not lock them in a storage room. The filter canister inside soaks up the toxic gas in the smoke. One use and it is finished, and it only holds for the time printed on it, so put it on and get out. Smoke is what kills most people in a fire. How to get out (stay low and crawl, feel the door before opening it, take the stairs and not the lift), see item 24 in section 13. Smoke alarms, see item 3 in section 1. Both of these come before the three items above.

## 3. What goes in the first-aid kit

On this item the official list says only one sentence: 「外用药品：止血粉、止血贴、纱布绷带等，用于处理伤口」, which means topical medicines such as haemostatic powder, adhesive wound dressings, gauze and bandages and the like, for treating wounds. Section 13 has already worked out which actions belong on the spot. Given those actions, the following in a household first-aid kit is reasonable.

- Stopping bleeding: one windlass tourniquet, which is the kind with a twist rod that tightens. Add sterile gauze and bandages, a triangular bandage and medical tape. How to use a tourniquet, when not to use one, and why you should not loosen it and let the blood flow, see item 12 in section 13
- Cleaning a wound: iodine swabs, saline, sticking plasters, sterile dressings, disposable gloves, scissors and tweezers
- Burns and scalds: no ointment at all. On the spot there is one thing to do, run cool tap water over it for 20 minutes, see item 14 in section 13 (run cool running water over a scald for 20 minutes)
- Records: prepare one card. Write down the allergies, the chronic illnesses and the regular medicines of everyone in the house. When the paramedics arrive, hand them the card

If someone in the house already knows they have a severe allergy, keep an adrenaline auto-injector as well. It is a prescription medicine, so a doctor has to prescribe it. See item 15 in section 13 (how to recognise anaphylactic shock and how to give the injection).

This first-aid kit list is grade C evidence. Grade C means it is written from experience and consensus, with no direct literature behind it. The reason is that the official document gives only the four characters 「外用药品」 on this item. What to buy specifically is worked backwards from the first-aid actions this book has already checked.

## 4. Lighting, communication and calling for help

For the torch, follow the official description and buy one that is waterproof and explosion-proof. Charge it every so often, or change the battery. The survival whistle is for sending a signal continuously when you are trapped. Blowing a whistle takes less effort than shouting with all your might, and the sound carries through a noisy place more easily. The radio is for receiving emergency broadcasts. The Beijing version of the list also lists an emergency escape rope and multi-function combination scissors. The best value in this group is a power bank for your phone, and most households already have one.

## 5. Check once a year, ten minutes

- Fire extinguisher: check that the needle on the pressure gauge is still in the green zone. Then look at the date of manufacture on the cylinder and work out whether it has reached its write-off age
- Smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms: press the test button. Change the battery once a year, see item 3 in section 1 (fit smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms)
- Respirators: check whether the packaging is damaged and whether they are past the shelf life printed on them
- First-aid kit: check whether any medicine or dressing has expired, and whether the clip on the tourniquet still locks
- Torch and power bank: check whether there is still charge in them

The Beijing Municipal Emergency Management Bureau puts it this way: 「定期对应急物资状况进行检查，并及时更换已过保质期的应急物资」, which means check the condition of emergency supplies at regular intervals and replace anything past its shelf life promptly. You can fix this to the day you change the alarm batteries each year and go through all of it at once.

## 6. Things you do not need to buy

- **A home AED (automated external defibrillator).** One costs more than ten thousand yuan. There is also no evidence to support a household buying one of its own. In cardiac arrest the thing to do is start compressions at once, get someone to dial 120, and send someone to fetch the AED from the nearest public place, see item 1 in section 13
- **"Fire masks" and "gas masks" with no CCC mark.** Article 24 of the Fire Protection Law says that fire products within the scope of mandatory product certification 「由具有法定资质的认证机构按照国家标准、行业标准的强制性要求认证合格后，方可生产、销售、使用」, which means may only be produced, sold and used once they have passed certification by a certification body with the legal qualifications, according to the mandatory requirements of national and industry standards. In other words, something that has not passed certification can be neither sold nor used. Also, the filter canisters used in industry are for toxic substances in a workshop, which is a different thing from smoke in a fire
- **A packaged "emergency kit".** These kits are mostly padded out with a cheap torch and a mask. Buying the fire extinguisher, the fire blanket, the respirator and the first-aid kit separately costs less, and it is easier to check the certification one item at a time
- **Food and water for more than three days.** The official list works it out at 3 days per person. Most of what you stack up beyond that ends up thrown away after it expires, see item 24 in section 5 (do not stockpile for sales promotions)

## 7. How much use this equipment really is

The evidence in this section is weaker than for the other items in section 1 of this book.

One Cochrane systematic review pooled 98 studies and 2,605,044 people. It assessed whether giving households safety education and safety equipment actually helps. The result is that these interventions may bring the rate of injuries down a little, IRR 0.89 (95% CI 0.78 to 1.01), about 10% lower. But the top of that credible range crosses 1, and 1 stands for no difference, so there may be no difference at all. The ones delivered in the home did better, IRR 0.75 (95% CI 0.62 to 0.91), about 25% lower. The ones that only taught and handed out nothing, IRR 0.78 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.92), about 20% lower. What seems to do the work is teaching in the home and modifying the home, not the act of handing things out. The review also has one conclusion: there is no evidence that these interventions reduce burns and scalds or poisoning.

In the same review, these interventions did improve people's safety habits. More households had a working smoke alarm fitted, OR 1.81 (95% CI 1.30 to 2.52), a share about 80% higher. More had worked out an escape plan in advance, OR 2.01 (1.45 to 2.77), about double. More stored their medicines properly, OR 1.53 (1.27 to 1.84), about 50% higher. More had a fire door fitted at the head of the stairs, OR 1.61 (1.19 to 2.17), about 60% higher.

Sources: Kendrick D 等 (2012). Home safety education and provision of safety equipment for injury prevention. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005014.pub3>

So buying everything on the official list in one go costs little and counts as reasonable preparation. But do not think that having bought it you are safe. Research has repeatedly shown that three things work: fitting smoke alarms, working out in advance how to get out, and moving out of the way the things in the home that can hurt people. Either none of these three is on a shopping list, or it takes up only a small part of one.
