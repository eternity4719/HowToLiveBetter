# Which licences a platform needs: a comparison table and a decision table for choosing a server

This long-form essay goes with section 26 of the README. It holds three tables and a few notes on the points that are easiest to get wrong. The items themselves and the sources are in the README. How to register a company and how to file tax, see section 12 (starting a business). The lines that technical staff employed by others should not cross, see section 11 (programmers and tech workers).

## 1. First work out which kind of business you are in

A single site often touches on several kinds of business at once. Touch on several kinds and you need several licences. Picking one and going no further does not work.

| What you are doing | The business category it falls under | What you need | The main legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| An information site that takes no money, a personal blog, a company website | Non-operating internet information services | An ICP filing. It is not a licence, it is telling the authority you are about to open a site | Article 4 of the Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services |
| Membership, value-added services and paid content that charge users | Operating internet information services | A Value-Added Telecommunications Business Operating Licence (information services business). What it covers is your charging users | Articles 3, 4 and 7 of the same Measures |
| Introducing buyers to sellers and handling transactions and orders | Online data processing and transaction processing services | A Value-Added Telecommunications Business Operating Licence (B21). What it covers is your handling transactions on behalf of both sides | B21 of the Catalogue of Telecommunications Business Categories (2015 edition) |
| Live streaming with hosts on camera, game live streaming | Online performance | A Network Culture Business Licence whose scope includes online performance. Without it your site cannot put hosts on camera | Article 4 of the Measures for the Administration of Online Performance Business Operations |
| Making your own video programmes, or collecting programmes from elsewhere, or letting users upload video to your site | Internet audio-visual programme services | a Licence for the Distribution of Audio-Visual Programmes through Information Networks. What it covers is broadcasting video programmes on the site | Articles 7 and 8 of the Provisions on the Administration of Internet Audio-Visual Programme Services |
| Selling goods in a live stream | Online live-stream marketing | The licences in the rows above. On top of that you have to verify the merchants and keep records | Article 8 of the Measures for the Administration of Online Live-Stream Marketing (Trial) |
| Running news and information | Internet news information services | an Internet News Information Service Licence. Without it your site cannot publish news | Article 5 of the Provisions on the Administration of Internet Live-Streaming Services |
| Building your own server room and selling machines and bandwidth | Internet data centre services, internet access services | A Value-Added Telecommunications Business Operating Licence (B11, B14). What it covers is your selling server space and bandwidth to other people | B11 and B14 of the Catalogue of Telecommunications Business Categories (2015 edition) |

Which licence goes with which kind of live streaming is set out most plainly in the guiding opinions of the seven departments in 2021: 「开展经营性网络表演活动的直播平台须持有《网络文化经营许可证》并进行ICP备案；开展网络视听节目服务的直播平台须持有《信息网络传播视听节目许可证》（或在全国网络视听平台信息登记管理系统中完成登记）并进行ICP备案；开展互联网新闻信息服务的直播平台须持有《互联网新闻信息服务许可证》。」, which means a live-streaming platform carrying out operating online performance activities must hold a Network Culture Business Licence and complete an ICP filing; a live-streaming platform carrying out internet audio-visual programme services must hold a Licence for the Distribution of Audio-Visual Programmes through Information Networks (or complete registration in the national audio-visual platform information registration system) and complete an ICP filing; a live-streaming platform carrying out internet news information services must hold an Internet News Information Service Licence.

There are three kinds of live streaming. If hosts perform on it, you get a Network Culture Business Licence. If it carries audio-visual programmes, you get a Licence for the Distribution of Audio-Visual Programmes through Information Networks, or you complete registration in the national audio-visual platform information registration system. If it carries news, you get an Internet News Information Service Licence. The first two also both have to do the ICP filing.

### Three points that are easy to get wrong

**An individual cannot get a value-added telecommunications licence.** The first of the conditions says 「经营者为依法设立的公司」, which means the operator is a company duly established. You need a company first, and an individual does not get one by turning up with an identity card. If you operate only inside one province, the registered capital of the company cannot be below 1,000,000 yuan. If you operate across provinces, it cannot be below 10,000,000 yuan. After the papers go in, the review period is 60 days, and the licence is valid for 5 years. If you want to run a business that charges people, you have to set the company up first. How to set up a company, see section 12 (starting a business).

**A private company basically cannot get the audio-visual programme licence.** The condition says 「具备法人资格，为国有独资或国有控股单位」, which means it has the status of a legal person and is a wholly state-owned or state-controlled entity. In other words this licence only goes to units the state puts money into or controls. So an individual founder making long videos or producing their own programmes does not get it. If you want to do live streaming, the licence to get is the Network Culture Business Licence.

**No official document says in so many words that "an e-commerce platform must get an EDI licence".** EDI is the licence in row B21 of the table above, and its formal name is online data processing and transaction processing services. The handling guide from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology says only one thing: 「按照业务界定申请相应的电信业务经营许可」, which means apply for the telecommunications business operating licence that corresponds to how the business is defined. The ministry has also given separate replies twice. A ride-hailing platform only has to file the website, and a platform for rights and interests or for bulk commodity trading only has to file the website as well. So this essay only repeats the definition of B21. Whether your line of business needs it is for you and the local communications administration to decide. Before you start the paperwork, telephone the local communications administration once and ask.

## 2. The platform's own day-to-day duties

Holding a licence only means you are allowed to open up. What follows is what you have to do every day once you have opened up. How much you can be fined for failing to do it is written up in the items in section 26.

| Duty | The hard requirement | Where it is written |
|---|---|---|
| Check and register the merchants selling on your platform | Re-check and update at least once every six months | Article 24 of the Measures for the Supervision of Online Transactions |
| Report the merchants' identity information | Report it to the market regulation authority in January and July each year | Article 25 of the same Measures |
| Report the tax-related information | Report it to the tax authority within the month after the end of each quarter | Article 4 of the Provisions on the Reporting of Tax-Related Information by Internet Platform Enterprises |
| Keep records of transactions | No less than three years from the day the transaction completed | Article 31 of the E-Commerce Law |
| Keep records of live-stream content and logs | Sixty days | Article 16 of the Provisions on the Administration of Internet Live-Streaming Services |
| Keep recordings of online performances | No less than sixty days | Article 13 of the Measures for the Administration of Online Performance Business Operations |
| Keep network logs | No less than six months | Article 23, item 3, of the Cybersecurity Law |
| Handle notices of infringement | Pass the merchant's statement to the complainant. If fifteen days pass with nothing further, restore it | Article 43 of the E-Commerce Law |
| Provide a complaints and reports entry point | Put it in a prominent place and make it easy to use | Article 16 of the Provisions on the Governance of the Ecological Environment of Network Information Content |

There are four retention periods and they are counted separately. Transaction records for three years, live-stream content for sixty days, network logs for six months. A merchant's identity information on the platform is kept for three years from the day they leave the platform. When you design the storage plan, design it for the longest of these, not the shortest.

## 3. Choosing a server: how to pick between the three tiers

Answer these questions first, and only then compare prices.

| Question | If the answer is | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Could you stand the site being down for a day | Yes | The cheapest VPS (virtual server) is enough |
| Do you have users signing up, trading and uploading | Yes | Use a cloud host from one of the mainstream cloud providers. Pick one where you can back up the whole machine at any time and add configuration temporarily |
| Is there a named person looking after the server | No | Do not put the whole machine in a colocation rack |
| Is bandwidth or hardware your largest running cost | Yes, and there is a named person looking after it | Only then consider putting the whole machine in a colocation rack |

**A small provider is not unusable, but check it first.** Putting machines in a colocation rack and giving other people access to the internet are two things that need a licence in themselves. They belong to value-added telecommunications business. To check, open the telecommunications business market information system of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology at tsm.miit.gov.cn and look the company up once by its full name. If no licence comes up, rule the provider out. The risk in the ones that are half the price usually comes in three forms: the machines have been oversold, the owner disappears, or the upstream is cut off. If something does go wrong and the provider is licensed, you can still complain to the communications administration. If it is not licensed, you do not even know who to appeal to.

**In China or outside it.** Put the server in China and you have to file. An access provider is not allowed to give access to a site that has not filed. Putting it outside China does let you get round the filing. But your users are in China, and the money is taken in China. None of the platform duties set out in items 5 to 10 in section 26 drops away. Putting the server outside China also adds one more layer of cost, which is data export. Sending the personal information of users in China to a machine outside China is what is called an export. To export, one of the four conditions listed in Article 38 of the Personal Information Protection Law has to be met. You also have to get the consent of the person separately, and it cannot be buried in a bundle of terms for someone to click through. How many people are involved is counted from 1 January of that year onwards. Below 100,000 people, none of the three routes below is needed. From 100,000 to 1,000,000 people, either sign the standard contract or go for the certification. Above 1,000,000 people, you have to file a security assessment.

**Backups.** Keep backups in at least two places, and do not put both copies with the same provider in the same region. There is no basis for this in any regulation. It comes from experience.
