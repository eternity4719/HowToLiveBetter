# After a chronic illness diagnosis: the first week, the first three months, and after that

This checklist is for people who have just been diagnosed with a chronic illness such as high blood pressure, diabetes, high blood lipids or gout. The items in this book that apply are spread across several sections. They are put in order of time here. Each line gives the action to take and then points at where it is written. Why it is worth doing, the numbers and the sources are all in the item being pointed at. If a line does not match your illness, skip it.

## The week of the diagnosis

1. If the illness is serious, have someone go with you on the day you collect the report, and do not stay at home alone afterwards. Put things like resigning or selling your flat off until later. See item 2 in section 29 (in the week you are told you have a serious illness).
2. If the doctor has prescribed medicine, start taking it as prescribed today, and do not stop when you begin to feel better. See item 1 in section 16 (take the medicine for the full course as the doctor ordered).
3. Take the medicine for high blood pressure or high blood lipids regularly. Do not stop it on your own. See item 12 in section 2 (if you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, take the medicine as prescribed).
4. If you have high blood pressure, buy an electronic blood pressure monitor and measure at home, until the readings reach target. See item 7 in section 1 (measure your blood pressure, and take drugs to bring it down to target).
5. When the prescription is being written, ask the doctor whether there is a generic version picked in centralised procurement. See item 12 in section 5 (generic drugs that passed the consistency evaluation and drugs picked in centralised procurement).
6. If you are diagnosed with type 2 diabetes, have the back of your eyes examined with dilation at the time. If you have type 1 diabetes, start at 5 years after the onset. See item 7 in section 16 (once diabetes is diagnosed, have the back of your eyes examined once).
7. Once gout is diagnosed, start urate-lowering medicine for the long term and push the blood urate below 360 µmol/L. See item 9 in section 16 (once gout is diagnosed, take urate-lowering medicine for the long term).
8. Han people taking allopurinol: ask the doctor first whether the HLA-B*5801 genotype should be tested. See item 9 in section 16 (once gout is diagnosed, take urate-lowering medicine for the long term).
9. If a check-up found high uric acid and you have never had an attack, that does not count as gout. Do not start urate-lowering drugs yet. See item 19 in section 6 (do not start urate-lowering drugs because a check-up found high uric acid and you have never had an attack).
10. If you smoke, set a quit date and stop in one go on that day. See item 4 in section 2 (set a quit date and stop in one go on that day).
11. Do not try to quit on willpower alone. Go to a smoking cessation clinic or a respiratory department to get the medicine. See item 3 in section 2 (do not try to quit on willpower alone, get the medicine first).

## The first three months

1. Get a notebook. At each follow-up test write down the date, the figures, the medicine you are taking and the dose. See item 3 in section 16 (write the figures from each one in the same notebook).
2. After every visit, put away your own copy of the records, the test reports and the images. See item 5 in section 24 (keep your own copy of the records, the test reports and the images).
3. In the place where you are insured, get the outpatient chronic and special disease status done. If you often go to hospital out of town, do the cross-region filing as well. See item 2 in section 16 (get the outpatient chronic and special disease status first and the cross-region filing second).
4. If someone at home has employee medical insurance, ask them to do the family binding once. The money in their personal account can then pay for your treatment and your medicine. See item 13 in section 5 (bind your family once in the medical insurance app).
5. Before you sign up with a family doctor in the community, ask which items medical insurance covers and which you pay for yourself. See item 6 in section 16 (before you sign up with a family doctor in the community, ask which items are covered).
6. Replace the salt in your home with low-sodium salt. If your kidney function is impaired or you are taking a potassium-sparing drug, ask the doctor first. See item 9 in section 2 (replace the salt in your home with low-sodium salt).
7. Start walking every day. The target is 7000–8000 steps, or 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week. See item 11 in section 2 (walk 7000–8000 steps a day).
8. Drink less alcohol. If you can manage not to drink at all, do not drink. See item 19 in section 2 (drink less alcohol, or none at all).
9. If you drink every day and stopping makes you shake and panic, do not stop on your own. Go to hospital first. See item 20 in section 2 (if you drink every day and stopping makes you shake and panic, do not stop on your own).
10. If somebody tells you to stop the medicine and try a folk remedy, do not stop. If you want to take a supplement, tell the doctor who is treating you first. See item 4 in section 16 (do not stop proper treatment in order to try a folk remedy or a health supplement).
11. Do not spend a lot of money on health supplements or herbal pastes to "look after your health". See item 10 in section 6 (do not spend a lot of money on health supplements and other health products, herbal pastes or tonics).
12. In the first 3 to 6 months of urate-lowering treatment, gout attacks are more likely. When it hurts, add an anti-inflammatory medicine as prescribed. Do not stop the urate-lowering medicine. See item 9 in section 16 (once gout is diagnosed, take urate-lowering medicine for the long term).
13. Before you buy a cold medicine or a painkiller yourself, read the ingredient list. Acetaminophen can only count once. See item 1 in section 34 (read the ingredient list: acetaminophen can only count once).
14. If you are taking anticoagulants or steroids, ask a doctor before taking a painkiller such as ibuprofen. See item 3 in section 34 (ask a doctor before taking a painkiller such as ibuprofen).

## Once the illness is stable

1. If the illness is stable and the medicine has not changed, get 12 weeks of it in one go from the community clinic. See item 5 in section 16 (a community health centre can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medicine at once).
2. Go for follow-up tests at the interval the doctor gives, and write each set of figures in the same notebook. See item 3 in section 16 (do your follow-up tests at the interval the doctor gives).
3. After you switch to a drug from centralised procurement, keep an eye on the figures in the notebook. If they really do get worse, go to the doctor. Do not stop the medicine on your own. See item 12 in section 5 (prefer generic drugs that passed the consistency evaluation and drugs picked in centralised procurement).
4. If you have diabetes, have your feet checked once a year, and the back of your eyes at the interval the doctor gives. See item 7 in section 16 (once diabetes is diagnosed, have the back of your eyes examined once).
5. Once your urate has reached target, keep taking the medicine. Do not stop. See item 9 in section 16 (once gout is diagnosed, take urate-lowering medicine for the long term).
6. If you have had a kidney stone, drink 2.5–3 litres of water a day and keep salt under 6 grams. See item 8 in section 16 (if you have had a kidney stone, drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day and keep salt under 6 grams).
7. If you have cardiovascular disease, have a flu vaccine in the autumn every year. See item 20 in section 1 (people with cardiovascular disease and older adults should get a flu vaccine every year).
8. For an ordinary illness, start at the community clinic. If you have to be admitted to hospital, go up by referral step by step through the community. The inpatient deductible carries on counting. See item 1 in section 24 (start at the community clinic and go up by referral step by step from the grassroots level).
9. Do not move up a level for an illness that can be dealt with at the grassroots level. In the same pooling region, each level you go up lowers the share reimbursed by about 10 percentage points. See item 2 in section 24 (the lower the level of the hospital the higher the share reimbursed).
10. If you need a large hospital for a specialist, go through a community referral or the hospital's referral centre. Do not buy appointments from scalpers. See item 3 in section 24 (go through a grassroots referral or the hospital's referral centre).
11. If you are planning to go to another province for treatment, ask at home before you set off. What comes back may be a good deal less than at home. See item 4 in section 24 (before going out of province for treatment, ask at home first).
