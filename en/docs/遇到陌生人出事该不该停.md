# When a stranger is in trouble on the street, walk on or stop

This is the long-form version of item 2 in section 13 (an elderly person falls, someone goes down, do not rush to lift them). The book sorts every item by who ends up benefiting, and the standard is how likely the benefit is to come back to you later. A stranger is the lowest group. The chance that the other person repays you is small, and you do not know what they are like. So nothing here tells you what to do. It sets out in full what each of the three roads costs, and which one you take is your own decision.

**No probabilities are given in this essay.** Every layer of cost below is real, and every one of them has a provision of law you can look up. But there is no published statistic for "out of a hundred people you help, how many actually end up in trouble". The rule in this book is that when a number is uncertain, leave it out. So do not read "seven layers are listed here" as "all seven will land on you".

## Three roads

| | Walk on | Stop but do not touch | Stop and help all the way |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legality | Legal. An ordinary person has no duty to rescue a stranger, and the criminal law has no offence of seeing an accident happen and not helping | Legal | Legal |
| Time | 0 | A few minutes. Dialling 120 leaves your number on record | Half a day at the least, and you may have to go to the hospital with them |
| Chance of being approached afterwards | Lowest | You are the one who left a number | Highest |
| What the other person gets | 0 | The ambulance arrives sooner | The most |

## The costs you may run into after stopping, lightest to heaviest

**One, a call back and a questioning.** If you dial 120 or 110, your number is left on record either way. The other party may ring you back to ask exactly where the place is. Later they may also ask you to come in and give a statement. In a criminal case, a person who reports it does not have to give their name in public, and the agency handling the case has to keep that confidence for them (Article 111 of the Criminal Procedure Law). There is no such confidentiality rule for a call to 120.

**Two, the injured person's family comes for you.** If the cameras do not turn up the vehicle that caused it, the family will find you most easily. You left a number, and you were at the scene. They may ring and press you, or they may come to your door.

**Three, you are sued in court.** Whoever says you hit them has to produce the evidence. If he cannot produce it, that consequence is his to bear (Article 90 of the Interpretation on the Application of the Civil Procedure Law). **But this provision only settles how the case is finally judged. It does not settle whether you get dragged into it.** The case is heard under the ordinary procedure, and the first instance has to be closed within 6 months, with a further extension of 6 months available in special circumstances. Under the summary procedure it is 3 months (Articles 152 and 164 of the Civil Procedure Law). Even if you win outright, there are only three things the other side can be made to pay: the case acceptance fee, the application fee, and the travel expenses and lost earnings of witnesses and appraisers when they attend court (Articles 6 and 29 of the Measures for the Payment of Litigation Costs). **The money you spend on your own lawyer is not in those three, and you pay it yourself.**

**Four, it reaches your workplace.** If someone comes to your workplace to make trouble, you can call the police. Where a person disturbs the order of an enterprise, institution or public service and makes it impossible for work to go on normally, the police give a warning or a fine of up to 500 yuan. Where the circumstances are heavier, there is 5 to 10 days of detention, plus a fine of up to 1000 yuan. Where a group comes together to make trouble, the leader is detained for 10 to 15 days (Article 26, item 1, of the Law on Administrative Penalties for Public Security). Where a person keeps harassing, pestering or following, and makes it impossible for you to get through your days normally, the punishment is under Article 50, item 5. With the approval of the head of the public security organ, it can also be ordered that he may not contact you for a set period. If the company dismisses you over this, that is in general an illegal termination of the employment contract, and the compensation is worked out at 2N (item 6 in section 19). But whether you call the police or go to labour arbitration, you are the one who makes the trips. And the stretch of time you lose the job first is not given back to you because you later get 2N.

**Five, someone films you and puts it online. ** **You may not be able to stop someone filming.** Without the consent of the person, you may not make, use or publish their likeness (Article 1019 of the Civil Code). But there is one exception. 「为实施新闻报道，不可避免地制作、使用、公开肖像权人的肖像」, which means making, using or publishing the likeness of the person holding the right to it where that is unavoidable in carrying out news reporting, counts as fair use and is not an infringement (Article 1020, item 2). A journalist filming in order to report the news falls under that exception. What you can do is refuse the interview, stay off camera, and say nothing in front of the lens. Whether you agree is your own decision, and nobody can agree on your behalf.

**Six, the video gets passed around and you get abuse.** Once the video is out, the comment section is out of your hands. The measures you can take yourself are written into the Provisions on the Governance of Cyberbullying Information (in force from 1 August 2024). The platform is supposed to give you these protective settings: block a stranger or a particular person named by you, and limit who can see your own information. You can also stop people from reposting or commenting, take private messages only from friends, or take private messages from nobody (Article 23). The platform is supposed to offer a one-click function for capturing evidence, which fixes the content that abuses you (Article 25). The platform is supposed to put a dedicated shortcut for complaints and reports in a prominent position (Article 26). Where a platform finds anything suspected of being illegal or of a crime, it is supposed to report it to the public security organ promptly and supply the lead (Article 15). How to keep evidence yourself and how to call the police, see item 15 in section 8.

**Seven, when you cannot take it any more.** The six layers above can each be answered with a provision of law. The seventh cannot. There is no reliable data from China to cite on how much being hounded by a crowd affects a person's mood. This book gives no figure. It gives one way out, the psychological assistance helpline 12356, see item 25 in section 1.

## Two situations where walking on is not free

- **You run or manage the place where this happens, or you organise a mass activity.** A place here means a hotel, a shopping centre, a bank, a station, an airport, a sports venue, an entertainment venue and places like them. You owe a duty of safety to the people inside. If you do not do it, you carry tort liability, which means paying money. Where the damage was caused by someone else and you still did not do it, you pay within the part that is your responsibility, and that is called supplementary liability (Article 1198 of the Civil Code). So a member of staff on duty, or the venue itself, walking away carries a different responsibility from a passer-by walking away.
- **You were already involved.** You hit the person, the vehicle is yours, or the danger came from something you did earlier. What matters then is not whether to look after someone else, but how to handle a traffic accident and a hit-and-run, see item 1 in section 8.

## When you decide to stop, the least-effort way to do it

1. **Do not start moving them or lifting them.** There is no second answer on this point. The reason is medical. Lifting someone roughly can make a brain bleed worse, and it can make an injury to the spine worse too. There is nothing to worry about on the legal side. Damage caused in the course of rescuing someone has already been exempted by Article 184 of the Civil Code.
2. Stand a little way off and shout to him, and see whether he is breathing. If he is not breathing, press hard on his chest, see item 1 in section 13. If he is breathing, stay where you are, dial 120 and give the location clearly.
3. Pull a passer-by in to do it with you, and start recording on your phone at the same time. A witness and a recording are more use than arguing about it afterwards.
4. Do not lift him into a car on your own, and do not put your own money in to take him to hospital. This step costs the most and it is the hardest to settle afterwards.
5. You are hurt yourself while rescuing someone, or you have put money in. If you want that money back, see item 39 in section 13 (you were hurt rescuing someone and you put money in, what to do).
