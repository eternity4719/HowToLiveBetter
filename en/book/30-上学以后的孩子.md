# 30. School-age children

Section 20 covers the first year after a child is born. Section 18 works out what raising a child costs in money and time. The dozen-odd years in between, from 3 to 18, are in this section. The item on managing screen time by age starts at 0, because the official bands are three: 0–3, 3–6 and school age, and writing them out separately only makes it harder to use. What this section answers is: when "do not let it delay schoolwork" and "the body" collide, which things cannot wait. Those things have a time window, and once it has passed they cannot be made up. Whether schoolwork should be this competitive is not discussed here; this section gives only the windows and the numbers.

The first eight items, item 12 (dilated refraction), item 13 (fissure sealants) and items 15 to 17, count death and the real physical and psychological harm that is left behind. Items 16 and 17 among them are about how a family manages a child: no hitting and no shouting, and going to a parenting course that teaches specific techniques. Item 9 (not buying products that claim to "cure" myopia) counts money. Item 10 (the written rules on sleep, homework and physical education rankings), item 11 (taking a leave of absence while keeping the student record) and item 14 (a child who loves computer games) count time. Drowning, traffic, helmets and the HPV vaccine: see section 1. Infants: see section 20. Children being deceived and top-ups for computer games: see section 5. The lines a minor should not cross: see section 9. How to recognise an emergency and how to call a vehicle: see section 13. Children after a parent dies: see section 29. None of that is repeated in this section.

### 1. If a child says the pain came on suddenly and is getting worse, go to hospital at once; do not let him sit it out until school is over
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Take half a day off and take the child to register at the emergency department.
- In plain language: Some conditions are counted in hours. A boy with sudden severe pain on one side of the scrotum has testicular torsion, which is the vessel attached to the testicle twisted round and the blood supply cut off. Treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles are saved. Left until 19 to 24 hours, only 42.5% are. Pain like this does not wait until school is over, and it does not wait until the exam is finished either.
- Benefit: 30 sets of case reports and 2116 patients with testicular torsion were collected properly and looked at together. 1283 of them are counted in the 6-hour band. Counting from the start of the pain, the proportion of testicles saved from 0 to 6 hours is 97.2%. 7 to 12 hours 79.3%. 13 to 18 hours 61.3%. 19 to 24 hours 42.5%. 25 to 48 hours 24.4%. Over 48 hours 7.4%. Pooled into broad bands, within 12 hours 90.4%, 13 to 24 hours 54.0%, over 24 hours 18.1%. The author's conclusion is that the time in which the testicle can be saved can be longer than the 6 to 8 hours usually quoted. So even if the pain has already lasted a long time, it still has to be treated; do not skip it on the grounds that "it is too late anyway"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: Say something to the boy in advance: if down there it suddenly hurts badly, say so at once, there is no need to be embarrassed about it. Testicular torsion is commonest in adolescent boys, and a good many sit on it because it is hard to bring up. Other conditions counted in hours the same way are acute appendicitis (which perforates if left), diabetic ketoacidosis and meningitis. This section has not checked item by item how much damage a delay of a few hours does in those. The signal they share is this: it starts suddenly, it keeps getting worse, and it is unlike anything that has happened before. How to recognise an emergency and how to call an ambulance, see section 13.
### 2. Do not put off treatment that has to be done for the sake of "after the exam"; some windows follow bone age, not the examination timetable
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The only decision is whether to do it early or late. Leave and follow-up appointments take time; the cost of the treatment itself is separate. The hard part is that the brace has to be worn for at least 18 hours a day, and the child has to put up with it the whole time.
- In plain language: Scoliosis is the spine curving out to the side. A brace is a hard shell worn over the body that holds the spine straight. Worn while the bones are still growing, 75% of children never reach the degree at which they would need an operation; among those who are only observed it is only 42%. The time for wearing it follows the bones finishing their growth, not the junior high or university entrance examination, so waiting until after the exam often leaves an operation as the only option.
- Benefit: A trial run by several hospitals together enrolled 242 adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis who met the conditions for wearing a brace. 116 of them were assigned at random and 126 chose for themselves whether to wear one. Success and failure were defined in advance: curvature reaching 50 degrees or more counts as treatment failure, and being below that degree when the bones finish growing counts as treatment success. Counted by the group first assigned, whether or not they actually went on to wear it enough. In the randomised bracing group the success rate was 75%, in the randomised observation-only group 42% (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16), so the chance of success was about 4 times that of observation only. Pooling the randomised group with the ones who chose for themselves, it is 72% against 48% (after taking out the differences between the two groups that were there to start with, OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46, still about 1.9 times). The more hours a day the brace is worn, the higher the success rate (P<0.001, so this relationship is unlikely to be chance). The trial was stopped early because the brace clearly worked
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Notes: Scoliosis is used as an example here because it has a randomised trial, because it has a clear time window, and because it happens to be one of the focus items of the student medical examination (see item 7 in this section). Others of the same kind are strabismus, amblyopia, dental caries, and operations for congenital heart disease where you can pick the time. What they have in common is that the later you do it the worse the result, or the higher the cost. Ask the specialist about the specific window. If you are afraid of missing lessons, a child can take a leave of absence during the compulsory education stage, for up to 1 year, and the student place is kept, see item 11 in this section.
### 3. If a child is bullied, report it to the school the same day and ask for a written response; where it involves hitting, taking money or spreading rumours, call the police
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Go to the head teacher, and also to the school's body for handling bullying. To call the police, dial 110. The hard part is standing up to advice like "don't make a fuss".
- In plain language: Children bullied by their peers have about 2.2 times the chance of having thoughts of suicide as those who are not bullied, and about 2.6 times the chance of having attempted suicide. A school that receives a report has to investigate at once, and where it finds bullying it has to impose a sanction; in serious cases it may not conceal it and has to report it to the public security organ. So report it the same day, ask for the recognition procedure to be used, and keep a written record.
- Benefit: 34 studies, 284,375 children and adolescents, pooled together. Being bullied by peers is associated with thoughts of suicide, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37), so the chance of the thoughts appearing is about 2.2 times that of the unbullied. A separate 9 studies, 70,102 people, found bullying associated with attempted suicide, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. Sex, age and the intrinsic quality of the studies all differ, and the result is the same throughout. Cyberbullying is more strongly associated with thoughts of suicide than bullying in person. Article twenty-one of the Provisions on School Protection of Minors（未成年人学校保护规定）lists five kinds of behaviour: hitting, kicking, slapping, scratching and biting, pushing and shoving, pulling and dragging; insulting, ridiculing and giving insulting nicknames; taking property, demanding by force or deliberately damaging property; deliberately excluding and isolating; fabricating facts online in order to slander or spreading rumours. The same article also provides that where the party with the advantage in age, in body or in numbers does these things deliberately or maliciously and causes personal injury, loss of property or mental harm, it may be recognised as bullying. Article twenty-two requires staff to find out about a student's abnormal emotions or physical injury in good time and report it. Article twenty-three requires a school that receives a report to investigate at once, submit it to the bullying-handling body for recognition and disposal, and notify the parents to take part. Where bullying is recognised, an educational sanction or a disciplinary sanction has to be imposed. Serious bullying that breaches public order administration or is suspected of being criminal may not be concealed, and has to be reported in good time to the public security organ and the education administration department (nationwide, in force from 2021-9-1)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；Ministry of Education (教育部) (2021). Provisions on School Protection of Minors（未成年人学校保护规定）（教育部令第 50 号，articles twenty-one, twenty-two and twenty-three）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Notes: If a child has an injury whose origin cannot be explained, suddenly refuses to go to school, and things keep going missing, ask about it first using the signals in article twenty-two. This analysis pools several studies together; it only follows records and has no control group, so it cannot show that bullying alone causes suicide, but the direction and the size of the risk are clear. The Plan on Strengthening the Comprehensive Governance of Bullying among Primary and Secondary School Students《加强中小学生欺凌综合治理方案》additionally requires a strict distinction between bullying and the rough-and-tumble play of ordinary students. So the school is obliged to make a finding, and cannot settle the matter with one line about children playing.
### 4. Two hours outdoors for the child every day; this is the only way of preventing myopia that randomised trials support
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. But you have to set aside time every day, and it will mostly have to come out of the time for homework and for online classes. The hard part is doing it every day, not a few times.
- In plain language: Adding 40 minutes of outdoor class a day to the first grade in primary school brought new cases of myopia after three years from about forty per cent down to about thirty per cent. The amount the health commission asks for is no less than 2 hours outdoors in the daytime every day, or 14 hours a week in total. Overcast days count, and standing outside at break counts too.
- Benefit: First-grade pupils at 12 primary schools in Guangzhou were assigned at random by school (952 in the intervention schools, 951 in the control schools, mean age 6.6 years). The intervention added 1 outdoor activity lesson of 40 minutes on every school day and encouraged parents to take the children out more outside lessons. Over 3 years new myopia was 30.4% in the intervention group, 259 cases among 853 who qualified for the analysis; 39.5% in the control group, 287 cases among 726. The difference between the two groups was -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001), so the true gap is somewhere between 4 and 14 percentage points lower. The increase in degree over the 3 years (the change in equivalent spherical equivalent) was -1.42 D against -1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group increased slightly less. D is the unit in which degrees are measured. The growth in axial length (the front-to-back length of the eyeball) showed no difference between the two groups (P=0.07). The Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents《防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条》requires children and adolescents to spend no less than 2 hours outdoors in the daytime every day, or 14 hours a week in total. It notes that outdoor activity has a protective effect even on overcast days, and it requires avoiding the hot, strongly sunlit hours after midday
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；General Office of the National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委办公厅) (2023). Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents（防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条）（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: This trial only ran for 3 years, and the children who took part all started at 6 years old. A 9.1 percentage point difference in the proportion with myopia can stop some people from becoming myopic; it cannot guarantee that somebody will not become myopic. The same Ten Key Points gives several other practices. For reading and writing, "one foot, one fist, one inch". After 20 minutes of looking at something close, look into the distance for at least 20 seconds. A primary or secondary school student using an electronic screen for anything other than study, no more than 15 minutes at a time and no more than 1 hour a day in total. "Being outdoors" and "having a physical education lesson" are not the same thing; standing outside at break counts too.

### 5. No screens from 0 to 3 years old, as few as possible from 3 to 6, and no more than 1 hour a day for primary and secondary school students using a screen for anything other than study
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is taking the screen out of the child's hands, and the parents cutting down their own use in front of the child.
- In plain language: The National Health Commission asks for no screens from 0 to 3 years old, as little contact as possible from 3 to 6, and for primary and secondary school students using a screen for anything other than study no more than 15 minutes at a time and no more than 1 hour a day. The reason is that long periods of close-up work bring myopia on earlier. The evidence on screens and myopia is not that solid; the thing actually tested and shown to work is 2 hours outdoors a day.
- Benefit: The wording of article four of the Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents《防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条》is「0～3岁婴幼儿不使用手机、平板、电脑等视屏类电子产品；3～6岁幼儿尽量避免其接触和使用手机、平板、电脑等视屏类电子产品；中小学生非学习目的使用电子屏幕单次时长不宜超过15分钟，每天累计时长不宜超过1小时」, that is, infants and toddlers from 0 to 3 years old do not use mobile phones, tablets, computers and other screen-based electronic products; children from 3 to 6 should avoid as far as possible being exposed to and using mobile phones, tablets, computers and other screen-based electronic products; primary and secondary school students using an electronic screen for purposes other than study should keep a single session to no more than 15 minutes and the total for the day to no more than 1 hour. The same article states that「长时间近距离使用电子视屏类产品，易消耗儿童远视储备量，是儿童青少年近视早发、高发的重要原因」, that is, long periods of using screen-based electronic products at close range easily use up children's reserve of far-sightedness, and are an important cause of myopia coming early and often in children and adolescents. The 2018 implementation plan from the Ministry of Education (教育部) and seven other departments gives parents the same requirement, and adds that「年龄越小，连续使用电子产品的时间应越短」, that is, the younger the child, the shorter the continuous period of using electronic products should be. The research evidence is much weaker: a systematic review took in 33 studies, 11 of which entered the pooled calculation, with subjects from 3 months to 33 years old. Looking only at mobile phones and tablets, the odds ratio for myopia is 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%). Pooled with computers it is 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%). The authors' conclusion goes no further than "possibly associated", and they state that not one of the 33 studies had a reliable measure of screen time
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: General Office of the National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委办公厅) (2023). Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents（防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条）（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; Ministry of Education (教育部) and seven other departments (2018). Implementation Plan for the Comprehensive Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents（综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案）（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Notes: The grade is B because what the official documents give are banded rules, not numbers measured in a trial. The lower bound of the odds ratio in the research sits exactly at 1.00, and the authors only dare say "possibly associated". So treat it as a good house rule to carry out, not as "no screens means no myopia". What is really supported by a randomised trial is item 4 in this section (2 hours outdoors). Preschool children need their eyes checked too: the same document states that at 1–3 years old, 4–6 years old and after the age of 7 all children should have regular refractive screening to see how much far-sightedness reserve is left; the method is in item 12 in this section (dilation and refraction testing). A meta-analysis has also been done on the relationship between screens and children's language skills: 42 studies, 18905 people, the longer the screen time the weaker the language skills (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10). But watching together, and watching programmes with educational content, are positively associated in the other direction (r=0.16 and 0.13). That is association, not causation; all it shows is that replacing talking with a child by putting a screen in front of them is not worth doing.
### 6. Treat myopia as damage that stays: the deeper the degree, the more retinal detachment, macular disease and glaucoma later on
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You have nothing to do about it yourself; what has to be done is in items 4 and 5 (2 hours outdoors, managing screens by age).
- In plain language: Myopia does not get better on its own and the degree does not come back down. Nor is it only a matter of wearing a pair of glasses. Compared with people who are not myopic, the chance of myopic macular degeneration is more than ten times higher with mild myopia and hundreds of times higher above 600 degrees. For retinal detachment it is about 3 times higher with mild myopia and about 13 times higher above 600 degrees. So the earlier a child becomes myopic and the faster it goes up, the larger the risk they are carrying for later.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis pooled research published up to 2019-6, sorting by degree into three bands: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D) and high (≤−6.00 D, 600 degrees and above). Compared with people who are not myopic, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration are in turn 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48) and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34). For retinal detachment, 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50) and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract, 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28) and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, mild is 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91), and moderate and high pooled together are 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52). For visual impairment at over 60 years of age the odds ratios for the three bands are in turn 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85) and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents《防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条》says the same thing: 600 degrees and above counts as high myopia, and in this group the incidence of blinding eye conditions such as cataract, open-angle glaucoma, myopic macular degeneration, retinal detachment and myopic optic neuropathy is markedly higher than in other groups, and that「近视可防可控不可逆」, that is, myopia can be prevented and controlled but not reversed
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; General Office of the National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委办公厅) (2023). Ten Key Points on the Prevention and Control of Myopia in Children and Adolescents（防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条）（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Notes: Everything pooled into this is observational research, and the odds ratios are unusually large. The credible range for high myopia on macular degeneration runs from 230 to 3104, which means the studies underneath it differ a great deal; the figure can only be read for its order of magnitude and must not be used as an exact value. Most of these complications only show up in middle and old age. Myopia itself is not a disability; the great majority of people have normal vision once they wear glasses to correct it. But it is not a matter of putting on a pair of glasses and being done with, and it is worth spending effort on delaying it and controlling it. How to follow up after diagnosis, see item 12 in this section (dilation and refraction testing). Do not buy products claiming they can cure it, see item 9 in this section (curing myopia).
### 7. Read the report from the annual student health examination yourself, and take any abnormal item to a hospital the same year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. In the compulsory education stage the examination fee is paid out of the school's public budget, and parents do not pay separately.
- In plain language: Primary and secondary schools organise one health examination of the students each year. The items to watch on the report are overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal curvature of the spine, poor vision and dental caries; these are the ones most easily let slide. Do not glance at height and weight and put the sheet away. If any of them is abnormal, take the child to a specialist that same year and get it cleared up.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Administration of Health Examinations of Primary and Secondary School Students (2021 Edition)《中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）》provides that primary and secondary schools organise 1 health examination of the students on roll each year. The surgical examination covers the head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin and lymph nodes. The ophthalmic examination covers the appearance of the eye, distance vision and refraction (the degree of myopia or hyperopia). The report handed to each individual should include the results found, an overall assessment and health advice. Overweight, obesity, malnutrition, abnormal curvature of the spine, poor vision and dental caries must be the focus of the guidance. Education administration departments should keep a file on students with health problems and follow them up (write it down and come back to it later). The cost of health examinations for students during the compulsory education stage is paid out of the school's public budget, and parents do not pay separately (nationwide, 2021-9)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委) and Ministry of Education (教育部) (2021). Measures for the Administration of Health Examinations of Primary and Secondary School Students (2021 Edition)《中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）》(国卫医发〔2021〕29 号). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Notes: The grade is B because this is a document telling schools how to do it, not a study of effects. What it guarantees is "examine every year, examine these items, follow up anything abnormal"; it does not guarantee that somebody will act on what is found. The examination is only a first screen, not a diagnosis. Poor vision needs dilation and refraction testing in the eye department (item 12 in this section). Abnormal curvature of the spine needs the orthopaedic or spinal surgery department (item 2 in this section, do not put off treatment to wait for an exam). Both of those steps have to be taken by a parent taking the child there.
### 8. Screen children aged 12 to 18 for depression once; do not take the school's psychological questionnaire for a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 0 to several tens of yuan, filling in a screening questionnaire at a community centre or an outpatient clinic. The 12355 and 12356 hotlines cost nothing to call.
- In plain language: The US preventive guideline recommends screening 12- to 18-year-olds for major depression; for 11 and under the evidence is not enough. Suicide is the second leading cause of death among 10- to 19-year-olds. The psychological questionnaire the school gives every year is a census, not a diagnosis. If the child says they feel awful, or eating, sleep, interests and school marks all change suddenly, register with psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (a grade B recommendation). A grade B recommendation means moderate confidence and a moderate expected benefit, so it is worth doing. Screening children aged 11 and under for major depression, the evidence is insufficient (an I statement, that is the evidence is not enough to give a recommendation). Screening children and adolescents specifically for suicide risk, the evidence on benefit and on harm is both insufficient (an I statement). The document points out that suicide is the second leading cause of death in the 10- to 19 age group. It also points out that major depression in children and adolescents is closely related to a good many later problems. These include recurrent depressive episodes in adulthood, other mental disorders, and a higher risk of suicidal thoughts, attempted suicide and death by suicide. The Ten Measures to Further Strengthen the Mental Health Work for Primary and Secondary School Students《进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施》requires a three-level service system of head teachers, full-time and part-time school mental health teachers, and psychiatrists. Each student is to have a psychological assessment once per school year, generally no more than 1 time. The document also requires referral to medical care for mental illness and a mechanism for returning to school after recovery. It requires the 12355 teenage service desk and the 12356 psychological assistance hotline to play their part (nationwide, 2025-10)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；General Office of the Ministry of Education (教育部办公厅) (2025). Ten Measures to Further Strengthen the Mental Health Work for Primary and Secondary School Students《进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施》（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Notes: The grade is B because what the original gives is a rating of the strength of the recommendation, not an effect that can be turned into a number. It is also a US recommendation; China has no matching official screening guidance. A positive screening result only means you have to do one more assessment; it is not a diagnosis. What to do when there are thoughts of suicide, see item 25 in section 1. How to handle it at home, see section 29.
### 9. Do not buy products and services claiming they can "cure myopia" or "reduce the degree"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money, and you save the money as well.

So what the other side can show is this: a little spanking, done conditionally, may not be worse than other methods. Heavy spanking and constant hitting and shouting, both sides agree, are worse. An occasional slap and a raised voice are not stated by the statute to be domestic violence; what it names is beating and persistent verbal abuse and intimidation. The main beneficiary is the child. If somebody else in the household is hitting the child, how to call the police and how to apply for a protection order: see item 43 in section 8 (personal safety protection order). Anyone who sees a child being hit over a long period in another family has the right to step in and stop it, or to report it to the police, the civil affairs department or the education department. For what is going on in a stranger's home, calling 110 or telling the residents' committee is enough; do not go to the house yourself and take it on.


### 17. If the child is hard to manage and you cannot stop yourself hitting or shouting, go to a parenting course that teaches specific techniques
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The parenting schools run by schools, kindergartens and communities are public-benefit and charge nothing. The courses with evidence behind them run weekly or every other week, one to two hours each, from four weeks to twenty-four weeks. You have to work through the exercises at home. The hard part is finishing the course and practising every day.
- In plain language: In trials where assignment was by lot, a course that teaches parents specific techniques reduced the child's hitting and rule-breaking, and it improved the parents' mood. Parents also hit and shouted at their children less; the effect was largest just after the last session, was still there one or two years later, but was much smaller. Shanghai has run this kind of course too, and it worked.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 13 trials, 10 of them assigned by lot, with 1078 parents. The children were 3 to 12 years old and all of them had behaviour problems. Among the parents who took the course, the children's behaviour problems went down. Rated by the parents themselves the effect is SMD −0.53 (95% CI −0.72 to −0.34, credible range), and rated independently by others −0.44 (−0.77 to −0.11). SMD and d are the same ruler, and 0.5 counts as moderate. The parents' own mental health improved, −0.36 (−0.52 to −0.20). Negative or harsh discipline became less frequent: −0.77 (−0.96 to −0.59) rated by the parents, −0.42 (−0.67 to −0.16) rated by others. The authors describe these as short-term effects. A second meta-analysis screened out 346 randomised trials, 60 of which measured behaviour such as hitting and shouting at children, across 22 countries. Hitting and shouting fell when the course had just ended, d = −0.46 (−0.59 to −0.33). After 1 to 6 months it was −0.24 (−0.37 to −0.11). After 7 to 24 months it was −0.18 (−0.34 to −0.02), and the later the measurement, the smaller it is. Most of these figures are reported by the parents themselves. There are trials in China as well. In Shanghai, 81 parents were assigned by lot and some took the Triple P group course. After the course, the children's adjustment problems and the parents' discipline and mood were all better than in the parents still waiting for their course, and still were six months later. In the countryside, 191 adults looking after children at one kindergarten took part; some worked through an online course on their own and then joined two group discussions. Two weeks after it, the child abuse they reported was about 33% lower than among those who were only handed a book to read (IRR 0.67). Three months later, the children's behaviour problems had fallen as well (d = −0.40). The Law on Promoting Family Education requires parents to learn about family education on their own initiative. Primary and secondary schools and kindergartens may run parenting schools and organise public-benefit family education guidance at regular intervals. Residents' committees and village committees may run community parenting schools. Governments at provincial level and above have to run public-benefit online parenting schools and service hotlines. Institutions giving family education guidance may not organise profit-making training (nationwide, in force from 2022-1-1)
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: The grade is A because there are pooled figures from Cochrane and from several randomised trials. The courses in the trials have a few things in common. The person leading the course teaches specific techniques, such as playing with the child, praising, rewarding and disciplining. The parents practise and rehearse in the class, and there is homework at home. Courses that only hand out material, or only discuss, were left out, so the evidence does not cover a single lecture. What the public-benefit parenting schools here teach, and how they teach it, differs from place to place and it may not be this kind of course. When you pick a course, look at whether it runs over several weeks and makes you practise specific techniques. Names you will often see are Triple P, Incredible Years and Parent-Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT). This book does not recommend particular institutions or paid products. The effect of less hitting and shouting gets smaller after a year or two, so you have to keep practising after the course ends. The beneficiaries are the child and you. If the child already has serious emotional problems, take them to psychiatry or to a child psychology clinic, see item 8 in this section (depression screening).
