# 14. Accounts and information security

This section measures money and personal information. The moment somebody signs into your account, the first thing you lose is money. They will also use your account to scam the people in your address book. Your identity has been taken as well.

### 1. Turn on two-factor verification for your email, payment and social accounts, prefer the prompt on your phone, and fall back on a text code
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You set it up once per account, and it takes two or three minutes.
- In plain language: Two-factor verification means that at sign-in you confirm a second time, on top of the password, that you are the person. The method where a prompt appears on your phone and you tap to confirm stops more than 90% of phishing takeovers. The old method of answering questions such as 「where did you last sign in」 or 「what is your backup email」 stops only about 10%.
- Benefit: Google counted 350,000 real takeover attempts, also called account hijacking. One class of challenge verifies through a device, such as a prompt on your phone that you tap to confirm, or plugging in a security key. This class stopped 「more than 94% of the hijacking attempts that originate from phishing and 100% of the automated hijacking attempts」. Phishing means tricking you into typing your password on a fake website, and automated means a machine trying leaked passwords in bulk. The other class verifies by asking you questions, such as where you last signed in and what your backup email is. That class 「stopped as little as 10% of phishing hijacking and 73% of automated hijacking」
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Doerfler P, Thomas K, Marincenko M, et al. (2019). Evaluating Login Challenges as a Defense Against Account Takeover. The World Wide Web Conference (WWW '19). <https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313481>
- Notes: The same study also found that this kind of challenge sometimes locks the real owner out. 52% of genuine users did not get in on the first attempt, but 97% of them did get in eventually. Start with your email, because most other accounts can be recovered through email
### 2. Give your email a password of its own, and do not reuse it on any other site
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. A password manager stores it so that you do not have to remember it. The hard part is dropping the habit of using one password everywhere.
- In plain language: A password stolen from another site is used to sign straight into your email. Once somebody is in your email, every account you can recover with email goes with it. So the email password has to be its own, and you use it nowhere else.
- Benefit: Taking leaked accounts and passwords from elsewhere and trying them one by one is called credential stuffing. It is the cheapest attack there is, and your email is tried this way too. Once somebody is in your email, every account you recover with email goes with it. The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency recommends a different strong password for every account, at least 16 characters long, kept in a password manager
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: US CISA. Use Strong Passwords. <https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/use-strong-passwords>
- Notes: If you cannot remember them, use the password manager built into your browser. It keeps the password for each site for you, which is far better than one password everywhere. Do not store passwords in your WeChat favourites or in a notes app
### 3. Set a lock-screen password on the phone and a PIN on the SIM card
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. One setting for the lock-screen password and one for the PIN.
- In plain language: The SIM card is the small card inside the phone, and it is what receives your text verification codes. If you lose the phone, whoever finds it pulls that card out, slots it into another phone to receive the codes, then resets your accounts one by one. With a PIN on the card, moving it to another phone means a password is needed at switch-on, and that route is closed.
- Benefit: After you lose your phone, the fastest route for whoever finds it is to put the SIM card into another phone and receive your text codes there. With the code, they reset your accounts one by one. A PIN on the SIM card means a password is needed before that other phone will start, so the finder cannot use it to receive codes
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: the author's own experience, no direct literature
- Notes: You set the PIN under 「SIM card lock」 in the phone's settings. The factory code is usually 1234 or 0000. Three wrong attempts in a row and you need the carrier's PUK code to open it. So write that code on paper as soon as you have set the PIN
### 4. If you lose your phone, work in this order: report the SIM card, lock it remotely, change your passwords, call the police, freeze the bank card
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The whole sequence takes a bit over ten minutes.
- In plain language: The order matters more than how fast you are. First report the SIM card, and the lifeline of your verification codes is cut. Then lock the phone remotely, change your email and payment passwords from a computer, report it to the police and get a receipt, and freeze the bank card if it is needed. If the phone is not with you, borrow somebody else's and call the carrier to report the card.
- Benefit: The order matters more than the speed. First, report the SIM card, and nobody can receive your codes any more. Second, lock the phone remotely and wipe its contents. Third, change your email and payment passwords from a computer. Fourth, report it to the police and get a receipt. Last, freeze the bank card as needed. The US Federal Communications Commission says the same: lock it remotely even when you think you have only lost it. If it was stolen, report it at once, give the model and the IMEI serial number, which is the phone's identity number, and tell the carrier immediately
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: US FCC. Protect Your Smart Device. <https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/protect-your-mobile-device> ; the order of the steps is the author's own experience ; for replacing an identity card see section 7, and for a loan taken out in your name see the entry on credit records in section 8
- Notes: Write down the customer service numbers of the three carriers in advance: China Mobile 10086, China Unicom 10010, China Telecom 10000. Also note which city your own number was registered in, because the operator will ask. You can call the operator from somebody else's phone and report the card there too
### 5. If your card is used by someone else, report and freeze it first, then call the police, then claim from the bank: proving 「that you made the payment」 is the bank's duty
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The moment you see anything odd on the card, report it or freeze it at once. Keep the police report, the record of the card report, and every transaction notice the bank sends you. If the card is still with you, make a small enquiry or a small deposit nearby, so you hold a record showing the real card was in your hands at the time. The hard part is holding back the urge to argue with customer service first. Report and freeze first.
- In plain language: You do not have to prove 「this was not me」 when somebody uses your card. The other way round, the bank has to produce evidence that you made the payment, and if it cannot, it pays you. The condition is that you report and freeze at once. If you put it off, the extra loss is yours to carry.
- Benefit: The Supreme People's Court regulation separates out 「who has to produce the evidence」. If you say this is a counterfeit-card or online theft, you have to produce evidence first. A counterfeit-card theft means a copy of the card was made and used. Evidence you can use includes legal documents already in force, where the real card was at the time of the transaction, and where the transaction took place. There are also account transaction records, transaction notices, police reports, records of the card report, and so on. **In the other direction, where the card-issuing bank or a non-bank payment institution (third-party payment) says the payment was made by the cardholder or with the cardholder's authorisation, it is for them to produce the evidence.** After you tell the bank, if the bank does not verify in time, or does not supply and keep the transaction documents and the surveillance video in time, so that the evidence cannot be obtained, the consequence of failing to produce evidence falls on the bank. Once the finding stands, a debit card (savings card) holder can require the issuing bank to pay over the principal and interest on the amount taken by the fraud, and to compensate the loss. A credit card holder can require the overdrawn amount and the penalty charge to be returned, and can claim compensation; where the bank instead asks you to repay the overdraft, the court will not support it. You can also require the issuing bank to remove the corresponding bad credit record in good time (nationwide, in force from 2021-5-25)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院) (2021). Provisions on Several Issues Concerning the Trial of Civil Disputes over Bank Cards, 关于审理银行卡民事纠纷案件若干问题的规定（第四、五、七、十四、十五条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/304771.html>
- Notes: Two situations leave you carrying the liability. The first is that you did not look after the card, the password and the verification codes properly and were at fault yourself. The original wording is 「未尽妥善保管义务具有过错」, which means you failed to keep them properly and were at fault, and you carry it in proportion to your fault. So do not tell other people your password, and do not forward your verification codes to them (see item 1 in this section, which prefers a prompt on your phone for two-factor verification). The second is failing to report the card in time and letting the loss carry on growing, in which case the extra part is yours. So the first step is always to report and freeze, not to argue with customer service first. The same rules apply to non-bank payment institutions. If their promotional material promises 「先行赔付」, payment before recovery, in specific and definite terms, you can hold them to it and ask them to pay first. If you were tricked into transferring the money yourself, that is a different route: see item 2 in section 8 (if you spot the fraud, call 110 or 96110 at once and ask for the payment to be stopped).
### 6. Now and then look at the signed-in devices and authorised apps on your accounts, and clear out the ones you no longer use
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Each time takes a few minutes. The hard part is that nobody reminds you, so you have to remember to look.
- In plain language: 「Signed-in devices」 means the phones and computers that can still use your account. Somebody taking over an account often waits a while before acting. When an unfamiliar device appears in the list, or software you stopped using long ago is still connected, sign out of everything and change your password.
- Benefit: An account takeover often does not happen on the spot, because the attacker waits a while first. The signed-in device list on an account records the phones and computers that can still use that account. The authorised apps list records the other software you allowed to sign in with it. An unfamiliar device in the list, and third-party software you stopped using long ago, are the easiest traces to spot
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: the author's own experience, no direct literature
- Notes: WeChat, Alipay, your email, your Apple account and your Android account all have this option. If you find a device you do not recognise, choose sign out of all devices and then change your password
### 7. Do not tap 「agree to all」 to get an app working: if the information is not necessary, refusing it does not let them refuse you the service
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is holding back from tapping 「agree to all」.
- In plain language: When an app asks for your information, if it is not necessary for providing the service, it cannot refuse you the service because you decline. What it may collect is also limited to what it actually needs. A map needs your location, and a torch does not need your contacts.
- Benefit: The law sets out two rules. First, it may not refuse to provide a product or service on the ground that a person declines, or withdraws consent, except where processing the information is necessary to provide the service. Second, collection must stay within the minimum scope needed to achieve the purpose of the processing, so it may only take what it actually needs
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2021). Personal Information Protection Law, 个人信息保护法. China NPC Net. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html> : Article six 「收集个人信息，应当限于实现处理目的的最小范围，不得过度收集个人信息」 ; Article sixteen 「个人信息处理者不得以个人不同意处理其个人信息或者撤回同意为由，拒绝提供产品或者服务；处理个人信息属于提供产品或者服务所必需的除外」 ; Article fifteen 「基于个人同意处理个人信息的，个人有权撤回其同意。个人信息处理者应当提供便捷的撤回同意的方式」
- Notes: The test is whether the information is necessary to provide that service. A map needs your location; a torch does not need your contacts. After installing an app, go first to the app permissions page in your phone's settings and switch off the permissions you do not need. When you do need one, choose allow just this once.
### 8. You have the right to see, copy, correct and delete your own personal information, and you can sue if you are refused
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. You only need to complain or sue if the other side drags its feet. A real court case runs from several months, and you pay the legal fees yourself, so complaining first is better value. The hard part is chasing them repeatedly while they stall.
- In plain language: You have the right to require a company to let you see, copy, correct and delete your own information. Where the service has stopped, the agreed retention period has run out, or you withdraw consent, the company should delete it by itself. If it refuses you, it has to give its reasons, and if it still will not act you can take it straight to court. Closing an account and deleting information are two different things, and after closing the account you still have to ask separately for the deletion.
- Benefit: There are several situations in which a company has to delete on its own initiative: the service has stopped, the agreed retention period has run out, you have withdrawn consent, the original purpose of the collection has been achieved, and so on. If it does not delete, you can require it to. If it refuses you the exercise of these rights, it has to give its reasons. You can sue in the people's court
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2021). Personal Information Protection Law, 个人信息保护法. China NPC Net. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html> : Article forty-five 「个人有权向个人信息处理者查阅、复制其个人信息……个人请求查阅、复制其个人信息的，个人信息处理者应当及时提供」 ; Article forty-six, the right to correct and supplement ; Article forty-seven, which lists five situations in which deletion is required, including 「（一）处理目的已实现、无法实现或者为实现处理目的不再必要」「（二）个人信息处理者停止提供产品或者服务，或者保存期限已届满」「（三）个人撤回同意」, and then 「个人信息处理者未删除的，个人有权请求删除」 ; Article fifty 「个人信息处理者应当建立便捷的个人行使权利的申请受理和处理机制。拒绝个人行使权利的请求的，应当说明理由」「个人可以依法向人民法院提起诉讼」
- Notes: Closing an account and deleting personal information are two different things, and after closing the account you still have to ask for the deletion separately. Before you change phones or sell the old one, sign out of every account on it and remove the bindings, and only then restore it to factory settings. What the law gives you is a right to delete afterwards, and it cannot take back what has already leaked out.

### 9. Face recognition is not something you have to agree to: if another way exists they cannot make it the only way, and if you refuse they must offer you another
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. When you are asked for a face scan, ask whether there is another way to verify. If they say there is not, require them to provide one. The hard part is asking in person.
- In plain language: As long as another way can get the same thing done, the other side cannot make a face scan the only option. If you refuse the scan, it has to give you another method such as a card, a password or an identity card, and it may not put pressure on you with 「then we cannot serve you」. In hotel rooms, public baths, changing rooms and toilets, nobody is allowed to install face recognition equipment.
- Benefit: The Measures for the Security of the Application of Face Recognition Technology (人脸识别技术应用安全管理办法) state: 「实现相同目的或者达到同等业务要求，存在其他非人脸识别技术方式的，不得将人脸识别技术作为唯一验证方式。个人不同意通过人脸信息进行身份验证的，应当提供其他合理、便捷的方式。」 — where the same purpose or the same business requirement can be met by a method other than face recognition, face recognition may not be the only form of verification, and a person who does not agree to identity verification through facial information must be given another reasonable and convenient method. The measures also state: 「任何组织和个人不得以办理业务、提升服务质量等为由，误导、欺诈、胁迫个人接受人脸识别技术验证个人身份。」 — no organisation or individual may mislead, deceive or coerce a person into accepting face recognition as identity verification on the pretext of handling a transaction or improving service quality. Processing facial information on the basis of your consent requires 「在充分知情的前提下自愿、明确作出的单独同意」, a separate consent given freely and expressly in full knowledge: you have to be asked about this one thing on its own, and only your separate nod counts. You have the right to withdraw consent, and the processor has to give you a convenient way to do it. Processing the facial information of a child under fourteen requires the consent of the parents or other guardians. A face recognition device installed in a public place 「应当为维护公共安全所必需」, it must be necessary for maintaining public safety, and it has to carry a clear sign. Inside the private spaces of public places such as hotel rooms, public baths, public changing rooms and public toilets, no organisation or individual may install one. Facial information is to be stored inside the face recognition device and may not be transmitted externally over the internet. There are two exceptions: where laws or administrative regulations provide otherwise, or where separate consent has been obtained (nationwide, in force from 2025-6-1)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室) and Ministry of Public Security (公安部) (2025). Measures for the Security of the Application of Face Recognition Technology, 人脸识别技术应用安全管理办法（第 19 号令，第十条、十二条、十三条，2025 年 6 月 1 日起施行）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/21/c_1744174262156096.htm>
- Notes: What you meet most often is a housing estate gate, a letting platform, a gym or a hotel asking you to record your face. When they say 「the system only supports face recognition」, read the words of the measures out to them. The words are: 「实现相同目的或者达到同等业务要求，存在其他非人脸识别技术方式的，不得将人脸识别技术作为唯一验证方式」 — where the same purpose or the same business requirement can be met by a method other than face recognition, it may not be the only form of verification. Then require them to give you another method such as a card, a password or an identity card. If they still will not, report it to the local cyberspace administration. Where the state has separate rules on face recognition for identity checks, such as in some financial and government settings, follow those rules. The biggest difference between a face and a password is that a leaked face cannot be changed, so it deserves more caution than a password does. A unit that stores facial information on more than 100,000 people has to file with the provincial or higher cyberspace administration within 30 working days, and asking about that is also a way to judge whether they are legitimate. For the right to see, correct and delete your own personal information, see item 8 in this section.
