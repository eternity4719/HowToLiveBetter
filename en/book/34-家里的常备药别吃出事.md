# 34. Do not mishandle the medicines at home

Fever reducers, painkillers, cold medicines, stomach medicines and anti-diarrhoeal medicines are mostly things you can buy yourself in a pharmacy. Being able to buy one without a prescription does not mean any way of taking it is safe. This section does not say which medicine to take for which illness. It picks only the items where a single action avoids a serious outcome.

Every item here is measured by mortality, including health outcomes such as liver failure, stomach bleeding and kidney failure in newborns. Most benefits are "one serious accident avoided". Research cannot give a figure for "how much less does doing it make you", so the size of each benefit is judged by how serious the outcome is, and the Notes field of each item says why.

What is written elsewhere is only pointed to here. What to do first after someone swallows a medicine or a cleaning product, see item 20 in section 13 (accidental swallowing). Taking a chronic illness medicine for as long as the doctor told you to, see item 1 in section 16 (take the medicine for as long as prescribed). Prescription medicines bought online have to pass prescription review first, see item 6 in section 28 (get the prescription at a hospital for weight-loss drugs).

In this section the beneficiaries are you and your family, which is the top two grades.

### 1. Before taking two cold medicines or two painkillers together, read the ingredient list: acetaminophen can only count once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Read the ingredient list on each box. It takes a minute or two.
- In plain language: Many cold medicines contain acetaminophen. Taking two boxes at once means taking double, and too much damages the liver. In the United States, nearly three in ten of the acute liver failure patients it causes die before they get a new liver. The Chinese package insert advises at most 2 grams a day, and no alcohol while you take it.
- Benefit: 22 United States hospitals registered 662 cases of acute liver failure over 6 years in a row. 275 of them (42%) were caused by acetaminophen, and the median dose swallowed was 24 grams. Of these 275 people, 131 (48%) had taken too much by accident, not on purpose. Of the people who took too much by accident, 38% were also taking two or more medicines containing this ingredient. Of these 275 people, 27% died before they got a new liver and 8% had a liver transplant. In 2020 the National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) required over-the-counter package inserts to state: overuse causes serious liver damage, and the maximum in one day should be no more than 2 grams. The insert must also state that you should avoid combining it with other medicines containing acetaminophen or other antipyretic analgesics. United States rules require the first liver warning on these over-the-counter products to name three situations: taking more than the daily maximum, taking them together with another medicine containing acetaminophen, and drinking more than 3 glasses of alcohol a day while you take them.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Larson AM, Polson J, Fontana RJ, et al.; Acute Liver Failure Study Group (2005). Acetaminophen-induced acute liver failure: results of a United States multicenter, prospective study. Hepatology, 42(6):1364-1372. <https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.20948>；National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) (2020). Announcement on Revising the Package Inserts for Immediate-Release and Extended-Release Acetaminophen Preparations (2020 No. 15), and Annex 2, Requirements for Revising the Package Insert of Immediate-Release Acetaminophen Over-the-Counter Medicines《对乙酰氨基酚常释制剂非处方药说明书修订要求》, reprinted by the Hunan Provincial Drug Administration (湖南省药品监督管理局). <http://mpa.hunan.gov.cn/mpa/xxgk/tzgg/wjtz/202003/t20200311_11809335.html>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(1)(iii)(A) Liver warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: Acetaminophen is also called paracetamol. Compound cold medicines whose generic name carries the two characters for acetaminophen (氨酚), such as those sold as acetaminophen with pseudoephedrine, or as acetaminophen, caffeine and chlorpheniramine, contain this ingredient. If you are not sure, take every box to the pharmacist in the shop and ask. The grade is B because the United States figures come from case registration with no control group, so nobody can work out how many incidents reading the ingredient list would prevent. The benefit is graded "large" by the severity of the outcome: close to three in ten acute liver failure patients die.
### 2. Do not give children aspirin, nimesulide or metamizole for a fever, and do not give minors any cold medicine that contains metamizole
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. When you buy a fever reducer, look at the generic name and the ingredient list.
- In plain language: When a child has a fever, do not use any of these three drugs. Aspirin is linked to Reye's syndrome, which damages the brain and the liver; in United States figures about three in ten affected children die, and after the warning was issued cases fell by more than 90%. Nimesulide is banned below 12 years old, and metamizole below 18.
- Benefit: Reye's syndrome causes encephalopathy and fatty change in the liver, and it usually appears after influenza or chickenpox. In the United States national surveillance from 1981 to 1997, 1207 cases were reported in people under 18. Salicylate, an ingredient of drugs such as aspirin, was measurable in the blood of 82% of the affected children. The overall fatality rate was 31%. Warnings for salicylate medicines began to be issued in 1980. That year 555 cases were reported, which was the peak; from 1987 there were no more than 36 cases a year. In 2011 the former State Food and Drug Administration (国家食药监局) ruled that oral nimesulide preparations are prohibited for children under 12. In 2020 the National Medical Products Administration ruled that metamizole tablets are prohibited for children and adolescents under 18. Compound products that contain metamizole, namely qinghao metamizole tablets, Zhongganling tablets and Zhongganling capsules, are likewise prohibited below 18.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Belay ED, Bresee JS, Holman RC, Khan AS, Shahriari A, Schonberger LB (1999). Reye's syndrome in the United States from 1981 through 1997. New England Journal of Medicine, 340(18):1377-1382. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199905063401801>；Notice on Strengthening the Administration of Oral Nimesulide Preparations (国食药监安〔2011〕209号). <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gzwj/gzwjyp/20110515145101552.html>；National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) (2020). Announcement on Revising the Package Inserts of Metamizole-Related Products (2020 No. 34), with Annexes 1 to 3. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20200317171201660.html>
- Notes: Metamizole is not the first choice for adults either. The insert says it may cause agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia and anaphylactic shock, and it is used for a short time only when the illness is severe and no other effective medicine is available. Zhongganling sounds like an ordinary cold medicine, and it contains metamizole. For which fever reducer a child should take, and how much by body weight, ask a paediatrician or a pharmacist. A child under 3 months old with a fever goes straight to hospital, see item 8 in section 20 (go straight to hospital at 38 ℃). The grade is B because the Reye's syndrome figures come from surveillance reports, and the fall in cases and the issue of the warning stand in sequence rather than in a controlled trial. The benefit is graded "large" because after the warning cases fell from 555 a year to fewer than 36.
### 3. If you are over 60, have bled from the stomach, or take anticoagulants or steroids, ask a doctor before taking a painkiller such as ibuprofen
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes asking a doctor or a pharmacist once.
- In plain language: Painkillers such as ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin harm the stomach. In trials at high doses, people taking ibuprofen had about 4 times the risk of stomach bleeding or perforation compared with people who did not take it. The risk is higher if you are older, have bled from the stomach, also take anticoagulants or steroids, or drink alcohol every day.
- Benefit: These drugs are called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and the common ones are ibuprofen, naproxen and diclofenac. A meta-analysis pooled 280 randomised trials of these drugs against placebo, 124513 people in total. Compared with placebo, the risk of upper gastrointestinal perforation, obstruction or bleeding: ibuprofen RR 3.97 (95% CI 2.22–7.10, about 4 times), naproxen 4.22 (2.71–6.56), diclofenac 1.89 (1.16–3.09). All of these drugs roughly double the risk of heart failure. United States rules require these over-the-counter products to carry a stomach bleeding warning. The warning names six groups at higher risk: those over 60, those who have had a stomach ulcer or bleeding, those taking anticoagulants or steroids, those taking another drug of this kind at the same time, those drinking more than 3 glasses of alcohol a day while they take them, and those who take more than the insert says or for longer than it says.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Coxib and traditional NSAID Trialists' (CNT) Collaboration; Bhala N, Emberson J, Merhi A, et al. (2013). Vascular and upper gastrointestinal effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs: meta-analyses of individual participant data from randomised trials. Lancet, 382(9894):769-779. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60900-9>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(2)(iii)(A) Stomach bleeding warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: The paper's conclusion is about high-dose use. The paper gives no separate figure for occasional use over a day or two at the dose on the insert. Many compound cold medicines also contain this ingredient, so taking them together with a painkiller repeats it; read the ingredient list first. If a doctor has put you on low-dose aspirin for the long term to prevent blood clots, do not stop it yourself, and do not add a second painkiller yourself.
### 4. Do not give a child under 2 compound cold medicines or cough medicines on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it also saves the money you would spend on medicines.
- In plain language: The National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) requires a warning on 14 compound cold medicines for children: parents are advised not to use them on a child under 2, and to use them under the guidance of a doctor or a pharmacist. In trials carried out on children, cough medicines and anti-allergy medicines did no better than placebo.
- Benefit: In 2021 the National Medical Products Administration revised the package inserts of 14 products under Announcement No. 57, among them the paracetamol, chlorpheniramine and dextromethorphan oral solution, paracetamol and chlorpheniramine granules for children, and paracetamol and amantadine granules for children. The new warning reads「不建议家长或监护人自行给2岁以下婴幼儿使用本品，应在医师或药师的指导下使用」, that is, parents or guardians are advised not to give this product to infants and toddlers under 2 on their own, and to use it under the guidance of a doctor or a pharmacist. The precautions add「应严格按照药品说明书用法用量使用，避免用药过量」, that is, use it strictly at the dose and usage on the package insert and avoid overdose. They also add「应避免合并使用含有相同或相似活性成份的抗感冒药」, that is, avoid using it together with other cold medicines that contain the same or a similar active ingredient. A Cochrane systematic review included 29 randomised controlled trials, 10 of them carried out on children. In the children's trials none of these classes of drug did better than placebo: cough medicines, antihistamines (the anti-allergy ingredients), antihistamines plus decongestants (the ingredients that clear the nose), and cough medicines plus bronchodilators. 21 studies reported adverse events, and the preparations containing antihistamines and dextromethorphan produced more of them.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) (2021). Announcement on Revising the Package Inserts of 14 Products Including the Paracetamol, Chlorpheniramine and Dextromethorphan Oral Solution (2021 No. 57), with annexes. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20210423105404141.html>；Smith SM, Schroeder K, Fahey T (2014). Over-the-counter (OTC) medications for acute cough in children and adults in community settings. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11):CD001831. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001831.pub5>
- Notes: Many of these 14 products carry the two characters for acetaminophen (氨酚) in their names, and they contain acetaminophen, so taking them together with a fever reducer repeats it; see item 1 in this section (acetaminophen). One trial in that review found honey better than placebo, but honey must not be given below 1 year of age, see item 6 in section 20 (honey). The grade is B because the review holds few trials for each class of drug and the authors could not pool them into a figure. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: no benefit has been shown for children, and the risks are overdose and repeated ingredients.
### 5. After 20 weeks of pregnancy, do not take a painkiller such as ibuprofen on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain language: After 20 weeks of pregnancy, most of the amniotic fluid is made by the fetus's kidneys. Taking ibuprofen or a drug like it then can harm the fetus's kidneys and reduce the amniotic fluid. The United States has received reports in which newborns died. The low-dose aspirin a doctor prescribes is not covered by this.
- Benefit: The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a safety warning in 2020-10. Using non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs at about 20 weeks of pregnancy or later can cause fetal kidney problems and lead to low amniotic fluid. The warning covers prescription and over-the-counter medicines alike, including aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and celecoxib. The FDA examined adverse event reports up to 2017-7 and found 35 cases of low amniotic fluid or newborn renal impairment, all of them severe outcomes. 5 were newborn deaths, all with newborn renal failure. In the literature, most cases of low amniotic fluid resolved between 72 hours and 6 days after the drug was stopped. The FDA advises pregnant women not to use these drugs after 20 weeks unless a doctor says so deliberately. Low-dose aspirin of 81 milligrams, prescribed by a doctor for specific problems in pregnancy, is not covered by this advice. When you need pain relief or a fever reducer, the FDA mentions that other drugs such as acetaminophen can be used; ask a pharmacist or a doctor first.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2020-10-15). FDA recommends avoiding use of NSAIDs in pregnancy at 20 weeks or later because they can result in low amniotic fluid. Drug Safety Communication. <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic>
- Notes: The FDA warns that many over-the-counter medicines for colds, flu and insomnia also contain these ingredients, so read the ingredient list. United States over-the-counter labelling used to warn only about the last 3 months of pregnancy; this time the line moved back to about 20 weeks. Low-dose aspirin for the prevention of pre-eclampsia, see item 5 in section 27 (low-dose aspirin). The grade is B because the figures come from adverse event reports, and there is no way to know how many people took the drug without harm. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: the outcome is serious, but it is rare.

### 6. Buy omeprazole yourself for acid reflux and heartburn, and take it for at most 7 days; if you have difficulty swallowing, vomit blood or pass black stool, go straight to hospital
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Take it for the number of days the insert gives.
- In plain language: Omeprazole enteric-coated tablets can be bought without a prescription from 2022, but the insert says not to take them yourself for more than 7 days, and not to take them again within two months. Do not take them yourself if you have difficulty or pain when swallowing, vomit blood, or pass blood or black stool; these can be signs of something serious. This medicine can also cover up the symptoms of stomach cancer and delay the diagnosis.
- Benefit: In 2022 the National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) moved omeprazole enteric-coated tablets from prescription to over-the-counter under Announcement No. 68, and issued a package insert template at the same time. The indications in the template are「胃酸过多引起的烧心和反酸症状的短期缓解」, that is, short-term relief of heartburn and acid reflux caused by excess stomach acid. The precautions state: use must not exceed 7 days, and if the symptoms do not ease, ask a doctor or a pharmacist. Do not take it again within two months, and if the symptoms keep coming back, see a doctor at once. Do not use it if you have difficulty or pain when swallowing, vomit blood, or pass blood or black stool; these can be signs of something serious. If alarm symptoms such as marked weight loss, repeated vomiting, difficulty swallowing, vomiting blood or black stool appear, a malignant tumour has to be ruled out first, because treatment may cover up the symptoms and delay the diagnosis. If you are over 55 and new symptoms appear or the symptoms change, ask a doctor. People taking clopidogrel should avoid taking omeprazole at the same time.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) (2022). Announcement on the Conversion of Omeprazole Enteric-Coated Tablets from a Prescription Medicine to an Over-the-Counter Medicine (2022 No. 68), and Annex 2, Over-the-Counter Package Insert Template《非处方药说明书范本》. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/yaopin/ypggtg/20220826171517123.html>
- Notes: Clopidogrel is the anti-clotting drug that people who have had a stent put in or have had a heart attack often take. Omeprazole weakens it, so if you take it, ask your doctor first. The grade is B because these are the rules in the package insert, and no study has worked out how many incidents following them would prevent. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: what it prevents is a stomach bleed or stomach cancer being caught late, but most acid reflux and heartburn is not serious.
### 7. Do not ask a doctor for antibiotics for an ordinary cold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it also saves the money you would spend on medicines.
- In plain language: Antibiotics for a cold do not make you recover any faster than not taking them. In adults the risk of side effects is more than twice as high as with no antibiotic. For a runny nose with pus lasting less than 10 days, antibiotics have no established benefit.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review pooled 6 randomised trials with 1147 people. Antibiotics for a cold compared with placebo showed no clear difference in the proportion who did not improve or whose symptoms went on (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.60–1.14, not significant). The risk of an adverse reaction was 1.8 times that of the placebo group (95% CI 1.01–3.21). In adults it was 2.62 times (1.32–5.18); in children there was no increase (0.91, 0.51–1.63). For acute purulent rhinitis with a runny nose of less than 10 days, antibiotics had no clear benefit either (RR 0.73, 0.47–1.13). Adverse reactions were higher instead (RR 1.46, 1.10–1.94, about 46% higher). The authors conclude that routine use of antibiotics is not recommended in either situation.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Kenealy T, Arroll B (2025). Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 11:CD000247. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000247.pub4>
- Notes: This version was republished in 2025, and the abstract states that the literature search ran up to 2013. Antibiotics are prescription medicines in China, so a pharmacy has to sell them against a prescription; do not take leftover antibiotics from home either. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: what it prevents is adverse reactions, not death.
### 8. When you have diarrhoea, start with oral rehydration salts; do not give anti-diarrhoeal medicines to children under 5
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A box of oral rehydration salts costs a few yuan to a dozen-odd yuan. Keep one box at home.
- In plain language: The most important thing in diarrhoea is replacing fluid, not stopping the diarrhoea. Mix oral rehydration salts with water as the insert says. Do not use juice drinks instead; they are too sweet and can leave you even shorter of fluid. Do not give children under 5 anti-diarrhoeal medicines such as loperamide; in severe cases these cause intestinal obstruction, which can be fatal.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization's manual on the treatment of diarrhoea states that "anti-diarrhoeal" medicines and antiemetics are of no real benefit in acute or persistent diarrhoea in children. They cannot prevent dehydration or improve nutrition. Some have dangerous and sometimes fatal side effects and must never be given to children under 5. Medicines that slow the movement of the intestine, such as loperamide, can cause severe paralytic ileus, which can be fatal, and can prolong an infection. The manual also states that drinks with too much sugar draw water out of the body into the intestine and cause hypernatraemic dehydration, for example soft drinks and packaged juice drinks. The WHO manual recommends a hypotonic formulation with a total osmolarity of 245 mOsm/L. Compared with the older formulation, it reduced unplanned intravenous infusions by 33%. A Cochrane review pooling 8 trials found a similar result (OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, roughly two fifths fewer).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: World Health Organization (2005). The treatment of diarrhoea: a manual for physicians and other senior health workers, 4th revision. <https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43209>；Hahn S, Kim S, Garner P (2002). Reduced osmolarity oral rehydration solution for treating dehydration caused by acute diarrhoea in children. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (1):CD002847. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002847>
- Notes: When you buy it, check the osmolarity on the insert and choose the hypotonic formulation. The manual states that blood in a child's stool is most often bacterial dysentery, which needs a doctor and antibiotics; rehydrating at home alone is not enough. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: the figures compare two rehydration solutions, not drinking against not drinking.
### 9. Do not take painkillers for more than 15 days a month, or compound painkillers for more than 10 days, or the headache may be the medicine
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to keep count of the days you take medicine, and you have to hold back from taking it whenever you reach for it.
- In plain language: If your headache keeps coming back and you take a painkiller every day, the pain may get worse the more you take. The international headache classification calls this medication-overuse headache. Plain painkillers taken 15 or more days a month, compound painkillers 10 or more days, for more than 3 months in a row, are enough to meet it. Most people improve after they stop the drug they were taking too much of.
- Benefit: The third edition of the International Classification of Headache Disorders of the International Headache Society sets out the diagnostic criteria. A person who already has a primary headache disorder, who has headache on 15 or more days a month, and who has regularly overused headache medicines for more than 3 months. For non-opioid painkillers such as acetaminophen, aspirin and ibuprofen, the threshold for overuse is 15 or more days a month. For compound painkillers the threshold is 10 or more days a month. A compound painkiller is two or more kinds of pain-relieving drug put together, or one with an added helper ingredient such as caffeine, for example acetaminophen with codeine. If you take several non-opioid painkillers in turn, add the days up. The classification states that epidemiological evidence from several countries shows that of the people with headache on 15 or more days a month, more than half have this type of headache. Most patients improve after they stop the drug they have overused, and preventive treatment for headache works better too.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (2018). The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition. Cephalalgia, 38(1):1-211. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0333102417738202>；ICHD-3 online version 8.2 Medication-overuse headache (including 8.2.3 and 8.2.5). <https://ichd-3.org/8-headache-attributed-to-a-substance-or-its-withdrawal/8-2-medication-overuse-headache-moh/>
- Notes: A painkiller whose name carries the character for coffee (咖) usually has caffeine in it, and the limit for a compound painkiller is 10 days. If the number of days you have a headache in a month keeps rising, see a neurologist instead of only adding medicines. The grade is B because the classification gives a diagnostic threshold, not a controlled trial. The benefit is graded "medium" as a judgement: what it buys back is days without headache, and it has nothing to do with death.
### 10. Clear out expired medicines and leftovers you will never use on a regular schedule, packaging and all, into the hazardous waste bin; do not leave them at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Go through the medicine cabinet once a year; it takes a dozen-odd minutes.
- In plain language: Nearly half the medicines young children swallow without their parents knowing are prescription tablets. About 30% of the prescription drugs they swallow are their grandparents'. Clearing out what nobody uses means one less thing a child can find. Put what you clear out into the hazardous waste bin with its packaging, or hand it in at a pharmacy that takes old medicines back.
- Benefit: US emergency department surveillance data estimate that between 2004 and 2013 children under 6 who swallowed a medicine without their parents knowing accounted for about 640,000 emergency department visits (estimated figure 640161). In 2013 alone there were about 59,000 (59092). Between 2010 and 2013 the commonest were oral prescription tablets, at 45.9%. Among prescription drugs the commonest were opioid painkillers (13.8%) and benzodiazepine sleeping pills and sedatives (12.7%). 5 US poison control centres surveyed the parents who called for help in 2017. Where a child had swallowed a prescription drug, 30.7% had swallowed a grandparent's. More than half (51.5%) of the prescription drugs had already been taken out of their original packaging by an adult before the child got hold of them. China's National Hazardous Waste Catalogue (2025 edition)《国家危险废物名录（2025 年版）》lists waste medicines and their packaging produced in everyday household life as hazardous waste in domestic waste. Shanghai's sorting guide puts waste medicines in the hazardous waste category and states that it includes expired medicines, medicine capsules, tablets and medicine packaging, with 「废弃药品宜连带包装一并投放」, that is, waste medicines should be put in with their packaging. Shanghai also provides that waste and expired medicines collected by pharmacies from households are put in temporary transfer points set up by each district's market regulation department. The US FDA says the best way to deal with expired and unwanted medicines is to hand them in at a collection point.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lovegrove MC, Weidle NJ, Budnitz DS (2015). Trends in Emergency Department Visits for Unsupervised Pediatric Medication Exposures, 2004-2013. Pediatrics, 136(4):e821-e829. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2015-2092> ; Agarwal M, Lovegrove MC, Geller RJ, et al. (2020). Circumstances Involved in Unsupervised Solid Dose Medication Exposures among Young Children. Journal of Pediatrics, 219:188-195.e6. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2019.12.027> ; Ministry of Ecology and Environment (生态环境部) and others (2024). ### 11. Do not drink alcohol while you are on cephalosporins, metronidazole or similar antibiotics, or in the 7 days after you stop; leave alone medicines that contain alcohol such as Huoxiang Zhengqi water and cough syrup
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Not drinking for a bit over a week, and looking at the ingredients to see whether a medicine contains ethanol before you take anything else.
- In plain language: Some cephalosporins, metronidazole and drugs like them, mixed with alcohol, make your face flush, your heart race, your chest tight and make you vomit; in severe cases your blood pressure drops. It is not only spirits and beer: Huoxiang Zhengqi water, cough syrup and other medicines containing alcohol can set it off too. Keep away from all of them while you are taking the antibiotic and for 7 days after you stop.
- Benefit: This is called a disulfiram-like reaction. A 2023 piece on safe medication use from the Fujian Provincial Drug Administration says that drinking alcohol before or after taking some cephalosporin medicines can trigger this reaction, and 「会严重损害健康甚至导致生命危险」, that it can seriously damage health and even endanger life. The symptoms include flushing of the face, sweating and palpitations, and in some cases difficulty breathing, falling blood pressure and anaphylactic shock. The drugs it lists, following the Clinical Drug Use Information of the Pharmacopoeia of the People's Republic of China《中华人民共和国药典临床用药须知》, include cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefoperazone, metronidazole, tinidazole and furazolidone. It states that 「用药后 7 天内禁止饮酒和食用含酒精的药物、食物等」, that for 7 days after taking the medicine you must not drink alcohol or take medicines, foods and so on that contain alcohol, giving chocolate liqueurs and Huoxiang Zhengqi water as examples. The reason is that the enzyme the drug inhibits commonly takes 4 to 5 days to recover. A hospital in Guangzhou compiled 82 cases over 2 years. 57 were caused by cephalosporins (69.51%) and 10 by nitroimidazoles (metronidazole, tinidazole and the like) (12.20%). In 40 cases the alcohol came from an alcoholic drink (48.78%), and in 31 from a medicine containing ethanol (37.80%). The swallowed medicines included Huoxiang Zhengqi water, cough syrup and Leonurus liquid extract. Of the 82 cases 68 were mild, 9 moderate and 5 severe; all eased within 1 to 3 hours, and none died. The US metronidazole instructions require no alcohol during treatment and for at least 3 days after stopping, and also say not to use products containing propylene glycol.
- Evidence grade: B

- Notes: Not every cephalosporin does this, but an ordinary person cannot tell which is which, so following this rule whichever one you are on is the simplest course. When a doctor prescribes a cephalosporin or metronidazole, say of your own accord whether you have been drinking in the last few days. That popularisation piece advises not drinking for 3 days before the medicine either, while admitting there is no authoritative view on the length of that interval. The moment your face flushes and your heart races, stop anything containing alcohol. The popularisation piece says mild cases settle on their own and severe ones need a hospital. Do not drink while taking acetaminophen either, see item 1 in this section (acetaminophen). It is graded B because the evidence is a set of case records and package inserts, with nothing to compare against. The benefit is graded small: all 82 cases eased within a few hours, nobody died, and only 5 were severe.
