# 3. 不要浪费精力

本节只算精力和时间账。看的是四样东西：你每天能用的注意力、反应快不快、出错多不多、被打断以后要多久才回得来。所有数字都是直接测出来的，来自实验，或者来自跟踪一群人做的记录（队列研究）。这些数字不换算成寿命，也不换算成死亡率。本节多数条目的证据等级是 B。这个领域本来就这样，不代表建议不成立。第 20 条讲的是和警察、医生、柜员打交道时该有的预期，引的是法规，不是实验，所以等级记 C。第 15 条（把悲观念头当症状看）和第 23 条（把「别人要求我完美」当症状看）引的是死亡率与自杀风险的数字，口径和本节其他条目不一样，不放在一起比较。

### 1. Turn off notifications you do not need, and keep the phone out of sight while you work
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Changing the settings takes a few minutes and needs almost no willpower.
- In plain language: When the phone buzzes, your score on an attention task drops even if you neither look at it nor touch it. The drop is about the same as when you actually answer a call or send a text. There is a second case: the phone is simply lying on the desk, you resist looking at it, and the thinking capacity left to you still goes down. The more dependent you are on your phone, the larger the drop.
- Benefit: Merely receiving a notification, without looking at it or touching it, clearly worsens performance on an attention task. The worsening is on a par with actually taking a call or sending a text. This was done in a laboratory, with university students as participants. Two other experiments show that even when people successfully resist looking at the phone, having their own phone next to them still reduces the thinking capacity available to them. People with the strongest phone dependence dropped the most.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015). The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance. <https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000100> ; Ward, Duke, Gneezy & Bos (2017). Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity. Journal of the Association for Consumer Research. <https://doi.org/10.1086/691462>
- Notes: All of these are single tasks in a laboratory. Nobody has measured what happens when you add them up day after day. If your job really has to answer at any hour, leave notifications on for a small number of contacts.
### 2. Keep a fixed wake-up time, at weekends too
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Lying in less at weekends takes some willpower.
- In plain language: In the most irregular fifth, the body clock runs about 2.5 hours later than in the most regular fifth. That amounts to living in a time zone shift for years. The more regular the schedule, the better the grades.
- Benefit: 61 university students kept records for 30 days. In the most irregular fifth, the body clock (circadian rhythm) ran about 2.5 hours later than in the most regular fifth. Concretely, melatonin started being released at 00:08 against 21:32. The period of peak sleepiness also came about 1.8 hours later. The more regular the schedule, the better the academic performance (correlation coefficient r = 0.37).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Phillips et al. (2017). Irregular sleep/wake patterns are associated with poorer academic performance and delayed circadian and sleep/wake timing. Scientific Reports. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03171-4>
- Notes: There is only this one study, with few participants and no control group. The authors infer that the body clock difference comes mainly from how much daylight each group gets. A fixed wake-up time therefore only works if you also get daylight in the morning. For how to recover after an occasional late night, see item 39 in section 2 (sleep earlier the following night): go to bed early that night, not until midday the next day.
### 3. Sleep 7 to 8 hours a night, and do not treat 6 hours as enough
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to free 1 to 2 hours a day for sleep. Going to bed earlier takes some willpower.
- In plain language: Two weeks of 6 hours of sleep a night drops brain performance to the level of going two days and two nights without closing your eyes. The trouble is that you cannot feel it. Self-rated sleepiness rises in the first few days and then stops rising. People on 6 hours rate themselves just as sleepy as people on 4 hours. So the sentence "I get by on 6 hours" is itself a symptom of sleep loss.
- Benefit: 48 healthy adults aged 21 to 38 slept 6 hours a night for 14 days in a row. Their cognitive performance fell cumulatively, down to the level of 2 nights with no sleep at all. Self-rated sleepiness rose only in the first few days and then barely rose again. The 6-hour group and the 4-hour group did not differ in self-rated sleepiness. This was a controlled trial that randomly assigned people to two groups (an RCT). Separately, short periods of no sleep (acute sleep deprivation, no more than 48 hours) have an effect size of g = -0.776 on mind-wandering in simple attention tasks and g = -0.125 on reasoning accuracy. That part pools 70 studies.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges (2003). The cumulative cost of additional wakefulness. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/26.2.117> ; Lim & Dinges (2010). A meta-analysis of the impact of short-term sleep deprivation on cognitive variables. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018883>
- Notes: "I don't feel tired" is not evidence that you slept enough. People who sleep 6 hours a night for years only feel used to it; their performance has not caught up. The part that pools several studies is about staying awake for a whole night. Only one randomised controlled trial supports "sleeping less over a long period".
### 4. No caffeine after 2 p.m.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. If you habitually drink coffee in the afternoon, holding out takes some willpower.
- In plain language: A large cup of coffee (400 mg) six hours before bed cuts measured sleep that night by more than an hour. A self-written sleep diary shows no difference at all. An ordinary cup of coffee (107 mg) has to be finished more than 8.8 hours before bed if it is not to disturb sleep. If you go to bed at eleven, the line falls at two in the afternoon.
- Benefit: Taking 400 mg of caffeine 6 hours before bed, measured total sleep time was 1.1 to 1.2 hours shorter than with a placebo. A placebo here is a control drink with no caffeine in it. The participants' own sleep diaries did not report any difference, so they did not notice it. Pooling several studies (meta-analysis), caffeine cuts total sleep time by 45 minutes on average. The share of the time in bed actually spent asleep (sleep efficiency) falls 7%. A cup of coffee (107 mg) has to be finished more than 8.8 hours before bed if it is not to disturb sleep.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Drake, Roehrs, Shambroom & Roth (2013). Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed. Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.3170> ; Gardiner et al. (2023). The effect of caffeine on subsequent sleep: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Sleep Medicine Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2023.101764>
- Notes: The Drake study was run in a single laboratory, and the 400 mg it used is on the high side, roughly two or three cups of coffee. If you metabolise caffeine slowly, your cutoff has to be earlier still. Cutting back on caffeine buys back sleep duration, and that adds to item 3 (sleep 7 to 8 hours a night).
### 5. Handle email and messages in a few fixed batches a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes a little time to tell colleagues how often you will reply. Resisting the urge to check email at any moment takes some willpower.
- In plain language: The same people were compared over two weeks. In the week when email was limited to 3 checks a day, daily stress was clearly lower, while the number of emails sent and received was the same. Without a limit, people checked email 15.5 times a day on average.
- Benefit: 124 adults were compared with themselves before and after over two weeks. In the week when email was limited to 3 checks a day, daily stress was clearly lower than in the week with no limit (effect size Cohen's d = 0.37). The number of emails received and replied to over the two weeks did not differ. At the start, with no limit, people checked email 15.5 times a day on average.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Kushlev & Dunn (2015). Checking email less frequently reduces stress. Computers in Human Behavior. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.11.005>
- Notes: One study only, with a small-to-moderate effect, and it measured stress rather than work output. Nobody has studied instant messaging directly, so that part is an inference by analogy.
### 6. When the work needs continuous thought, block out interruptions of even a few seconds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Close the door, put on headphones, or hang up a do-not-disturb sign.
- In plain language: An interruption averaging 2.8 seconds doubles the chance of an error in the operations that follow. An interruption of 4.4 seconds triples it. Field observation also found that it takes an average of 25 minutes to return to the interrupted task, with two other tasks inserted in between. People who are interrupted work faster, but report more stress, more frustration and more effort.
- Benefit: An interruption averaging 2.8 seconds doubles the error rate on tasks carried out step by step in order (sequential tasks). An interruption of 4.4 seconds triples it. This was done in a laboratory. A separate study observed 24 office workers (knowledge workers) in the field. They switched the subject of their work every 11 minutes on average. 57% of work episodes were interrupted. Returning to the interrupted task the same day took 25 minutes 26 seconds on average. 2.26 other tasks were inserted in between. A third experiment with 48 people found that interrupted people worked faster but reported more stress, more frustration, more time pressure and more effort.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Altmann, Trafton & Hambrick (2014). Momentary interruptions can derail the train of thought. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030986> ; Mark, Gonzalez & Harris (2005). No task left behind? Examining the nature of fragmented work. CHI 2005. <https://doi.org/10.1145/1054972.1055017> ; Mark, Gudith & Klocke (2008). The cost of interrupted work: More speed and stress. CHI 2008. <https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357072>
- Notes: That field study split interruptions into two kinds: ones where somebody else comes to you, and ones you open yourself. Closing the door only blocks the first kind. The second has to be handled together with item 1 (turn off notifications you do not need) and item 5 (handle messages in batches).
### 7. Do one thing at a time, and do not answer messages during a meeting
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Holding back from answering messages during a meeting takes some willpower.
- In plain language: Every time you switch tasks you react more slowly and make more mistakes. People who habitually keep several screens open at once have not trained any skill at doing several things at the same time. They do worse on task-switching tests instead.
- Benefit: After a task switch, reactions are clearly slower and errors more likely. That part comes from a review of several studies. A separate laboratory study used university students. People who routinely use several kinds of media at once (heavy media multitaskers) are more easily pulled off course by irrelevant things and irrelevant memories. They do worse on task-switching tests.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Monsell (2003). Task switching. Trends in Cognitive Sciences. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7> ; Ophir, Nass & Wagner (2009). Cognitive control in media multitaskers. PNAS. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0903620106>
- Notes: How much extra time switching back and forth costs varies a lot with the kind of work, so no single figure is given here. The Ophir study is a comparison at one point in time, and it cannot separate "multitasking worsens attention" from "people with worse attention multitask more".
### 8. No light-emitting screens in the hour before bed; read paper or an e-ink screen instead
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Putting the phone down in the hour before bed means fighting habit over the long run, and that needs willpower.
- In plain language: Reading a light-emitting screen before bed means falling asleep about 10 minutes later than reading paper. Melatonin is suppressed by 55%. The body clock the next day is pushed back by more than 1.5 hours, so the next morning is sleepier and takes longer to become fully awake.
- Benefit: In the laboratory the same people did the two conditions in turn (a crossover trial). Reading a light-emitting e-book before bed took about 10 minutes longer to fall asleep than reading paper (25.7 minutes against 15.8 minutes). Evening melatonin was suppressed by 55%. The body clock (circadian rhythm) the next day was delayed by more than 1.5 hours, with melatonin release starting at 22:31 against 21:01. The next morning is sleepier, and it takes longer to be fully awake.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler (2015). Evening use of light-emitting eReaders negatively affects sleep, circadian timing, and next-morning alertness. PNAS. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418490112>
- Notes: Few participants, in a laboratory with the conditions well controlled. At everyday phone brightness and everyday usage length the effect may be smaller. How much the phone's night mode helps was not measured in this study.

### 9. Sleep when bedtime comes; do not stay up for games, short videos or pornography
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is fighting the "one more episode" habit over the long run. Keeping the phone out of the bedroom is the least effort.
- In plain language: In 2025 people in China slept an average of 6.97 hours at night and fell asleep at 0:10 on average. People who use a phone or tablet before bed are more than twice as likely to get too little sleep, and 2.7 times as likely to be sleepy during the day. The figures are about the same when the device is merely present in the bedroom and not used. So the simplest method is to keep the phone out of the bedroom.
- Benefit: In 2025 residents of China slept an average of 6.97 hours at night, and the average time of falling asleep was 0:10 (Chinese Sleep Research Society (中国睡眠研究会)). People who use a portable device before bed are about 2.17 times as likely to get too little sleep (OR 2.17), about 1.46 times as likely to have poor sleep quality (OR 1.46) and about 2.72 times as likely to be sleepy during the day (OR 2.72). With the device merely present in the bedroom and not used, the figures are about the same: 1.79, 1.53 and 2.27 times (OR 1.79/1.53/2.27). That part pools 20 cross-sectional surveys of 125198 children and adolescents. A separate cross-sectional survey: among 844 adults aged 18 to 94, people who still used a phone after lights out took longer to fall asleep, had a lower share of the time in bed actually spent asleep, and were more tired during the day.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Carter et al. (2016). Association Between Portable Screen-Based Media Device Access or Use and Sleep Outcomes. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.2341> ; Exelmans & Van den Bulck (2016). Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2015.11.037> ; Chinese Sleep Research Society (中国睡眠研究会) (2026). 2026 China Sleep Health Research White Paper, as reported by Health News and reposted by the Fujian Provincial Health Commission (福建省卫健委). <https://wjw.fujian.gov.cn/jggk/csxx/xcc/mtbd/202603/t20260323_7114382.htm> ; Grubbs JB et al. (2018). Pornography Problems Due to Moral Incongruence: An Integrative Model with a Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Archives of Sexual Behavior. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-018-1248-x> (one of the two sides in the disputed note below) ; Prause N, Pfaus J (2015). Viewing Sexual Stimuli Associated with Greater Sexual Responsiveness, Not Erectile Dysfunction. Sexual Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1002/sm2.58> (the other side in the disputed note below)
- Notes:Disputed. The cost of staying up late itself is in item 3 in this section (sleep 7 to 8 hours a night). What pornography does to ordinary people's energy and thinking cannot be pinned down by the evidence at present, nor even attributed to it. One analysis pooling several studies argues that what gets called a "pornography problem" comes mostly from a mismatch between a person's beliefs and their own behaviour, not from the material itself. Another study pooled data on 280 men. The result was that hours watched had no relation to erectile function during sex with a partner. This entry is only about the sleep time it takes up. For the two figures, 6.97 hours and 0:10, the Chinese Sleep Research Society has not posted the release on its own website (checked in September 2026), so the Health News report reposted by the Fujian Provincial Health Commission is what this entry relies on.
### 10. No major decisions late at night, and no urgent messages
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. You only push things until after you have slept.
- In plain language: When you are rested you learn quickly to avoid the high-risk options. After two nights without sleep you pick the high-risk one time after time instead, and the older you are the clearer that becomes. Nobody has measured how much a few hours of lost sleep matters, but push important decisions until after you have slept.
- Benefit: 34 healthy people did the Iowa Gambling Task after 49.5 hours without sleep. It is an experiment used to measure risky decision-making. When they were rested they learned quickly to avoid the high-risk options. After sleep loss they picked the high-risk options more often instead. Older people were affected more. This is one experiment, and there is only this one study.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Killgore, Balkin & Wesensten (2006). Impaired decision making following 49 h of sleep deprivation. Journal of Sleep Research. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2869.2006.00487.x>
- Notes: The study covers the extreme case of two nights with no sleep at all. What happens to an ordinary person who stays up until two in the morning has not been measured directly. The "do not send messages" half is the author's own experience (grade C), and sending them the next day is not too late.
### 11. If you are sleepy in the afternoon, sleep 10 minutes, not half an hour
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes 10 minutes, plus finding somewhere to lie down or lean.
- In plain language: A 10-minute nap wakes you up on the spot, and the effect lasts two and a half hours. A 5-minute nap does almost nothing. A 20-minute nap only takes effect after half an hour. A 30-minute nap leaves you groggy on waking, and performance in that spell is worse than not sleeping at all.
- Benefit: After an insufficient night, a 10-minute nap immediately improves sleepiness, fatigue, energy and cognitive performance, and part of the benefit lasts past 155 minutes. A 5-minute nap brings almost no benefit. The benefit of a 20-minute nap appears only after 35 minutes. A 30-minute nap is followed by a stretch of sleep inertia: performance is briefly worse just after waking, and improves afterwards.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Brooks & Lack (2006). A brief afternoon nap following nocturnal sleep restriction: Which nap duration is most recuperative? Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831>
- Notes: One laboratory study only, and all the participants were healthy young people. If you cannot sleep, there is no need to force it. There is no research support for lying down with your eyes closed for 10 minutes. Napping for more than an hour is instead linked to higher mortality and coronary heart disease risk: see item 38 in section 2 (keep the nap under half an hour).
### 12. When you work in an open-plan office, use earplugs or a quiet room for work that needs memory
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Earplugs cost a few tens of yuan. Booking a quiet meeting room takes a little time.
- In plain language: When background noise rises from 39 decibels to 51 decibels, fewer words are remembered, people get more tired, and they want to work less. During a break, watching nature footage with sound restores energy better than listening to office noise.
- Benefit: Students worked in a simulated open-plan office. When background noise rose from 39 decibels to 51 decibels (dB, LAeq), they remembered fewer words, rated themselves as more tired, and wanted to work less. During breaks, watching nature footage with sound lifted their energy rating more than listening to river sounds or office noise alone.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Jahncke, Hygge, Halin, Green & Dimberg (2011). Open-plan office noise: Cognitive performance and restoration. Journal of Environmental Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2011.07.002>
- Notes: One laboratory study only. The most damaging part of the noise is speech you can make out. White-noise headphones help with that, but this study did not test headphones.
### 13. Do not work more than 49 hours a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: You may earn some overtime pay less. You may also have to spend effort negotiating with your manager.
- In plain language: Records from British armaments factories during the First World War show that within 49 hours a week, output rises in proportion to hours worked. Past 49 hours, each extra hour adds less and less. A week of 70 hours produces almost the same as 56 hours, and the extra 14 hours are wasted.
- Benefit: Output records for workers at British armaments factories in the First World War: within 49 hours a week, output is proportional to hours worked. Past 49 hours the rate of growth in output slows down. The maximum arrives at about 63 hours. Output at 70 hours a week is almost the same as at 56 hours, so the extra 14 hours are wasted.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Pencavel (2015). The Productivity of Working Hours. The Economic Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ecoj.12166>
- Notes: The data comes from factories a hundred years ago doing physical work, and the workers were mostly women. The threshold may sit somewhere else in mental work, and the author himself says other occupations may be higher or lower. What is reliable is the direction: past a certain point, more hours add almost no output. The specific figure of 49 is not reliable.
### 14. When you notice yourself replaying the same bad thing, switch to a job that needs your hands
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is noticing that you are replaying it (this is called rumination), and noticing takes practice.
- In plain language: Replaying the same bad thing in your head does not produce an answer. It worsens depression, fixes negative thoughts more firmly in place, makes problems harder to solve and harder to act on, and wears out the people near you who are willing to listen. When you notice you are going round in circles, go and do something with your hands.
- Benefit: A review of several studies says that replaying the same bad thing in your head (rumination) worsens depression. It also strengthens negative thinking and weakens the ability to solve problems. It gets in the way of actual action, and it wears out the people near you who are willing to listen. It is associated with anxiety, binge eating, heavy drinking and self-harm.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky (2008). Rethinking Rumination. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6924.2008.00088.x>
- Notes: The review gives direction only, with no numbers. Switching to another activity is the author's own practice. One sentence is all the literature supports: diverting your attention beats going round in circles. Nobody has compared which activity to switch to.
### 15. Treat "things will definitely get worse" thoughts as a symptom, not as a fact
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is treating such a thought as a symptom instead of as a fact.
- In plain language: In a twin study followed for 20 years, each step up in pessimism came with about 13% higher mortality over the same period, and about 20% higher risk of dying from cardiovascular disease. In the other direction, a high optimism score brought no matching benefit. What needs dealing with is pessimism, not "not being positive enough".
- Benefit: 2978 Australian twins over the age of 50, followed for 20 years on average, during which 1068 of them died. Each 1 standard deviation higher on the pessimism scale came with about 13% higher mortality over the same period (risk ratio 1.134). The credible range for that figure is 95% CI 1.065–1.207, P=8.85×10⁻⁵. Cardiovascular mortality was about 20% higher (1.196, 1.045–1.368, P=0.0093). There was no relation to cancer mortality. Optimism scores showed no clear association with mortality either.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Whitfield JB, Zhu G, Landers JG, Martin NG (2020). Pessimism is associated with greater all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, but optimism is not protective. Scientific Reports, 10, 12609. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-69388-y>
- Notes:Disputed. This is observation only, with no groups. Some would say that people become pessimistic because they are already ill, and the authors answer that if that were true, cancer mortality would be related as well. Unmeasured factors may still be there. Optimism had no protective effect, so there is no need to force yourself into "positive thinking". What matters is not treating a pessimistic expectation as an accurate forecast of the future.
### 16. Cut down on the relationships that drain you, and learn to refuse requests you do not want
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. There will be some social friction in the short term, and you may lose some relationships. Saying no takes real willpower.
- In plain language: One person who drains you does more damage, and does it more reliably, than one supportive person brings benefit. So subtract before you add.
- Benefit: A survey of 120 widowed women aged 60 to 89: contact that leaves you feeling worse is more consistently and more strongly linked to psychological well-being than contact that feels comfortable. On this result, the damage done by one person who drains you is larger than the benefit brought by one person who supports you.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Rook (1984). The negative side of social interaction: Impact on psychological well-being. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.46.5.1097>
- Notes: The group surveyed is unusual, and it was a one-off questionnaire, so the order of events is not visible. What it measured is well-being, not energy. Placing it in this section is the author's inference. There is no objective standard for which relationships count as "draining", so you have to judge that yourself.

### 17. Fix decisions of low value, such as what to wear and what to eat
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. At the start you spend a little time setting the default options.
- In plain language: This entry has no hard evidence behind it. The original experiment on "making choices drains willpower" did not come out in two large replications. One of them involved two thousand people and the other three thousand, with several labs working together, and the effect was basically zero. Fixing what you wear and what to eat is still worth doing, because it saves those few minutes and those few moments of dithering, not because it "saves willpower".
- Benefit: Cannot be quantified. The original experiment claimed that making choices and exercising self-control draw on one shared limited resource (ego depletion). Two large replications have since been run, both preregistered and both carried out by several labs following the original procedure, and neither reproduced the result. One involved 23 labs and 2141 people, with effect size d = 0.04, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.15] (credible range). The other involved 36 labs and 3531 people, d = 0.06. The difference is too small to count.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Baumeister, Bratslavsky, Muraven & Tice (1998). Ego depletion: Is the active self a limited resource? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1252> ; Hagger et al. (2016). A Multilab Preregistered Replication of the Ego-Depletion Effect. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873> ; Vohs et al. (2021). A Multisite Preregistered Paradigmatic Test of the Ego-Depletion Effect. - Notes:Disputed. Popular books treat "decision fatigue" as settled, but most later replications of the original experiment did not produce the same result. It is still worth doing, because it costs almost nothing and the time it saves is real. Do not expect it to improve your judgement in the afternoon.
### 18. When you are angry, walk away first and treat the other person as weather, not as an enemy
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to hold back your answer on the spot, turn and walk away, and decide afterwards whether to deal with it. That takes a little practice.
- In plain language: Explaining what just happened from a different angle, or taking the other person's point of view, both bring the emotion down for real, and the second works better. Suppressing it without showing anything does not work. People who punish the offender also ruminate on it for longer afterwards; people who do not retaliate move on faster. People overestimate in advance how good revenge will feel.
- Benefit: Pooling 306 experimental comparisons (a meta-analysis): reinterpreting what happened (cognitive reappraisal) has an effect size on emotion of d = 0.36. Taking the other person's point of view gives d = 0.45. Diverting attention gives d = 0.27. Suppressing the feeling does not work (d = -0.04). Three further experiments show that people who punish the offender ruminate on the person for longer afterwards, while people who do not punish move on faster. People overestimate in advance how good revenge will feel.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Webb TL, Miles E, Sheeran P (2012). Dealing with feeling: A meta-analysis of the effectiveness of strategies derived from the process model of emotion regulation. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027600> ; Carlsmith KM, Wilson TD, Gilbert DT (2008). The paradoxical consequences of revenge. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0012165>
- Notes: The part pooling several studies measured emotional intensity, not energy, and the effect is small to moderate. The revenge part rests on a single set of experiments, so it is graded B. The empty boat is a parable from the "Mountain Tree" chapter of the Zhuangzi: you are not angry when an empty boat drifts into you, and you are angry when there is a person aboard, yet the collision is the same. That is an analogy, not evidence. If there is real loss, take the legal route in section 8 and 9, which costs far less than losing your temper on the spot.
### 19. When you are low, do the highest-value things first: move, get daylight, sleep on time, talk to someone, call 12356
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money, or very little. Brisk walks, jogging or strength training several times a week. Go outside into the daylight during the day. Fix your daily routine. Make a phone call when you cannot get out of it.
- In plain language: For depression, brisk walking and jogging work best, and yoga and strength training come next; the more intense the exercise, the larger the effect. Light therapy works strongly on seasonal depression, and also works on depression that is not seasonal. Self-help cognitive behavioural courses online do work, and they work better with someone guiding you when the symptoms are heavier. If you cannot get out of it, call 12356 first.
- Benefit: Pooling 218 randomised controlled trials and 14170 people (a network meta-analysis). The comparison group was people doing other activities. Against them, walking or jogging has an effect size on depression of g = -0.62 (95% confidence interval -0.80 to -0.45). Yoga gives -0.55 and strength training -0.49. The more intense the exercise, the larger the effect. Pooling the light therapy studies: effect size 0.84 for seasonal depression (0.60 to 1.08), and 0.53 for non-seasonal depression (0.18 to 0.89). A further analysis pooled the individual patient data from the studies. It looked at internet cognitive behavioural therapy (online self-help courses). Both guided and unguided beat the comparison group. For moderate to severe cases (PHQ-9 above 9), guided did better. 12356 is the national mental health assistance hotline, open at least 18 hours a day.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Noetel M et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847> ; Golden RN et al. (2005). The efficacy of light therapy in the treatment of mood disorders: a review and meta-analysis of the evidence. American Journal of Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.4.656> ; Karyotaki E et al. (2021). Internet-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Depression: A Systematic Review and Individual Patient Data Network Meta-analysis. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364> ; National Health Commission (国家卫生健康委) (2024). Notice on the use of the "12356" national unified mental health assistance hotline number (国卫医政函〔2024〕259 号). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202412/content_6994470.htm>
- Notes: All of these are for mild to moderate depression. For moderate to severe depression, or if you have suicidal thoughts, see a doctor, and call 12356 first (see item 25 in section 1). The exercise trials mostly had few participants, and they cannot blind people to whether they are exercising, the way a drug trial can. The light therapy research only goes as far as 2003, and the samples were small too. "Sleep on time" and "talk to someone" are the author's own experience, with no separate reference. Do not treat "move" as a demand on people with depression; ten minutes of walking counts.
### 20. Treat police, doctors and clerks as people working to rules, not as characters: what moves a case forward is paperwork and deadlines, not emotion
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. What changes is your expectations and the order of your actions. The first time, you have to resist the urge to argue the case until the other side agrees.
- In plain language: Police, doctors and clerks all work to the rules of their post, and they answer to paperwork, deadlines and supervision. Whether the person is pleasant has almost nothing to do with whether your case gets done, or how many days it takes. So ask for a receipt, note the deadline, and appeal through the proper channel, instead of trying to argue until the other side agrees.
- Benefit: Change the way you handle dealings with institutions: from "beg, get angry, and keep repeating your grievance" to "take the paperwork, note the deadline, appeal through the proper channel". What you save is the time spent on repeated conversations and repeated recollection. This is a one-off adjustment of how you think, not a daily saving of time. What you can actually check are the deadlines themselves, and they are all written into the documents. For public security cases, the time limit for handling runs from the day the case is accepted and is no more than 30 days; where the case is major or complicated, it may be extended by 30 days with the approval of the public security organ at the next level up. Where a case with a complainant is decided not to be opened, the notice of refusal must be served within 3 days. The complainant may apply for reconsideration within 7 days, and the public security organ must decide within 30 days. If still not satisfied, a further 7 days may be used to apply for review by the organ at the next level up.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Ministry of Public Security (公安部) (2020). Provisions on the Procedures for Handling Criminal Cases by Public Security Organs (公安机关办理刑事案件程序规定)（公安部令第 159 号修正后，第一百七十一、一百七十八、一百七十九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2020/content_5549884.htm> ; Ministry of Public Security (公安部) (2013/2019 修正). Provisions on the Procedures for Handling Administrative Cases by Public Security Organs (公安机关办理行政案件程序规定)（公安部令第 125 号、第 149 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2321117.htm>
- Notes: The reason for grading C: the judgement that "many people treat the person behind the counter as a character rather than as someone at work" has no literature to cite. Only the rules half can be cited: the deadlines, the paperwork and the routes of appeal are all in writing. Every figure in this entry comes from the two Ministry of Public Security regulations cited in item 39 in section 8 (what happens after you call the police). Following the procedure will not fix an unpleasant manner, and it does not guarantee a good outcome; what it changes is your expectations and your first move. Bank clerks and government service windows are the same: their operations leave a trail, they need a second pair of eyes, and errors are held against them. So what you should do is keep the slip, ask for the receipt, complain through the proper channel, and not guess on the basis of "will he cheat me". Where the lawful order of priority is written down in the open, follow it. Emergency triage is by severity of illness, not by order of arrival (item 8 in section 24, emergency pre-triage). To thank a doctor who saved your life, use a letter of thanks and the satisfaction survey, see item 12 in section 24. If you want to "give them something so they take more care", to someone handling or enforcing a case that is bribery, and the law makes it an aggravated offence, see item 40 in section 8. If you meet obvious differential treatment, complain about it, and do not treat it as an unwritten rule to copy.

### 21. Do not treat "how other people are doing" as daily reading: limit or switch off the apps that show people your own age
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Setting a time limit on an app, or closing the account, takes a few minutes. The hard part is not opening it afterwards, which means fighting habit over the long run.
- In plain language: People who habitually compare themselves online with people who are doing better tend to be in worse mood and to have lower self-esteem. People who switched Facebook off for four weeks showed a small rise in well-being, and also a drop in what they knew about current affairs. People who limited three social platforms to 10 minutes a day each had lower loneliness and depression after three weeks.
- Benefit: Pooling several studies (a meta-analysis): 54 independent samples, 94 effect sizes, N = 36,583, using a three-level random-effects model. The average correlation between online upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment is r = 0.330 (95% CI 0.289–0.370, credible range). Broken out by outcome: social-evaluative negative emotions such as fear of being looked down on 0.438, anxiety 0.382, depression 0.306, low well-being 0.268, low self-esteem 0.263. Age, cultural background and the year the data were collected do not change the strength of the association. Studies with long-term follow-up do not give stronger results than cross-sectional surveys. A separate randomised controlled trial enrolled 2,743 US Facebook users. 61% of them were willing to accept a switch-off for less than 102 dollars. They were randomly assigned to a four-week switch-off group or a control group. Switching off freed the average participant 60 minutes a day. The subjective well-being index rose by 0.09 standard deviations. Broken out: life satisfaction 0.12, anxiety 0.10, depression 0.09, happiness 0.08. These still hold after the element of chance from multiple comparisons is removed. The author gives a reference point: psychological interventions that make people more positive. Such interventions raise subjective well-being by about +0.34 standard deviations and lower depression by about -0.23 standard deviations. So this effect is roughly 25% to 40% of that of an ordinary psychological intervention. The cost over the same period: the current-affairs knowledge index fell by 0.19 standard deviations, and the degree of polarisation on political positions fell by 0.16 standard deviations. After the experiment ended, Facebook use in the switch-off group was still 12 minutes a day below the control group (0.31 standard deviations, a drop of 23%). A further randomised controlled trial enrolled 143 undergraduates, randomly split into two groups. One group limited Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes a day each, and the other carried on as before. After three weeks, loneliness and depression fell clearly in the limited group. Anxiety and fear of missing out fell from their starting levels in both groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lei Y, Hu S, Sun Y, Zheng L (2026). "Looking up" linked to feeling down: a meta-analysis of online upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment. Frontiers in Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1825169> ; Allcott H, Braghieri L, Eichmeyer S, Gentzkow M (2020). The Welfare Effects of Social Media. American Economic Review, 110(3), 629–676. <https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190658> ; Allcott H, Braghieri L, Eichmeyer S, Gentzkow M (2019). The Welfare Effects of Social Media. NBER Working Paper No. 25514 (the specific figures quoted in this entry were checked word for word against this version; the published abstract contains no figures). <https://www.nber.org/papers/w25514> ; Hunt MG, Marx R, Lipson C, Young J (2018). No More FOMO: Limiting Social Media Decreases Loneliness and Depression. Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology, 37(10), 751–768. <https://doi.org/10.1521/jscp.2018.37.10.751>
- Notes: The "correlation" half only shows that two things occur together, and cannot be read as one causing the other; the authors themselves say more long-term follow-up is needed. The randomly assigned half can speak about causation, but the participants were US Facebook users just before the 2018 midterm elections, and applying that to Chinese platforms is a forced fit. The Hunt study has only 143 undergraduates, and there is only that one study. The effect sizes are all small, so do not expect switching off an app to solve a mood problem. What you need to block is the "seeing how other people are doing" kind of use. Contacting family and friends, looking things up and looking for work are not part of that. If you cannot hold the line, start by turning off notifications, turning off "people you may know" and turning off recommendations on the home page. For the total screen time, see items 15 and 16 in section 4 (a hard limit on short videos, and no doomscrolling). For what to do first when you are low, see item 19 in this section (move, get daylight, sleep on time). For using purchases to rebuild a sense of identity, see item 23 in section 6. For spending more money to "move up a notch", see item 24 in section 6.
### 22. When you think everybody saw you embarrass yourself, halve the estimate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. You only ask yourself one extra question when you are nervous: did they really see it?
- In plain language: Walk into a room of people wearing an embarrassing T-shirt, and the wearer thinks nearly half of them can recognise who is printed on it. In fact fewer than a quarter can. In a T-shirt the wearer considers decent, the estimated number is six times the real one. People also tend to underestimate how much the other person likes them after a conversation. So when you think everybody remembers you embarrassing yourself, halving it is still too high.
- Benefit: Experiment one: participants wore a T-shirt printed with a picture of Barry Manilow and walked into a room with other participants. The wearer estimated on average that 46% of the bystanders could correctly name the person on the shirt. The bystanders' actual accuracy was 23%, so the estimate was exactly twice the real figure. Taking estimate minus actual for each session, the average overestimate was 23 percentage points (95% CI 9–38, credible range). Dropping the two sessions that had only two and three bystanders, it was 27 percentage points (11–43). Experiment two: participants wore a T-shirt of their own choosing that they considered flattering. The average estimate was 48%, the bystanders' actual accuracy 8%, six times over. A separate series of studies covers three settings: paired conversations between strangers, first-year university roommates, and adult workshops. The result is that people systematically underestimate how much the other person likes them, and also underestimate how much the other person enjoyed the conversation. The authors call this the "liking gap". It is present in conversations of different lengths, and it persists for months between first-year roommates.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Gilovich T, Medvec VH, Savitsky K (2000). The spotlight effect in social judgment: An egocentric bias in estimates of the salience of one's own actions and appearance. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 78(2), 211–222. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211> ; Boothby EJ, Cooney G, Sandstrom GM, Clark MS (2018). The Liking Gap in Conversations: Do People Like Us More Than We Think? Psychological Science, 29(11), 1742–1756. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618783714>
- Notes: The reason for grading B: these are small laboratory and campus studies, in settings of wearing clothes and talking to strangers. Applied to "colleagues are talking about you" or "you said the wrong thing at the meeting", there is no direct evidence. These numbers show the direction of the overestimate and the rough multiple; they cannot be used as a coefficient. Other people's opinions are not all unimportant either. Where you really are being assessed, such as an interview, a viva, an appraisal or a medical report, the assessment is real, and you should prepare. Discount only that everyday feeling of "everybody is watching me"; what you are dealing with there is your own estimation bias.
### 23. Treat "others expect me to be perfect" as a symptom, not as a fact
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is treating such a thought as something that needs handling, rather than as an accurate measurement of what others actually require.
- In plain language: People who always feel that "the people around me expect me to be perfect and will reject me if I fall short" are more likely to have suicidal thoughts. The link between "I expect myself to be perfect" and suicidal thoughts is much weaker, about a third of the first. After subtracting the thoughts people already had, the first feeling is still related to a later increase in thoughts, though the size is small.
- Benefit: Pooling several studies (a meta-analysis): 45 studies, N = 11,747 with undergraduate students, medical students, community adults and psychiatric patients as participants, using a random-effects model. Below are the weighted mean correlations with suicidal ideation. Perfectionism concerns r = .28 (95% CI .24–.32, credible range; 42 studies, 7,936 people). Perfectionism concerns is a composite that includes socially prescribed perfectionism, worry about making mistakes, doubts about one's own actions, and the gap between reality and one's standards. Socially prescribed perfectionism on its own also gives r = .28 (.25–.32, 30 studies, 3,640 people). Perfectionism striving r = .10 (.07–.13, 31 studies, 4,588 people); it is a composite of self-oriented perfectionism and personal standards. Parental criticism r = .20 (.11–.29). Now the relationship with suicide attempts. Perfectionism concerns r = .12 (.07–.17, 15 studies, 5,275 people). Socially prescribed perfectionism r = .19 (.08–.29, 8 studies, 689 people). For the part that follows people over time: after subtracting the suicidal ideation present at the start, socially prescribed perfectionism still moved in the same direction as suicidal ideation at the later follow-up, with a small size. Neither self-oriented nor other-oriented perfectionism shows any association.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Smith MM, Sherry SB, Chen S, Saklofske DH, Mushquash C, Flett GL, Hewitt PL (2018). The perniciousness of perfectionism: A meta-analytic review of the perfectionism-suicide relationship. Journal of Personality, 86(3), 522–542. <https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12333>
- Notes: The reason for grading B: most of the studies included are one-off questionnaires, so the order of events is not visible. The correlation is moderate (r of about .28) and explains only a few percent to a tenth of the difference. What it measured is suicidal ideation and suicide attempts, not death itself, so the benefit is graded small on the grounds of "surrogate endpoint only". The authors themselves say the set of perfectionism concerns and suicide attempts looks like it has publication bias. Publication bias means studies with unflattering results do not get published. Putting the estimated missing studies back in does not change the direction of the conclusion. High standards in themselves are not the problem, and you need not lower them for that reason. The difference lies in where the standard comes from, and in whether failing to meet it makes you feel "I am finished as a person". Like the pessimistic expectations in item 15 (treat "things will definitely get worse" thoughts as a symptom), this is something that can be treated as a symptom. If you have suicidal thoughts, call 12356 first and move lethal means out of reach, see item 25 in section 1. For what you can do when someone near you shows such thoughts, see item 15 in section 8. For depression screening in children, see item 8 in section 30.
### 24. When you are angry, do not let it out by smashing things, hitting a punch bag or running a lap; slow the body down first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. In the heat of the moment, spend a few minutes on something that slows your heartbeat: breathe slowly, sit down, stay still. The hard part is that what you most want to do then is exactly to let it out.
- In plain language: Hitting a punch bag, throwing things down, going for a run — these ways of letting it out that make the body more excited do not calm anger overall. Slow breathing, mindfulness and meditation, which calm the body down, clearly reduce anger and the urge to hit someone.
- Benefit: Pooling 154 studies (a meta-analysis), 184 samples in all and 10,189 people. Methods that calm the body down, such as deep breathing, mindfulness and meditation, lower anger and aggression. Effect size g = -0.63, 95% CI -0.82 to -0.43 (credible range). The result is stable across people of different sexes, races, ages and cultures. Methods that make the body more excited, such as hitting a punch bag, jogging and cycling, have no effect overall. Their effect size is g = -0.02, 95% CI -0.13 to 0.09. The authors' conclusion is that letting anger out, and going for a run, are not effective ways to calm down.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Kjærvik SL, Bushman BJ (2024). A meta-analytic review of anger management activities that increase or decrease arousal: What fuels or douses rage? Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102414>
- Notes: What it measured is anger and aggression, not energy. The measure is recorded as time because that is the author's own classification: one less row to clear up after one less argument. For something you can use on the spot, see item 9 in section 22 (cyclic sighing), and item 18 in this section (walk away when you are angry). Running is not useless. Over the long run it works for low mood: see item 7 in section 22 (walk or run when you feel down). It is just not suitable for calming down on the spot. Do not drive, do not send messages and do not make decisions while angry. If somebody is hitting you, leave first and call the police. What needs handling then is not your emotions: see item 43 in section 8 (domestic violence).

### 25. When something is weighing on you, write down your thoughts and feelings over several days, but do not expect it to cure anything
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. The usual research design is to write 3 to 4 times, for 15 to 20 minutes each time. Write about one thing that upset you, and write your deepest thoughts and feelings about it.
- In plain language: Writing a bad thing down brings a benefit on average, and it is a small one. The pooled conclusions are inconsistent, and two of the pooled analyses found no effect. It costs almost nothing, so it is worth trying. If the emotional problem is serious, do not use it instead of seeing a doctor.
- Benefit: One analysis pooled 146 randomised studies (a meta-analysis). The participants wrote down or spoke about something that mattered to them. The average effect is positive and significant, but very small, with effect size r = .075. Another pooled analysis took in randomised controlled trials only, and pooling 30 trials found no significant effect on either physical health or mental health. Pooling 16 randomised trials in cancer patients and survivors found nothing significant on the psychological, physical or quality-of-life measures. On the psychological measure, g = 0.04, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.14 (credible range).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Frattaroli J (2006). Experimental disclosure and its moderators: a meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.823> ; Mogk C, Otte S, Reinhold-Hurley B, Kröner-Herwig B (2006). Health effects of expressive writing on stressful or traumatic experiences - a meta-analysis. Psycho-Social Medicine. <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2736499/> ; Zachariae R, O'Toole MS (2015). The effect of expressive writing intervention on psychological and physical health outcomes in cancer patients--a systematic review and meta-analysis. Psycho-Oncology. <https://doi.org/10.1002/pon.3802>
- Notes:Disputed. The supporting side is the pool of 146 studies, with a positive but very small effect. The opposing side is two pooled analyses that took in randomised controlled trials only, and neither found a significant effect. The number of sessions and the length of each one in the Cost field come from the earliest experimental design, and are stated in the abstract of the Mogk review. It costs almost no money and little time, and one person can do it alone. When you do not want to talk to someone, or there is nobody around to talk to, you can use it. What it measures is not energy; recording the measure as time is the author's own classification. If writing makes you feel worse as you go, stop and call 12356 instead, see item 25 in section 1 (12356).
