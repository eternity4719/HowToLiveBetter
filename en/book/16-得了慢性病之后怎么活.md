# 16. Living with a chronic illness

This section measures all-cause mortality and money. After a chronic illness is diagnosed you usually have decades left to live, and this section works out how to manage them.

### 1. Take the medicine for the full course as the doctor ordered, and do not stop because you feel better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and you save the money you would later spend on treating complications. Taking the tablets takes a few minutes a day. The hard part is taking them every day without a gap.
- In plain language: Among people who take the medicine for the full course as the doctor said, the chance of dying in the same period is about half that of people who take it on and off. Read that figure with care. People who take a placebo for the full course also have a lower mortality, which means part of the benefit comes from 「the people who keep taking their tablets are simply more careful about their health」. The harm of stopping is certain, and especially so for blood pressure medicine, anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: Pooling 21 studies and 46847 people (meta-analysis): compared with people who take their medicine on and off, those who take it for the full course as ordered are about 44% less likely to die in the same period. The underlying figure is an odds ratio of 0.56, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.63 (credible range). Looking only at treatments that do work, it is about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49 to 0.62)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Notes:Disputed. This is also the figure in the book that most needs reading with a discount. In the same pooled analysis, people who took a placebo (a dummy drug) for the full course also had a lower mortality (0.56, 0.43 to 0.74). That means a large part of the benefit comes from 「the people who keep taking their tablets are simply more careful about their health」. The authors call this confounder the healthy adherer effect. The harm of stopping the drug itself is nonetheless clear, and especially so for blood pressure medicine, anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs
### 2. Get the outpatient chronic and special disease status first and the cross-region filing second, and hypertension, diabetes, chemotherapy and radiotherapy, dialysis and anti-rejection treatment settle directly across regions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before you travel, do the cross-region medical filing once in the medical insurance app, which you can do on your phone.
- In plain language: For hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemotherapy and radiotherapy for a malignant tumour, dialysis for uraemia and anti-rejection treatment after an organ transplant, the outpatient costs can be settled directly against your medical insurance in another place. You do not pay out of pocket first and then claim in your home area. But you have to get the outpatient chronic and special disease status in your home area first, and the cross-region filing second. Both are needed for it to work.
- Benefit: A document from the National Healthcare Security Administration says this has already been achieved. The words are: 「基本实现高血压、糖尿病、恶性肿瘤门诊放化疗、尿毒症透析、器官移植术后抗排异治疗等 5 种门诊慢特病费用跨省直接结算县域可及」 — outpatient chronic and special disease costs in 5 kinds of condition, namely hypertension, diabetes, outpatient chemotherapy and radiotherapy for a malignant tumour, dialysis for uraemia and post-operative anti-rejection treatment after an organ transplant, can now be settled directly across provinces and reached at county level. 「县域可及」, reached at county level, means you can get it done in a county. The document also adds 5 more conditions to these 5
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Office of the National Healthcare Security Administration and General Office of the Ministry of Finance (国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅) (2024). Notice on Safely and in Order Expanding the Range of Outpatient Chronic and Special Diseases Settled Directly across Provinces, 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Notes: The condition is that you do both: get the outpatient chronic and special disease status in your home area first, then make the cross-region filing. How far the 5 added conditions have been opened up varies from place to place, so follow what your local medical insurance department publishes
### 3. Do your follow-up tests at the interval the doctor gives, and write the figures from each one in the same notebook
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Each test costs from tens to several hundred yuan. A trip to the hospital plus waiting for the results takes some of your time. The hard part is having to go again after a while, and it is easy to keep putting it off.
- In plain language: A chronic illness is judged by the change over a period, not by one reading. Write the date, the figures, the medicine you were taking and the dose in the same notebook. When you change hospital, take it out, and you do not have to have the tests done again or pay for them again.
- Benefit: A chronic illness is judged by how the figures change over a period, and one reading is not enough. Keeping everything in one place has a second benefit: when you change hospital you do not repeat the tests and you do not pay for them twice
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: the author's own experience, no direct literature
- Notes: Record at least four things each time: the date, the value, the medicine you were taking, and the dose. Photographing the lab report on your phone is fine, but keep all of it in one place rather than scattered
### 4. Do not stop proper treatment in order to try a folk remedy or a health supplement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and you save the money you would have spent on the supplement. It takes no time. The hard part is standing firm when other people keep telling you to try it.
- In plain language: The harm of stopping the drug is certain; the benefit of a folk remedy or a supplement is uncertain. That is trading a certain loss for an uncertain gain. If you want to try a supplement, tell your treating doctor first. Some supplements change the level of anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs in your blood.
- Benefit: The harm of stopping the drug is certain; the benefit of a folk remedy is uncertain. This book lists a batch of the common categories that do not work in section 6
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: the author's own experience, no direct literature ; for the related evidence see section 6
- Notes: If you want to try a supplement, tell your treating doctor first. Some supplements interact with prescription drugs, for example by changing the level of anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs in your blood
### 5. A stable chronic illness: a community health centre can prescribe up to 12 weeks of medicine at once
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask at the community health centre whether they can issue a long-term prescription. That is one question.
- In plain language: If your illness is stable and you have not changed the medicine you take, the community health centre can give you 12 weeks of it in one go. That saves the half-day of travelling to a big hospital every month to queue and register. In the same insurance region, the reimbursement rate for treatment at a primary-level hospital is usually about ten percentage points higher than at a hospital one level up.
- Benefit: You no longer travel to a big hospital every month to queue for a prescription. In the same insurance region (also called the pooled region), the reimbursement rate for treatment at a primary-level hospital is in principle about 10 percentage points higher than at a hospital one level up
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (国务院办公厅) (2026). Several Measures on Accelerating the Building of a Tiered Diagnosis and Treatment System, 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm> : 「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」; the same document: 「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」, and it requires higher-level hospitals to run outpatient clinics for common chronic conditions such as hypertension, diabetes and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease at the primary level; the same document: 「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」, 「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Notes: Whether you 「meet the conditions」 is decided by the doctor; in general it means the diagnosis is already clear and the treatment plan is stable. The medicine is the same medicine. What you save is the half-day of the monthly trip and the registration fee. If the primary level does not stock the kind you need, ask whether they keep a register of missing drugs and can deliver them. The same document also requires tertiary hospitals to cut back step by step on ordinary outpatient clinics for chronic illnesses of this kind, and to move the ordinary clinics of the same specialists into the community. If you keep treating the big hospital as your place to get medicine, getting an appointment there will only get harder.

### 6. Before you sign up with a family doctor in the community, ask which items are covered by medical insurance and which you pay for yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. One extra question before you sign, and it takes a minute.
- In plain language: The basic service package you sign for goes through medical insurance as the rules require. You pay the whole cost of a personalised service package yourself. When somebody sells you an 「upgrade package」, first find out whether it is the basic package or a personalised one, whether it has been filed, and what it costs for a year.
- Benefit: The basic service package is paid by medical insurance as the rules require. A personalised service package is paid for entirely by the individual, and such a package also has to be filed with the county health commission
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council General Office (国务院办公厅) (2026). Several Measures on Accelerating the Building of a Tiered Diagnosis and Treatment System, 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm> : 「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Notes: The value of signing up is that somebody follows your illness over the long term, not how many items are bundled into the package. When somebody sells you an 「upgrade package」, ask three questions first: is this the basic package or a personalised one, has it been filed, and what does it cost for a year.
### 7. Once diabetes is diagnosed, have the back of your eyes examined once, then at the interval the doctor gives; have your feet checked once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One dilated examination of the back of the eye costs from tens to several hundred yuan. The foot check is normally part of the outpatient visit and costs nothing extra. The hard part is remembering to go when your eyes and feet give you no trouble at all.
- In plain language: Diabetes damage to the back of the eye and to the feet gives almost no warning before the damage is bad enough to make you blind or to rot. Find it early and it can still be treated. For type 2 diabetes, have a dilated examination of the back of the eye at the time of diagnosis; for type 1, start five years after the onset. If one or two examinations have found nothing and your blood sugar is on target, you can stretch it to once every one or two years. Have your feet checked once a year.
- Benefit: Diabetes damage to the back of the eye is called retinopathy, and damage to the feet is called diabetic foot. Both give almost no warning before something goes wrong. Find it early and it can still be treated. Once you cannot see, or the foot has started to rot, it is too late
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: American Diabetes Association (美国糖尿病学会) (2026). Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Chapter 12, Retinopathy, Neuropathy and Foot Care, 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care: 「People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis.」「Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes.」「If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered.」「Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations.」<https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Notes: For type 2 diabetes you have the examination at the time of diagnosis, and for type 1 you start five years after the onset. The difference is that type 2 has often been quietly high for years. If one or two examinations have found nothing and your blood sugar is on target, you can stretch it to once every one to two years. If the feeling in your foot has become dull, look at the sole once a day yourself, using a mirror if that is easier.
### 8. If you have had a kidney stone, drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day and keep salt under 6 grams
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Turn drinking water throughout the day into a habit. On days when you sweat more you have to drink more still, and the hard part is keeping count every day.
- In plain language: Among people who form a calcium stone for the first time, about a quarter have another one within five years if they do nothing. Among those who drink enough water every day, a little over one in ten has a recurrence within five years, which is more than half fewer. The British guideline gives 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day and no more than 6 grams of salt.
- Benefit: One controlled trial randomised into two groups ran for 5 years, with 199 patients who had a first calcium stone and no cause found for it, also called idiopathic calcium stone. One group drank at least 2 litres of water a day and the other had no intervention. Recurrences over the 5 years were 12/99 (12.1%) in the water group and 27/100 (27.0%) in the no-intervention group, P=0.008. The smaller the P value, the less the difference between the two groups can be chance. The average interval to recurrence was 38.7±13.2 months against 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. The 24-hour urine volume measured at the start was clearly lower in the stone patients than in those without stones: for men 1,057±238 mL against 1,401±562 mL (P<0.0001), and for women 990±230 mL against 1,239±440 mL (P<0.001). Recommendation 1.8.1 of NICE guideline NG118 advises adults to drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water a day, and children and adolescents 1 to 2 litres according to age. Add a squeeze of fresh lemon to the water, do not drink fizzy drinks, and keep salt under 6 grams a day for adults
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3> ; National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.8.1 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: Recurrence is down by about 55% and it costs nothing, so by the ordering rules of this book it is the best value for money in this section. What you watch is the 24-hour urine volume, with a target of 2 to 2.5 litres or more, not how much you drink in. People who sweat more and people who work in high temperatures have to drink more. That trial covered patients having a first attack of idiopathic calcium stone. Stone composition is not one thing: uric acid stones, infection stones and cystine stones each have their own answer. Anyone who has had a recurrence is worth having a stone composition analysis and a 24-hour urine test, and only then deciding whether to add a drug. Do not cut calcium out in order to prevent stones: see item 21 in section 6. If the pain in your back and belly is severe enough to be colicky, go to hospital; drinking more water does not flush it out. Blood in the urine without pain is a separate thing to investigate: see item 27 in section 1

### 9. Once gout is diagnosed, take urate-lowering medicine for the long term, push the blood urate below 360 µmol/L and keep it there
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Allopurinol costs from a few yuan to a few tens of yuan a month. At the start you test the blood urate every few weeks to adjust the dose, and once it is stable every few months. For the first 3 to 6 months you also take a medicine that prevents attacks. The hard part is that you have to keep taking it when nothing hurts.
- In plain language: Gout needs long-term urate-lowering medicine that brings the urate below 360 µmol/L; you cannot just take painkillers when it hurts. Among patients whose dose a nurse helps adjust against the lab results, 95% reached the target after two years, against only 30% of those treated as usual. You cannot stop the medicine once you have reached the target either. Diet does not bring the level down to that line, and giving up alcohol lowers it by only 1.6 mg/dL.
- Benefit: A controlled trial with 517 adults who had an attack in the past 12 months, randomised into two groups. One group was led by a nurse, who explained what the illness was and then adjusted the urate against the target figures. The other group saw a general practitioner as usual. At 2 years the proportion with blood urate down to <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL) was 95% against 30%. The chance of reaching the target was 3.18 times that of the usual-care group (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18, that interval is the credible range for the figure), P<0.0001. The secondary outcomes in year 2 — attack frequency, gouty tophi and quality of life — were also better than in the usual-care group. Each extra year of life gained, converted for quality of life (quality-adjusted life years), cost £5,066. The same trial also ran a follow-up study: 438 people were sent a questionnaire and 82% returned it. Both groups reported their own number of attacks over the past 12 months. The median in the group that reached the target was 0 attacks (the middle half fell between 0–0). In the usual-care group it was 1 attack (0–3), P<0.001. The range in brackets is called the interquartile range. The proportion still taking urate-lowering medicine was 1.19 times that of the usual-care group in the group that reached the target (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The 2020 American College of Rheumatology gout guideline strongly recommends starting urate-lowering treatment in three kinds of people: those with gouty tophi under the skin, those whose imaging shows gout eating defects into the bone, and those with ≥2 attacks a year. The practice is to keep monitoring the blood urate and adjust the dose against the results until the target is met, which is <6 mg/dL. Allopurinol is the first choice, and it is also the first choice for people with stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, with a starting dose of ≤100 mg/day. Preventive anti-inflammatory medicine is given at the same time when treatment starts, for at least 3 to 6 months. The same guideline also cites a set of case observations. These patients stopped the medicine after good long-term control, and their blood urate still stayed below <7 mg/dL after stopping. Among them, only 13% (27/211) had no attack at all during the 5-year follow-up. On diet: people who limit or give up alcohol have blood urate 1.6 mg/dL lower than people who do not, and one serving of beer raises blood urate by 0.16 mg/dL. Healthy eating, the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet make a smaller difference. There is disagreement over which urate-lowering drug to use first. The American College of Rheumatology guideline strongly recommends allopurinol as the first choice, on the grounds that it works when the dose is right, is well tolerated, safe and cheap. It also recommends conditionally: people on febuxostat who have a history of cardiovascular disease, or who have just had a cardiovascular event, should switch to something else where there is something else to switch to. That recommendation matches the black-box warning the FDA put on febuxostat. One of the bases is the CARES trial, in which 6190 gout patients with coexisting cardiovascular disease were put on febuxostat or allopurinol at random. In the febuxostat group the risk of all-cause death was 1.22 times that of the allopurinol group (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher). Cardiovascular death was 1.34 times (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). The European FAST trial reached a different conclusion. It enrolled 6128 patients aged over 60 with cardiovascular risk factors, and febuxostat did not produce more major cardiovascular events than allopurinol (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03). Deaths were 7.2% in the febuxostat group and 8.6% in the allopurinol group. For people taking a drug that makes the kidneys excrete urate, the American College of Rheumatology advises against alkalinising the urine as a conditional recommendation, because there is no evidence that it works and the certainty of the evidence is very low. It also requires telling these people to drink more water. People with known kidney stones, or stage 3 or higher chronic kidney disease, should not use this kind of drug
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5> ; Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333> ; FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180> ; White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895> ; Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0> ; Chinese Medical Association, Branch of Endocrinology (中华医学会内分泌学分会) (2020). 中国高尿酸血症与痛风诊疗指南(2019). 中华内分泌代谢杂志, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Notes:Disputed. Countries' guidelines disagree about which drug to lower urate with. The US guideline makes allopurinol the first choice and says people with cardiovascular disease should get off febuxostat where they can, but the European FAST trial did not find febuxostat more dangerous. Benzbromarone is also sold in China; it works by making the kidneys excrete urate. The Chinese Medical Association guidelines (the 2019 edition and the 2024 update) give separate recommendations on choosing the drug and on alkalinising the urine. On whether to add sodium bicarbonate to alkalinise the urine when taking benzbromarone, the US guideline says there is no evidence; follow your doctor's advice, and do not skip the extra water. **Before a Han Chinese person takes allopurinol, it is worth testing for HLA-B\*5801**. It is an inherited gene type that governs the immune response, and one test gives a result that never changes. Among Han Chinese, Korean and Thai people, 7.4% carry this gene; among white and Hispanic people it is only 0.7%. Asian and Black people who take allopurinol run three times the risk of white people of developing allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome, and in severe cases that disease strips the skin off over large areas and can kill. The 2020 American College of Rheumatology gout guideline suggests considering a test first for these two groups. **Starting urate-lowering treatment actually makes an attack more likely at first**, so the guideline explicitly requires a preventive anti-inflammatory medicine at the same time for the first 3 to 6 months. 「It got more painful once I started the urate-lowering drug, so I stopped」 is the commonest way this goes wrong. When the pain comes, what you do is add an anti-inflammatory drug as the doctor ordered, not stop the urate-lowering drug. Guides write the target value differently: <6 mg/dL is about 360 µmol/L, and Chinese lab reports mostly use µmol/L. A high urate found on a check-up with no attack ever is a different matter: see item 19 in section 6. For sugary drinks and alcohol, see items 7 and 19 in section 2 (do not drink sugary drinks, drink little or no alcohol)
