# 26. Running a website or platform: licences, filings, servers

You want to build a website or an app. This section answers four questions: what kind of business it is in law, which licence you need, where the servers sit, and who is answerable for what users post and for the money taken in. The measures are personal freedom and money. The items come in this order: first the ones that mean prison, then the ones that need a licence before you start, then what the platform has to do every day, and last the experience behind choosing a server, which can give you a judgement but no figures. If you are a technical employee working for somebody else, the red lines are in section 11. How a company is registered and how the tax is filed are in section 12, and they are not repeated here. The table comparing the licences and the decision table for choosing a server are in [docs/做平台要办哪些证.md](../docs/做平台要办哪些证.md).

### 1. The platform should not take the buyer's money and pass it on to the seller; let the money go straight through a licensed payment institution
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes some time to connect the product of a licensed payment institution. A licensed payment institution is a company that has a payment licence from the central bank. With its split-settlement or guaranteed-transaction function, the buyer's money goes straight to the seller and never touches the platform's own account
- In plain language: If the buyer's money comes into your account first and you pass it on to the seller, you are doing payment business. That business needs the approval of the People's Bank of China, and it needs 100 million yuan of paid-in capital to be shown for real. Do it without the approval and you are shut down, the money you took is confiscated and you are fined on top of that, with the boss and the person handling it fined separately. In the serious cases there is a prison sentence as well. There is only one safe way: the money must not come into your account.
- Benefit: The payment business the regulations describe is 「根据收款人或者付款人提交的电子支付指令转移货币资金」 — transferring money according to the electronic payment instruction submitted by the payee or the payer. In plain terms, it means moving money for somebody else on somebody else's instruction. To do that business a company 「应当经中国人民银行批准，取得支付业务许可」 — must be approved by the People's Bank of China and hold a payment business licence. The registered capital is at least 100 million yuan and it must be paid in cash: that 100 million yuan has to go into the company's account in real money. 「未经依法批准，擅自设立非银行支付机构、从事或者变相从事支付业务的，由中国人民银行依法予以取缔，没收违法所得」 — anyone who, without approval as required by law, sets up a non-bank payment institution, or engages in or takes on payment business without it, is shut down by the People's Bank of China and has the illegal gains confiscated. Anyone who does this without the approval is shut down at once and the money they took is taken away. Where the illegal gains are 500,000 yuan or more, there is a further fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal gains. Where there are no illegal gains, or fewer than 500,000 yuan, the fine is 500,000 to 2 million yuan. The legal representative and the person directly responsible are fined 100,000 to 500,000 yuan each. The Criminal Law also counts 「非法从事资金支付结算业务」 — carrying out fund payment and settlement business without a licence — as operating without a licence. Where the circumstances are serious it carries 5 years or less, and in the especially serious cases 5 years or more. What counts as serious is drawn in the interpretation of the Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院) and the Supreme People's Procuratorate (最高人民检察院). The line is 5 million yuan or more in the value of the illegal business, or 100,000 yuan or more in illegal gains (nationwide, from May 2024)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2023). Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Non-Bank Payment Institutions, 非银行支付机构监督管理条例（国务院令第 768 号，第二、六、八、二十七、四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11086/202401/content_6924970.html> ; People's Bank of China (中国人民银行) (2024). Detailed Rules for the Implementation of the Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Non-Bank Payment Institutions, 非银行支付机构监督管理条例实施细则（中国人民银行令〔2024〕第 4 号，第六十八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11546/202408/content_6970979.html> ; National People's Congress (全国人大) (1997). Criminal Law, 刑法（第二百二十五条第三项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院) and Supreme People's Procuratorate (最高人民检察院) (2019). Interpretation on Several Issues Concerning the Application of Law in Handling Criminal Cases of Illegal Fund Payment and Settlement Services and Illegal Foreign Exchange Trading, 关于办理非法从事资金支付结算业务、非法买卖外汇刑事案件适用法律若干问题的解释（第一、三、四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201901/t20190131_407161.shtml>
- Notes: Designing the money so that it never comes into your account is the only safe way. The trade calls this kind of collection-and-payment-on-behalf 「secondary clearing」, but the word is not in any official document. The detailed rules use the wording 「变相从事支付业务」 — taking on payment business in a disguised way, that is, moving money for others on the user's instruction without the approval. The interpretation of the Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院) and the Supreme People's Procuratorate (最高人民检察院) lists only three situations: cashing out through fabricated transactions, cashing out through a company account, and cashing out through cheques. It does not name a platform collecting and paying on behalf of others, so one cannot say that a platform doing that 「certainly amounts to a crime」
### 2. A website that charges money needs a licence, matching transactions is a different licence, and a free site only needs a filing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The filing costs nothing. The Value-Added Telecommunications Business Licence (增值电信业务经营许可证) costs money, and it decides 「向用户收钱的网站能不能开」 — whether a website that charges its users is allowed to open. To get that licence you need a company first. A business operating only inside one province needs registered capital of at least 1 million yuan. A business operating across provinces needs at least 10 million yuan. The review takes 60 days, and the licence is valid for 5 years
- In plain language: A website that charges its users has to get the Value-Added Telecommunications Business Licence first. That licence is only issued to companies; an individual cannot get it. A website that does not charge only has to file, and the filing is free. Open a site and charge money without the licence and the income is confiscated and you are fined, and in the serious cases the site is closed. Whether your platform falls into the class that matches buyers with sellers is not decided for you in this item, and the reason is in the notes.
- Benefit: The regulations say 「国家对经营性互联网信息服务实行许可制度；对非经营性互联网信息服务实行备案制度。未取得许可或者未履行备案手续的，不得从事互联网信息服务」 — the state runs a licensing system for commercial internet information services and a filing system for non-commercial ones, and without the licence or without the filing you may not carry on internet information services at all. A site that charges has to get the licence first; a site that does not charge has to file first; with neither, you may not open. What counts as commercial? The regulations call it 「通过互联网向上网用户有偿提供信息或者网页制作等服务活动」 — providing information or web page production to internet users for money. In simple terms, it means charging the users. The class that matches transactions is online data processing and transaction processing, abbreviated in the trade as EDI. The regulations define it as 「利用各种与公用通信网或互联网相连的数据与交易/事务处理应用平台，通过公用通信网或互联网为用户提供在线数据处理和交易/事务处理的业务」. It also 「包括交易处理业务、电子数据交换业务和网络/电子设备数据处理业务」. In other words, a website that processes data online for users, or that matches and processes transactions between buyers and sellers. Operating without the licence, or operating beyond the scope of the licence, means the illegal gains are confiscated and a fine of 3 to 5 times the illegal gains is imposed. Operating beyond the scope means holding the licence and doing things that go beyond what it says. Where there are no illegal gains, or fewer than 50,000 yuan, the fine is 100,000 to 1 million yuan. Where the circumstances are serious, the website is ordered closed (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2000). Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services, 互联网信息服务管理办法（国务院令第 292 号，2024 年 12 月 6 日第二次修订，第三、四、七、十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> ; State Council (国务院) (2024). Decision on Amending and Repealing Certain Administrative Regulations, 关于修改和废止部分行政法规的决定（国务院令第 797 号，第十八项）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11786/202501/content_6997034.html> ; State Council (国务院) (2000). Regulations on Telecommunications, 电信条例（2016 年第二次修订，第七、九、十三、六十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2016/content_5139478.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部) (2017). Measures for the Administration of Licences for the Conduct of Telecommunications Business, 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第六、九、十一、十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm>
- Notes: Whether what you are doing is in the EDI class is for you to work out by matching the definition in the Catalogue of Telecommunications Business (《电信业务分类目录》). No official document says in so many words that an e-commerce platform with matching and fund handling must hold an EDI licence. The service guide of the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部) only says 「按照业务界定申请相应的电信业务经营许可」 — apply for the telecommunications business licence that matches how the business is defined. So whether you need one has to be judged from the definition yourself. One more thing: an individual cannot get this licence; the applicant has to be a company established in accordance with the law
### 3. Streaming needs the Internet Culture Business Licence, and the licence for audio-visual programmes is basically out of reach for a private company
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The Internet Culture Business Licence (网络文化经营许可证), called the web culture licence in short, decides 「能不能靠直播、游戏解说这类网络表演赚钱」 — whether you may earn money from online performances such as livestreaming or game commentary. It is approved by the provincial cultural administrative department, which must give a result within 20 days of accepting the application, and it is valid for 3 years, with the renewal filed 30 days before it expires. The Audio-Visual Programme Licence (视听节目许可) decides 「能不能在网上放节目」 — whether you may put programmes online, and it requires the applicant to be a wholly state-owned or a state-controlled entity
- In plain language: Livestreaming and game commentary need the Internet Culture Business Licence from the provincial cultural department. Online performance has to be written into the scope of business on the licence. A result comes within 20 days of the application, and the licence lasts 3 years. You have to keep the videos you have streamed for at least 60 days yourself. Open up without the licence and you are told to stop and warned, then fined up to 30,000 yuan, and if you do not stop you go on a blacklist.
- Benefit: A unit doing online performance has to get the licence first. The regulations say 「应当根据《互联网文化管理暂行规定》，向省级文化行政部门申请取得《网络文化经营许可证》，许可证的经营范围应当明确包括网络表演」. Playing games in front of an audience and commenting on them are managed on the same footing, and the regulations say 「参照本办法进行管理」. Video material of all online performances 「保存时间不得少于60日」 — must be kept for no less than 60 days, so you keep the videos you have streamed yourself for the full 60 days. Doing commercial internet culture business without approval gets an order to stop and a warning, then a fine of up to 30,000 yuan. Anyone told to stop and who goes on is put on the cultural market blacklist. Putting programmes online is a different licence: 「应当取得《信息网络传播视听节目许可证》」. The applicant 「具备法人资格，为国有独资或国有控股单位」 — must have the status of a legal person and be a wholly state-owned or a state-controlled entity, so it has to be a unit wholly owned or controlled by the state, and a private company basically cannot get it. Anyone who does it without the licence is warned and ordered to put it right, and may be fined up to 30,000 yuan. Where the circumstances are serious it is punished under the Regulations on the Administration of Broadcasting and Television (广播电视管理条例) (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Culture (文化部) (2016). Measures for the Administration of the Business of Online Performance, 网络表演经营活动管理办法（文市发〔2016〕33 号，第二、四、六、十三、二十条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/scgl/202012/t20201206_918194.html> ; Ministry of Culture (文化部) (2011). Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture, 互联网文化管理暂行规定（文化部令第 51 号，2017 年经文化部令第 57 号修订，第七、八、九、十六、二十一、二十八条）. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/bmgz/202012/t20201204_905340.html> ; National Radio and Television Administration (广电总局) and Ministry of Information Industry (信息产业部) (2007). Provisions on the Administration of Internet Audio-Visual Programme Services, 互联网视听节目服务管理规定（第 56 号令，2015 年修订，第二、七、八、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2022-08/23/content_5722664.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (国家网信办) and six other departments (2021). Guiding Opinions on Strengthening the Standardised Management of Online Livestreaming, 关于加强网络直播规范管理工作的指导意见（国信办发文〔2021〕3 号，第 9 项）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586472.htm>
- Notes: Which business goes with which licence is set out most clearly in the document of the seven departments. A platform that earns money from livestreaming needs the web culture licence plus the ICP filing, and the ICP filing is the registration the website does with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部). Putting programmes online needs either the audio-visual programme licence or the completed registration. Publishing news needs the Internet News Information Service Licence. One more thing: the Interim Provisions on the Administration of Internet Culture (互联网文化管理暂行规定) reprinted on the government website in 2025 are the unrevised 2011 text, so quote the version currently in force from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism (文化和旅游部)
### 4. A server inside China needs a filing, and the access provider must itself hold the value-added telecommunications licence
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: The filing costs nothing. It is submitted together with the server at the provider, and it usually takes a few days to a few weeks. Ten minutes is enough to check once whether a provider is qualified
- In plain language: A server inside China has to be filed before the site opens. The filing is free and goes in with the server at the provider, and it generally takes a few days to a few weeks. Without the filing you are ordered to put it right within a set period and fined 10,000 yuan, and if you do not put it right the site is closed. Not putting the filing number on the home page is a further fine of 5,000 to 50,000 yuan.
- Benefit: The regulations say 「未经备案，不得在中华人民共和国境内从事非经营性互联网信息服务」 — without a filing you may not carry out non-commercial internet information services inside the People's Republic of China. A website that does not charge its users still has to file first if it sits inside China. A site with no filing is ordered to put it right within a set period and is fined 10,000 yuan. Anyone who refuses to put it right has the site closed. The provider who connects your server to the network is covered too: it 「不得为未经备案的组织或者个人从事非经营性互联网信息服务提供互联网接入服务」. A provider that takes the money for you is covered in the same way. Such a provider 「不得为未依法取得经营许可证或者履行非经营性互联网信息服务备案手续的单位或者个人提供接入或者代收费等服务」. The meaning is that a proper provider may not take on a site that has neither the licence nor the filing. Then there are the two services the regulations call internet data centre business, which is colocation and cloud hosting, and internet access service business, which is broadband access. Both need a licence in their own right, so the provider must hold a licence first. A website whose home page does not show the filing number is ordered to put it right and fined 5,000 to 50,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Information Industry (信息产业部) (2005). Measures for the Filing of Non-Commercial Internet Information Services, 非经营性互联网信息服务备案管理办法（信产部令第 33 号，2024 年经工信部令第 68 号修订，第五、十三、十八、二十二条）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/gyhxxhb/jgsj/cyzcyfgs/bmgz/xxtxl/art/2024/art_84a0cfa0ebd049bbbe751dca9a008e56.html> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部) (2017). Measures for the Administration of Licences for the Conduct of Telecommunications Business, 电信业务经营许可管理办法（工信部令第 42 号，第二十、二十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2017/content_5240090.htm> ; Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (工业和信息化部) (2015). Catalogue of Telecommunications Business (2015 edition), 电信业务分类目录（2015 年版，B11、B14、B21、B25）. <https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tg/art/2020/art_e98406cd89844f7e92ea1bcf3b5301e0.html>
- Notes: Check whether the provider holds a licence before you talk about the price. To check a licence, use the MIIT integrated telecommunications business market management information system, tsm.miit.gov.cn, and search by the full name of the company or by the licence number. To check a website filing, use beian.miit.gov.cn. A 「data centre」 without the matching licence leaves you not knowing even who to appeal to on the day it fails. Putting the machine outside China does get round the filing. But where the users are in China and the money is in China, not one of the obligations written in items 5 to 10 in this section goes away, and you add the cost of sending data out of the country.

### 5. Let users sell on the platform and you have to verify, register, report the information and keep it for three years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to verify the sellers' qualifications in the back office and build a registration file. Report the tax information once a quarter and keep the transaction data for three years. The hard part is that this goes on all year; it is not something you do once
- In plain language: If you let other people sell on your platform, you must first verify the seller's identity, address, contact details and licences, then register the seller and open a file. Once the file exists, you go back and check again at least once every six months. Who the seller is goes to the market regulation authorities, and the tax position goes to the tax authorities once a quarter. Keep the goods and the transaction records for three years. Miss one item and you are first ordered to put it right, and if you do not, the fine is 20,000 to 100,000 yuan.
- Benefit: The law sets out a stretch of what the platform has to do. The words are 「应当要求申请进入平台销售商品或者提供服务的经营者提交其身份、地址、联系方式、行政许可等真实信息，进行核验、登记，建立登记档案，并定期核验更新」. Before a seller comes on the platform they hand over their identity, address, contact details and the licences they are supposed to hold. You check them, register them and open a file, and then after a while you have to go back and check again. How often is 「定期」 — regularly? The departmental rule is more exact: 「至少每六个月核验更新一次」 — at least once every six months. You also have to report the identity information of the sellers on the platform to the market regulation authorities. The tax authorities get a report too, in the words 「依照税收征收管理法律、行政法规的规定，向税务部门报送平台内经营者的身份信息和与纳税有关的信息」. So who the seller is goes to the market regulation authorities, and the seller's identity and tax position go to the tax authorities. Information on goods and services and information on transactions 「保存时间自交易完成之日起不少于三年」 — must be kept for no less than three years counting from the day the transaction is completed. If you fall short on any of the above, you are ordered to put it right within a set period. If you do not, the fine is 20,000 to 100,000 yuan. Where the circumstances are serious, you are ordered to suspend business for rectification and fined a further 100,000 to 500,000 yuan. The tax information has to be reported 「于季度终了的次月内」 — within the month following the end of the quarter. Missing that deadline carries the same fine (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2018). E-Commerce Law, 电子商务法（第十、二十七、二十八、三十一、八十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; State Administration for Market Regulation (市场监管总局) (2021). Measures for the Supervision of Online Trading, 网络交易监督管理办法（市场监管总局令第 37 号，第八、二十四、二十五、三十一条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5602020.htm> ; State Council (国务院) (2025). Provisions on the Reporting of Tax-Related Information by Internet Platform Enterprises, 互联网平台企业涉税信息报送规定（国务院令第 810 号，第二、三、四、十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12146/202507/content_7030983.html> ; State Taxation Administration (国家税务总局) (2025). Announcement on Matters Concerning the Reporting of Tax-Related Information by Internet Platform Enterprises, 关于互联网平台企业报送涉税信息有关事项的公告（税务总局公告 2025 年第 15 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202506/content_7029727.htm>
- Notes: The seller has to register as a market entity as well. The law lists a few cases where you do not: 「个人销售自产农副产品、家庭手工业产品，个人利用自己的技能从事依法无须取得许可的便民劳务活动和零星小额交易活动」 — a person selling their own agricultural and sideline products or home handicrafts, and a person using their own skills in convenience service activities that need no licence under the law and in occasional small-value transactions. 「零星小额」 — occasional and small in value — has a fixed figure: Article 8 of the Measures for the Supervision of Online Trading sets it at an annual turnover of 100,000 yuan. On the platform side there are two more things to do: mark the registered sellers and the unregistered ones separately, and do not allow the ratings left by consumers to be deleted
### 6. You are answerable for what users post: a review system, a reporting channel, and stop the transmission and report at once when you find something unlawful
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Money is needed to pay people to do the reviewing, and it is the platform's largest ongoing expense. The reporting channel and a record of every action taken both have to be kept. The hard part is that somebody has to watch it every day; one day off and something can go wrong
- In plain language: When something goes wrong with what a user has posted, the platform is answerable. You have to build this set of rules: registration, review of posts, review of comments, a daily sweep, and what to do when something goes wrong. Put the reporting channel somewhere the user can see at once. When you find unlawful information, stop the transmission at once, keep the records and report it to the authority in charge. If you do nothing, the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and more in the serious cases.
- Benefit: The regulations say the platform 「应当履行信息内容管理主体责任」 — must carry the main responsibility for the content, so what users post is the platform's own work to manage. What you have to set up is 「健全用户注册、账号管理、信息发布审核、跟帖评论审核、版面页面生态管理、实时巡查、应急处置和网络谣言、黑色产业链信息处置等制度」 — a system covering user registration, account management, the review of what is posted, the review of replies and comments, the management of the ecology of the pages and layouts, real-time patrolling, emergency handling, and the handling of online rumours and information about criminal supply chains. From registration, posting and comments to the daily sweep and the handling of an incident, every step needs a rule written down. You also 「应当在显著位置设置便捷的投诉举报入口」 — must put an easy complaint and reporting channel in a prominent position. When you find unlawful information you 「应当依法立即采取处置措施，保存有关记录，并向有关主管部门报告」. The Cybersecurity Law is more exact. The words are 「发现法律、行政法规禁止发布或者传输的信息的，应当立即停止传输该信息，采取消除等处置措施，防止信息扩散，保存有关记录，并向有关主管部门报告」. Anyone who fails to do this is ordered to put it right, is warned and is named in a public notice, and may be fined 50,000 to 500,000 yuan. Anyone who refuses to put it right, or where the circumstances are serious, is fined 500,000 to 2 million yuan, and may be ordered to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the licence or the business licence revoked. The person directly responsible is fined 50,000 to 200,000 yuan (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室) (2019). Provisions on the Governance of the Ecology of Network Information Content, 网络信息内容生态治理规定（网信办令第 5 号，第八、九、十、十六、三十七条）. <http://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-12/20/c_1578375159509309.htm> ; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2025). Cybersecurity Law, 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第四十九、六十九条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; State Council (国务院) (2000). Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services, 互联网信息服务管理办法（第十五、十六、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm>
- Notes: You do not have to think of the review rules yourself; copy them. The nine kinds of content that may not be posted are set out clearly in Article 15 of the Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services. If you livestream there are three more things: you have to 「建立直播内容审核平台」 — set up a platform for reviewing the content of the stream, you have to be able to cut off a livestream that is running at any moment, which the article calls 「即时阻断」 — instant blocking, and you have to keep the content of the stream and the logs for 60 days. The video material of an online performance is likewise kept for no less than 60 days
### 7. If you provide information posting or instant messaging, you must require real identity information from users
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Money is needed to connect a real-name verification channel, and these channels charge per check. Design the product on the principle of a real name in the back office and a free choice in the front: store the real name in the back office, show a nickname on the page
- In plain language: If your site lets people post, or lets them chat, you have to ask for the user's real identity when they take out the service. A user who does not give it may not be allowed to use it. The way to do it is to store the real name in the back office and let the user pick the nickname shown on the page; the phone number is enough for the check. If you do not take real names, you are first ordered to put it right. If you do not, the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and the site may be closed.
- Benefit: The law says 「网络运营者为用户办理网络接入、域名注册服务，办理固定电话、移动电话等入网手续，或者为用户提供信息发布、即时通讯等服务，在与用户签订协议或者确认提供服务时，应当要求用户提供真实身份信息。用户不提供真实身份信息的，网络运营者不得为其提供相关服务」. If you did not ask for the real name, or the user gave none and you let them use the service anyway, you are ordered to put it right. If you refuse to put it right, or where the circumstances are serious, the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and you may be ordered to suspend the relevant business, to suspend business for rectification, to close the website or the application, or to have the licence or the business licence revoked. The person directly responsible is fined 10,000 to 100,000 yuan. Livestreaming services follow the principle of a real name in the back office and a free choice in the front, with 「基于移动电话号码等方式的真实身份信息认证」 — verification of real identity information by means such as a mobile telephone number. The meaning is that the platform keeps the user's real name in the back office, the user decides the nickname shown on the page, and the check rests on a phone number or the like. Livestreaming and selling on livestreaming is stricter: 「基于身份证件信息、统一社会信用代码等真实身份信息认证」 — verification by real identity information such as identity document details and the unified social credit code. The unified social credit code is the string of characters on the business licence (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2025). Cybersecurity Law, 网络安全法（2025 年修正，2026 年 1 月 1 日施行，第二十六、六十四条；2016 年文本为第二十四、六十一条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室) (2016). Provisions on the Administration of Internet Livestreaming Services, 互联网直播服务管理规定（第十二、十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-11/04/c_1119847629.htm> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (国家网信办) and six other departments (2021). Interim Measures for the Administration of Online Livestreaming Marketing, 网络直播营销管理办法（试行）（国信办发文〔2021〕5 号，第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/23/content_5601682.htm>
- Notes: The article numbers have moved. The Cybersecurity Law was amended in 2025 and comes into force on 2026-1-1. The clause on real names moved from Article 24 to Article 26, and the penalties moved from Article 61 to Article 64. The wording did not change. If the material in your hands still cites the old numbers, they have to be corrected
### 8. Do not open livestreaming accounts for people under 16, and handle tipping money in bands by age
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Add one step at each of the two points, registration and payment: first check the age, then confirm that the guardian agreed
- In plain language: A person under 16 cannot register a livestreaming account with you. Someone who has reached 16 must have their identity verified, and must have the consent of their parents or other guardian. Tipping money falls into three bands: under 8 no tipping at all; 8 to 16 needs the guardian's consent; over 16 needs the guardian's consent or a check of proof of income. Where a child tips money behind the back of the guardian and the guardian demands it back, the court rules that it is returned.
- Benefit: The law says 「网络直播服务提供者不得为未满十六周岁的未成年人提供网络直播发布者账号注册服务；为年满十六周岁的未成年人提供网络直播发布者账号注册服务时，应当对其身份信息进行认证，并征得其父母或者其他监护人同意」. The regulations also require 「建立网络直播发布者真实身份信息动态核验机制」 — a mechanism for checking the real identity of the people who livestream, again at any time. They also require you to 「合理限制不同年龄阶段未成年人在使用其服务中的单次消费数额和单日累计消费数额」 — to set reasonable limits on a single payment and on the daily total for minors at each age band. Tipping now falls into three bands: 「不得向八周岁以下的未成年人提供打赏服务。向八周岁至十六周岁的未成年人提供打赏服务，应征得其监护人同意。向十六周岁以上的未成年人提供打赏服务，应征得其监护人同意或核验其收入证明材料」. There is one more clause: where a minor with limited capacity for civil conduct tips money without the guardian's consent and the guardian asks for the money back, the court supports it and the money can be recovered. A minor with limited capacity for civil conduct is one who cannot yet act entirely on their own in law (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2020). Law on the Protection of Minors, 未成年人保护法（第七十四、七十六条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2020-10/22/c_1604928959588622.htm> ; State Council (国务院) (2023). Regulations on the Protection of Minors Online, 未成年人网络保护条例（国务院令第 766 号，第三十一、四十四条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10806/202311/content_6913813.html> ; General Office of the Cyberspace Administration of China (中央网信办秘书局) (2026). Notice on Strengthening the Standardised Management of Online Livestreaming Tipping, 关于加强网络直播打赏规范管理的通知（第 7 项）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/13/c_1777815804150225.htm> ; Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院) (2020). Guiding Opinions on the Proper Handling of Civil Cases Related to the Novel Coronavirus Epidemic under the Law, 关于依法妥善审理涉新冠肺炎疫情民事案件若干问题的指导意见（二）（法发〔2020〕17 号，第九条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/230181.html>
- Notes: If a refund really is disputed, handle it in the direction that is best for the minor. The wording on this has changed. The opinions of the four departments in 2022 and the notice of the National Radio and Television Administration (广电总局) in 2020 both said that minors may not tip at all. The new notice of April 2026 changed it to bands at 8 and 16, but did not say that the earlier two documents are repealed, and the authorities have not said how the two fit together. So the sentence 「no minor may ever tip」 is no longer accurate.

### 9. Handle an infringement notice in time, and if nothing follows within 15 days of passing on the counter-notice, put the content back
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. You need a fixed process: receive the notice, pass it to the seller, take the seller's counter-notice, and publish the result. Keep a record at every step
- In plain language: When somebody sends you an infringement notice with preliminary evidence attached, you have to delete the material, block it or take the link down at once, and at the same time pass the notice to the seller. If you sit on it, you have to pay the extra part of the loss together with the seller. If the seller counters, you pass the counter-notice to the other side and wait 15 days. If they neither complain to an authority nor sue, you put the material back.
- Benefit: The rights holder 「有权通知电子商务平台经营者采取删除、屏蔽、断开链接、终止交易和服务等必要措施。通知应当包括构成侵权的初步证据」 — is entitled to tell the operator of the e-commerce platform to take the necessary measures, such as deleting, blocking, cutting the link, ending the transaction and ending the service, and the notice has to include preliminary evidence of the infringement. The rights holder is the owner of the trademark, the copyright or the patent. A sentence on its own is not enough; the preliminary evidence has to go with it. After the platform receives the notice, it 「应当及时采取必要措施，并将该通知转送平台内经营者；未及时采取必要措施的，对损害的扩大部分与平台内经营者承担连带责任」. If you sit on it, you share the liability with the seller for the extra part of the loss. After the seller has countered, the platform 「在转送声明到达知识产权权利人后十五日内，未收到权利人已经投诉或者起诉通知的，应当及时终止所采取的措施」 — once the counter-notice has reached the rights holder, if within 15 days no notice comes that the rights holder has complained to an authority or sued, the platform must end the measures it took at once. The meaning is that you pass the seller's counter-notice to the rights holder and wait 15 days. If they neither complain to an authority nor sue, you put back what you deleted or took down. A platform that knows or should know of an infringement and does nothing shares the liability with the infringer. The intellectual property authority can also order you to put it right within a set period; if you do not, the fine is 50,000 to 500,000 yuan, and where the circumstances are serious it is 500,000 to 2 million yuan. The other way round, 「恶意发出错误通知，造成平台内经营者损失的，加倍承担赔偿责任」 — anyone who maliciously sends a false notice and causes the seller a loss pays double the damages (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2018). E-Commerce Law, 电子商务法（第四十二、四十三、四十四、四十五、八十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf8af050b81> ; National People's Congress (全国人大) (2020). Civil Code, 民法典（第一千一百九十五、一千一百九十六、一千一百九十七条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html>
- Notes: If you are not an e-commerce platform but an ordinary website, the notice-and-takedown rules of the Civil Code apply. The difference is how long you have to wait after the other side counters: the E-Commerce Law gives a clear 15 days, while the Civil Code only says 「合理期限」 — a reasonable period. Publishing the notice you received, the seller's counter-notice and the result of your handling is an express requirement of Article 44 of the E-Commerce Law
### 10. Do not put users' information outside the country in passing; transfers out of China have statutory conditions and a headcount threshold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. The least trouble is to keep the user data inside China. Where it really has to go out, use the standard contract or get certified
- In plain language: Sending information about users inside China to machines outside the country is what the law calls a transfer out of China. Having the server outside China does not mean the data has not gone out. To take it out you must use one of four routes: a security assessment by the cyberspace administration authority, certification by a professional body, a contract signed on the standard contract, or whatever else the law provides. You also have to tell the users who receives the information outside China and what it is used for, and you have to ask them about that one thing on its own.
- Benefit: Handing information about users inside China to a company outside China, or storing it on a machine outside China, is in law 「向境外提供个人信息」 — providing personal information abroad, and it 「应当具备」 — must have one of four conditions. First, a security assessment organised by the cyberspace administration authority. Second, certification for the protection of personal information by a professional body. Third, a contract signed with the recipient outside China on the standard contract drawn up by the cyberspace administration authority. Fourth, other conditions set by laws and administrative regulations. At the same time you have to tell the users the name of the recipient outside China, its contact details, the purpose of the processing, the way it is processed, the kinds of personal information, and how the user exercises their own rights. And you have to 「取得个人的单独同意」 — obtain the separate consent of the individual, by asking the user about this one thing on its own. The thresholds are set by the total number of people. An ordinary company is, in the regulations, a data handler other than an operator of critical information infrastructure. Such companies have one line below which nothing has to be done: 「自当年1月1日起累计向境外提供不满10万人个人信息（不含敏感个人信息）的」 — where, counted from 1 January of that year, fewer than 100,000 people's personal information other than sensitive personal information has been provided abroad. Below that line, all three of declaring for an assessment, signing the standard contract and being certified are waived. At 100,000 people or more but fewer than 1,000,000, you must sign the standard contract or pass the certification. At 1,000,000 or more, or where sensitive personal information reaches 10,000 people or more, you must declare for a security assessment (nationwide, from 2024-3)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2021). Personal Information Protection Law, 个人信息保护法（第三十八、三十九、四十条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202108/t20210820_527244.shtml> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室) (2024). Provisions on Promoting and Standardising the Cross-Border Flow of Data, 促进和规范数据跨境流动规定（网信办令第 16 号，第三、四、五、七、八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11366/202405/content_6954192.html> ; Cyberspace Administration of China (国家互联网信息办公室) (2022). Measures for the Security Assessment of Data Transfers Out of China, 数据出境安全评估办法（网信办令第 11 号，第四条）. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-07/07/c_1658811536396503.htm>
- Notes: Having the server outside China is not the same as 「no data has gone out」. A user registers inside China and you send their information to a machine outside China, and that is a transfer out. There is one class that does not have to take the three routes of the security assessment, the certification and the standard contract: cross-border shopping, cross-border delivery and booking a flight or a hotel, because these are 「为订立、履行个人作为一方当事人的合同」 — transfers genuinely necessary to conclude or perform a contract to which the individual is a party. One more thing to watch is how the number of people is counted: it is counted 「自当年 1 月 1 日起累计」 — from 1 January of that year and accumulated, not over the twelve months counting back from today
### 11. Choosing a server: first ask whether you can put up with the downtime, then whether anybody maintains it, and compare prices last
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Cloud servers from the main cloud providers run from several hundred to several thousand yuan a month. A VPS from a small provider starts at a few tens of yuan a month. A VPS is a small virtual server. Colocation for a dedicated server starts at several thousand yuan a year, and the hardware you have to buy yourself
- In plain language: The three tiers differ not in price but in what you are holding on the day something fails. A large provider has a filing channel, publishes its qualifications, and when something breaks there is a notice, there is a compensation clause in the contract, and there is somebody you can find. A small provider costs half as much, but when it oversells, folds or is cut off upstream you do not even know who to complain to. For a static site with no user data, a VPS costing a few tens of yuan is enough.
- Benefit: The three tiers differ not in price but in what you are holding on the day something fails. The main cloud providers have a filing channel and publish their qualifications; when there is a fault there is a notice, the contract carries a compensation clause, and there is somebody you can find. A small provider can be half the price, but when it oversells, folds or is cut off upstream you have nobody to appeal to, so before you negotiate the price go to the MIIT system and check whether it holds a Value-Added Telecommunications Business Licence. Colocation for a dedicated server only makes sense when both conditions hold: bandwidth or hardware is clearly cheaper, and you have your own person to maintain it. How to choose: for a static site with no user data, the cheapest VPS is enough. For a site with user registration and transactions, choose a cloud server that scales on demand and takes snapshots, and keep the backups in at least two places
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: the author's own experience, no direct literature; the statutory requirements on the qualifications of a provider and on the filing are in item 4 in this section, and the duty to keep the logs for 6 months and the graded protection duties are in item 16 in section 11
- Notes: That is a judgement from experience, with no literature behind it. Only one thing can really be checked: the provider's licence. If you want to save money, save on the specifications and on the bandwidth, not on the qualifications
