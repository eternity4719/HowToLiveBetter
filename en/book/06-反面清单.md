# 6. The reverse list

This section collects the things that look cheap and effective. Open the original studies and their benefit is close to zero, exactly zero, and sometimes negative. The order rests on two things: how much money people spend on them, and how strong the evidence behind them is. The earlier an item appears, the more worth stopping it is.

### 1. Do not take multivitamins to live longer or prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 0.5 to 2 yuan a day, 200 to 700 yuan a year. Taking them takes no time, it is one pill. Taking them for a long time builds a habit, and then a day without one feels like something is missing.
- In plain language: 14,000 male doctors took multivitamins for eleven years. Major cardiovascular events and deaths were just as common as in those who did not. Pooling 9 trials with more than 50,000 people shows no effect on mortality either. Not buying saves you two hundred to seven hundred yuan a year. This does not cover people who come up genuinely deficient in a nutrient, or pregnant women taking folic acid.
- Benefit: The PHS II trial enrolled 14,641 male doctors and followed them for 11.2 years (the median; half were followed longer and half shorter). Among those who took the multivitamins and those who did not, the rate of major cardiovascular events was the same. Major cardiovascular events HR 1.01 (HR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same). The 95% CI 0.91–1.10 is the credible range for that figure. Mortality was the same too, all-cause mortality HR 0.94 (0.88–1.02). In 2022 the US Preventive Services Task Force, the body that grades preventive measures in the United States, pooled 9 randomised controlled trials with 51,550 people and found no association between multivitamins and all-cause mortality. It graded them I, which means the evidence does not support a conclusion either way. It looks like good value because "one pill covers every nutrient" sounds sensible and because it costs only a few tenths of a yuan a day
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sesso HD et al. (2012). Multivitamins in the prevention of cardiovascular disease in men: the Physicians' Health Study II randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14805> ; US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Vitamin, Mineral, and Multivitamin Supplementation to Prevent Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.8970> ; Gaziano JM et al. (2012). Multivitamins in the Prevention of Cancer in Men. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14641> (the cancer result discussed in the notes)
- Notes:Disputed: the same cohort of male doctors was also examined for cancer. Among those who took the multivitamins the rate of cancer was about 8% lower (0.86–0.998, P=0.04, which only just clears the bar of "not merely chance"). Death from cancer was about 12% lower (0.77–1.01). That interval crosses 1, so it may be chance. The benefit is small, and it has only been seen in male doctors. This does not cover people who come up genuinely deficient, or pregnant women taking folic acid, or people whose diet is genuinely poor; supplement those as the doctor says.
### 2. Do not take ordinary fish oil to prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 1 to 3 yuan a day, 400 to 1000 yuan a year. Taking them takes no time.
- In plain language: Two large trials enrolled 26,000 people and 15,000 people with diabetes. Among those taking 1 gram of fish oil a day, major cardiovascular events and deaths were just as common as in those who did not. "Eating fish is good for you" does not mean "fish oil capsules are good for you". Not buying saves you four hundred to a thousand yuan a year.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial enrolled 25,871 people, gave 1 gram of fish oil a day, and followed them for 5.3 years (median). Neither major cardiovascular events nor mortality differed between the two groups. Major cardiovascular events HR 0.92 (HR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same). The 95% CI 0.80–1.06 is the credible range for that figure. It crosses 1, which means no difference can be seen. All-cause mortality HR 1.02 (0.90–1.15). The ASCEND trial enrolled 15,480 people with diabetes and also used 1 gram a day, followed for an average of 7.4 years, and again found no difference. Serious vascular events RR 0.97 (0.87–1.08). All-cause mortality RR 0.95 (0.86–1.05). It looks like good value because many people take "eating fish is good for you" to mean "fish oil capsules are good for you"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Marine n-3 Fatty Acids and Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1811403> ; ASCEND Study Collaborative Group (2018). Effects of n-3 Fatty Acid Supplements in Diabetes Mellitus. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1804989> ; Bhatt DL et al. (2019). Cardiovascular Risk Reduction with Icosapent Ethyl for Hypertriglyceridemia. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792> (the high-purity prescription fish oil trial discussed in the notes)
- Notes:Disputed: one trial did work. The REDUCE-IT trial enrolled 8,179 people who already had cardiovascular disease or diabetes, were all taking statins, and had high triglycerides (135–499 mg/dL). They took 4 g of prescription pure EPA a day, and 17.2% had an event against 22.0% in the comparison group. The risk was about a quarter lower (HR 0.75, 0.68–0.83). But that is a high-dose prescription drug for high-risk patients, which is not the same as an ordinary person taking a fish oil supplement. Neither does this cover eating fish itself.
### 3. Do not give vitamin D to people who are not deficient, in the name of living longer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 0.2 to 0.5 yuan a day, 100 to 200 yuan a year. The money is not much; the real cost is believing you have already done something about prevention.
- In plain language: Among people who were not deficient in vitamin D, five years of supplements made no difference to how many developed cancer, cardiovascular disease or died. People who come up genuinely deficient, people with osteoporosis and people who get no sun are not covered here; they supplement as the doctor says.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial enrolled 25,871 people, gave 2000 IU a day, and followed them for 5.3 years (median). No difference could be seen in cancer, cardiovascular disease or mortality. For the cancers that spread, HR 0.96 (HR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same; the 95% CI 0.88–1.06 is the credible range). Major cardiovascular events HR 0.97 (0.85–1.12). All-cause mortality HR 0.99 (0.87–1.12). The D-Health trial enrolled 21,315 Australians over 60 years old. They took 60,000 IU a month and were followed for 5 years. This showed no difference either, all-cause mortality HR 1.04 (0.93–1.18). The mortality rates were 5.3% against 5.1%. It looks like good value because some studies only keep records without intervening at all, find that people with a low vitamin D level die more often, and that finding has been misread as "supplementing it helps"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Vitamin D Supplements and Prevention of Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1809944> ; Neale RE et al. (2022). The D-Health Trial: a randomised controlled trial of the effect of vitamin D on mortality. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-8587(21)00345-4>
- Notes:Disputed: neither trial deliberately recruited people who were genuinely deficient in vitamin D, so the conclusion only covers people who were not deficient to begin with. People who come up genuinely deficient, people with osteoporosis, people who get no sun, and infants should still supplement as the doctor says. This only looked at all-cause mortality, cancer and cardiovascular disease, and at nothing else.
### 4. Do not take antioxidant supplements (beta carotene, vitamin E, vitamin A) to prevent cancer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 0.5 to 2 yuan a day. For a smoker the real cost is that the risk of lung cancer goes up with it.
- In plain language: This one is not merely useless. Pooling 78 trials with close to 300,000 people, people taking antioxidant supplements had a slightly higher mortality rate. In two large trials that recruited smokers specifically, beta carotene raised the rate of lung cancer by 18% to 28% and death by 8% to 17%. Smokers and people exposed to asbestos should be especially careful.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 78 randomised controlled trials with 296,707 people. Among people taking antioxidant supplements, all-cause mortality RR 1.02 (RR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same; the 95% CI 0.98–1.05 is the credible range). Looking only at the better-designed trials that are harder to get wrong, RR 1.04 (1.01–1.07), a little higher. Beta carotene alone RR 1.05 (1.01–1.09), vitamin E alone RR 1.03 (1.00–1.05). The ATBC trial enrolled 29,133 male smokers who took 20 mg a day; lung cancer was 18% higher (3%–36%) and all-cause mortality 8% higher (1%–16%). The CARET trial enrolled 18,314 smokers or people exposed to asbestos. Their risk of lung cancer was about 28% higher (RR 1.28, 1.04–1.57). Their risk of death was about 17% higher (all-cause mortality RR 1.17, 1.03–1.33). It looks like good value because the chain of reasoning "antioxidants mean anti-ageing, and anti-ageing prevents cancer" sounds like common sense at every step
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Bjelakovic G et al. (2012). Antioxidant supplements for prevention of mortality in healthy participants and patients with various diseases. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007176.pub2> ; The Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta Carotene Cancer Prevention Study Group (1994). The effect of vitamin E and beta carotene on the incidence of lung cancer and other cancers in male smokers. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199404143301501> ; Omenn GS et al. (1996). Effects of a combination of beta carotene and vitamin A on lung cancer and cardiovascular disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199605023341802>
- Notes: In 2022 USPSTF, the official United States body that grades preventive measures, gave beta carotene and vitamin E a grade D, meaning it recommends against them. Smokers and people exposed to asbestos have already been harmed by them. Antioxidants contained in fruit and vegetables are not affected; keep eating those.
### 5. Do not expect glucosamine or chondroitin to treat knee osteoarthritis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 2 to 5 yuan a day, 700 to 1800 yuan a year.
- In plain language: Among people with osteoarthritis of the knee given a placebo for 24 weeks, six in ten also said the pain eased. Taking glucosamine or chondroitin adds only four or five percentage points on top of that, small enough to be chance. Seven hundred to eighteen hundred yuan a year buys you, in effect, the placebo response of those six in ten.
- Benefit: The GAIT trial enrolled 1,583 people with knee osteoarthritis and ran for 24 weeks. Among those given a placebo, 60.1% also reported that their pain eased. Glucosamine was only 3.9 percentage points above that (P=0.30). Chondroitin was 5.3 points higher (P=0.17). The two together were 6.5 points higher (P=0.09). All these gaps are too small to rule out chance. The real drug used as the comparator in the same trial, celecoxib, was 10.0 points higher (P=0.008). It looks like good value because "the raw material for cartilage" sounds exactly right for the problem, and because six out of ten people report a benefit from the placebo alone
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Clegg DO et al. (2006). Glucosamine, chondroitin sulfate, and the two in combination for painful knee osteoarthritis. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa052771>
- Notes:Disputed: in the same trial, among the people who hurt most at the start, 79.2% of those taking both improved against 54.3% in the comparison group (P=0.002). The authors say this was found after the fact in the data, so it can only be a lead, not a conclusion. The approaches to knee osteoarthritis that really have evidence behind them are losing weight and strengthening muscle; see section 2.

### 6. Do not rely on vitamin C to prevent colds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: An effervescent tablet costs about 1 to 2 yuan, 300 to 700 yuan a year.
- In plain language: Taking vitamin C every day will basically not make you catch fewer colds, and starting once the cold has begun works only sometimes. Taking it every day shortens a cold by about 8%, which is about half a day less of feeling unwell; whether that is worth three or five hundred yuan a year is for you to work out. Marathon runners and soldiers marching in cold regions are the exception: among them, taking it halves the number who catch a cold.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 29 controlled trials with 11,306 people. Among ordinary people taking vitamin C every day, the number of colds caught does not really fall. The rate of catching a cold RR 0.97 (RR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same). The 95% CI 0.94–1.00 is the credible range. In the trials where people started taking it only after the cold had begun, the text says「未见一致效应」: some trials found an effect and some did not. In the same review, taking it every day over the long term shortens how long a cold lasts by about 8% in adults (95% CI 3%–12%) and by 14% in children (7%–21%). Marathon runners, skiers and soldiers marching in cold regions are the exception. Across 5 trials with 598 people, those who took it caught about half as many colds. Incidence RR 0.48 (95% CI 0.35–0.64). It looks like good value because it is cheap, it tastes sweet and sour, and the claim that vitamin C prevents colds has been in circulation for half a century
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Hemilä H, Chalker E (2013). Vitamin C for preventing and treating the common cold. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000980.pub4>
- Notes:Disputed: the same review does find that taking it every day shortens how long a cold lasts. By about 8% in adults (3%–12%) and by 14% in children (7%–21%). Worked out, that is about half a day less illness per cold. Marathon runners, skiers and soldiers marching in cold regions are the exception. Across 5 trials with 598 people, those who took it caught about half as many colds (RR 0.48, 0.35–0.64). Almost nobody falls into either of those two groups.
### 7. Do not give yourself a whole-body PET-CT or a tumour marker panel when you have no symptoms
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A whole-body PET-CT costs about 7000 to 10000 yuan each time, and you take a dose of ionising radiation. A tumour marker panel costs a few hundred yuan. The real cost comes later: one "abnormality" turns up, it was nothing (this is a false positive), and then the follow-up scan, the tissue sample and the operation all cost money and cause suffering.
- In plain language: Among people with no symptoms, the most common result is a scare. More than four in five people who have a whole-body CT have at least one "abnormality" found, and nearly four in ten are called back for further checks, but most of those are benign. Women screened for ovarian cancer markers actually died of ovarian cancer slightly more often, and some had an operation they did not need because of a scare.
- Benefit: USPSTF, the United States body that grades preventive measures, gave a grade D to screening asymptomatic women for ovarian cancer with CA-125 and ultrasound, meaning it recommends against it. The evidence is the PLCO trial. Death from ovarian cancer was 0.34% in the screened group and 0.29% in the usual care group. RR 1.18 (95% CI 0.82–1.71). Screening did no good. The share who went under the knife needlessly because the result was wrong (a false positive) was 0.2%–3.25% across the trials, and up to 15% of those people had a serious surgical complication. Another study gave 1,192 people without symptoms a whole-body CT screening. 86% had at least one "abnormality" found, and 37% were advised to have further tests. The text says「大多数发现按描述是良性的」. It looks like good value because "screen the whole body once, find it early, treat it early" is so intuitive
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2018). Screening for Ovarian Cancer: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA（同一份建议另有 USPSTF 官方页）. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.21926> , <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/ovarian-cancer-screening> ; Furtado CD et al. (2005). Whole-body CT screening: spectrum of findings and recommendations in 1192 patients. Radiology. <https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2372041741>
- Notes: The tumour marker part is backed by randomised controlled trials. The whole-body CT and PET-CT part rests only on follow-up records of what happened to the people who had it, with no control group, so the evidence is a grade weaker, equivalent to B. The screenings that really do have evidence behind them, such as colorectal cancer, cervical cancer, breast cancer, and low-dose chest CT for people at high risk, are a different matter; see section 1. A PET-CT in someone who has symptoms, or who already has a cancer diagnosis, is medical care rather than screening and has to be judged separately.
### 8. Do not expect a wristband or watch that counts your exercise to help you lose weight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: The device costs 200 to 2000 yuan. On top of that you have to break your concentration each day to look at the data.
- In plain language: Overweight people doing the same diet and exercise plan lost 3.5 kg over two years while wearing a fitness band, and those not wearing one lost 5.9 kg. If you want to lose weight, spending money on a device does not help. The exercise itself is what works.
- Benefit: The IDEA trial enrolled 471 overweight or obese adults and followed them for 24 months. Everyone followed the same diet and exercise plan; the only difference was whether they wore a wearable device. The group that wore one lost 3.5 kg (95% CI 2.6–4.5, the credible range). The group that did not lost 5.9 kg (5.0–6.8), which is more. The two groups differ by 2.4 kg (1.0–3.7, P=0.002). It looks like good value because "you cannot manage what you cannot measure" sounds sensible, and because the device only has to be bought once
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Jakicic JM et al. (2016). Effect of Wearable Technology Combined With a Lifestyle Intervention on Long-term Weight Loss: The IDEA Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.12858>
- Notes: There is only one trial here, and it used the devices worn on the arm in the early 2010s, so it does not show that all wearables are useless. Exercise itself does work; see section 2. The conclusion is one sentence: if you want to lose weight, a device is not money you have to spend.
### 9. Do not pay a premium for organic food in the name of being "healthier"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Usually 1 to 3 times the price of the same kind of ordinary food.
- In plain language: There is no strong evidence that organic food is more nutritious. Only 3 studies have looked at how people's health turned out after eating it, and none found a difference. Organically grown produce does test positive for pesticide residues about 30 percentage points less often, but a positive test is not the same as exceeding the limit. Eating more fruit and vegetables does help, and whether they are organic makes no difference to that.
- Benefit: One systematic review brought together 17 studies carried out in people and 223 studies measuring food composition. The conclusion is that there is「缺乏有力证据表明有机食品比常规食品显著更有营养」. Only 3 of those studies looked at people's health after eating the food, and they looked at allergies and symptomatic infections; no difference was found between the two. The share of organically grown produce testing positive for pesticide residues is about 30 percentage points lower, but a positive test is not the same as exceeding the limit. It looks like good value because "no pesticides is safer, and safer is healthier" sounds like common sense at every step
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Smith-Spangler C et al. (2012). Are organic foods safer or healthier than conventional alternatives?: a systematic review. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-157-5-201209040-00007>
- Notes: Very few studies look directly at how people's health turns out after eating it. So the conclusion is "there is no evidence that it is better", not "it has been proved to have no benefit". The effect on the environment, on animal welfare and on taste are not covered here. Eating more fruit and vegetables does work, and whether they are organic makes no difference to that.
### 10. Do not spend a lot of money on health supplements and other health products, herbal pastes or tonics to "look after your health"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: From several hundred yuan to more than 10,000 yuan. Once it is packaged as a gift set, the price often goes up several times over.
- In plain language: The common ingredients — multivitamins, fish oil, vitamin D — have each been checked one by one in the first six items of this section, and none of them showed a benefit. The sentence 「保健食品不是药物，不能代替药物治疗疾病」on the pack is one the authority in charge of health foods requires to be printed. This money is usually spent on buying something for your parents and carrying it to their door. The thought behind it is not the problem; the choice of product is.
- Benefit: What you get back is the money you did not spend. The reasons are in the first 6 items of this section: the commonest ingredients in health products — multivitamins, fish oil, vitamin D, antioxidants, glucosamine, vitamin C — have shown none of the expected benefits in randomised controlled trials, and the figures for each are in the Benefit field of those 6 items. The regulator's rules only say what a product may claim; they are not evidence that it does nothing. The State Administration for Market Regulation requires the front of a health food package to carry one sentence in no less than 20% of the area: 「保健食品不是药物，不能代替药物治疗疾病」. The same rules also pin down what a health food may be used for, limiting it to 「补充膳食营养物质、维持改善机体健康状态或者降低疾病发生风险因素」. It looks like good value because "it does not cure, it only looks after you" can never be called wrong, and nobody can prove otherwise. And when you spend this money on a gift, what you are relying on is affection, not evidence
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局) (2019). Press Conference Held by the State Administration for Market Regulation on the Guidelines for Warning Wording on Health Food Labels and the Administrative Measures for the Catalogue of Health Food Raw Materials and Health Functions. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/tssps/sjdt/tpxw/art/2023/art_4b658b824b1b4b0ba57c09a56cc93aad.html>
- Notes: The conclusion is "there is no evidence in favour", not "it has been proved ineffective", so it is only graded C. For a product that lists its ingredients, look up the matching study as in the earlier items. If you really are short of a nutrient, or you are being treated for something, do what the doctor says. What should be rejected is the product, not the thought. If older people bought it themselves and are already taking it, as long as it has not replaced a medicine they are taking and has caused no harm, there is no need to go and argue the point; arranging the vaccinations and the screening they are due is more important. The same money spent on the following is better value, and every one of them can be traced in this book. Going with an older person for the annual flu vaccine: see item 20 in section 1. The shingles vaccine after the age of 50: see item 21 in section 1. The pneumococcal vaccine over the age of 65: see item 22 in section 1. Buying a blood pressure monitor and keeping them on their prescribed blood pressure medicine until their readings are at target: see item 7 in section 1. Modifying the bathroom and stairs at home and practising balance and leg strength with them: see item 13 in section 1. Cancer screening at the age for it: go with them once, see items 17 to 19 in section 1. For an older person already bedridden, pressure ulcer prevention and long-term care insurance: see items 7 and 8 in section 17. If you are bringing something to their door, fruit, rice and flour, or something they like to eat, is more use than a herbal paste. The two things really worth stopping are these: investing in a retirement plan where you pay money up front, and anything that would replace a medicine they are taking; see item 5 in section 17

### 11. Do not take probiotics long term when you have no bowel problem
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About 2 to 5 yuan a day, 700 to 1800 yuan a year.
- In plain language: Pooling 45 trials in healthy adults, probiotics do not change the bacteria in the gut for long; they go back to where they were when you stop, and blood lipids do not improve either. Specific situations such as diarrhoea caused by antibiotics have their own evidence and are not covered here.
- Benefit: One review pooled 45 trials in healthy adults and concludes that it「未能支持益生菌能引起肠道菌群的持久改变，也未能改善健康成人的血脂」. The bacteria in the gut do change, but only temporarily, and they go back when you stop. It looks like good value because "gut health" is broad enough that nothing can be said against it, and because one bottle a day comes to very little per bottle
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Khalesi S et al. (2019). A review of probiotic supplementation in healthy adults: helpful or hype? Eur J Clin Nutr. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-018-0135-9>
- Notes: The same review does report a few small improvements in healthy people, such as more formed and suitable stools and more lactobacilli in women's vaginas. Diarrhoea caused by antibiotics, and one particular strain used for one particular condition, each have their own evidence and have to be judged separately. This item is only about healthy people taking it daily as a supplement.

### 12. Do not force yourself into cold showers in the name of "strengthening immunity"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Every shower is uncomfortable for 30 to 90 seconds, and keeping it up through winter takes willpower. People with cardiovascular disease also have to be careful, because cold stimulation is a risk for them.
- In plain language: In the trial of 3018 people, those who took cold showers asked for fewer sick days off, 29% fewer by number, but the total number of days off was the same as everyone else's, so they were not less ill. A 2025 pooling of 11 trials found that immediately after cold water the markers of inflammation in the body actually rose briefly, and the immune markers did not change. Take cold showers if you enjoy them, but do not treat it as an investment in your health.
- Benefit: The Netherlands ran a randomised controlled trial with 3,018 people who took a cold shower for 30, 60 or 90 seconds after a hot one. They took 29% fewer sick days by number (IRR 0.71, which is a ratio of counts). But the text states「病假天数没有显著组间差异」: the total number of days off was the same in both groups. Quality of life, work productivity and anxiety also showed no meaningful difference. A 2025 systematic review pooled 11 randomised trials with 3,177 people. Immediately after the cold immersion, and again 1 hour later, the markers of inflammation in the body rose rather than fell. The immune markers did not change. Stress fell only at the 12-hour time point. The authors themselves say the evidence is「受限于 RCT 少、样本小」, which means there are too few randomised trials and each one has too few people in it. It looks like good value because it is free, and because "suffering makes it work" is a very smooth intuition
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Buijze GA et al. (2016). The Effect of Cold Showering on Health and Work: A Randomized Controlled Trial. PLOS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161749> ; Cain T et al. (2025). Effects of cold-water immersion on health and wellbeing: A systematic review and meta-analysis. PLOS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0317615>
- Notes:Disputed: the number of sick days taken really did fall by 29%, and that gap does not look like chance. But the total number of days ill did not fall, so it looks more like "going to work even when ill" than genuinely being less ill. Cold water used to help athletes recover after training is a separate question and is not covered here. A cold shower does no harm to a healthy person in itself; just do not treat it as an investment in your health.
### 13. Do not buy detox, colon cleansing, enzyme or alkaline water products

### 16. Do not buy blue-light glasses to "protect your eyesight", and do not believe that "your eyes are ruined after a few months in front of a screen"; but a swollen, red eye is an emergency
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money, and you also save the several hundred to several thousand yuan added to the price of the lenses.
- In plain language: Blue-light filtering lenses do not work. Not buying them saves you the several hundred to several thousand yuan. Compared with ordinary lenses, people wearing them are neither less tired nor able to see more sharply, and no evidence has been found that they protect the back of the eye. Eye strain and dryness from looking at a screen do settle. What really damages the eye is welding arc light, ultraviolet light and laser light, and an acute attack of glaucoma.
- Benefit: The Cochrane 2023 systematic review included 17 randomised controlled trials of between 5 and 156 people each, with follow-up ranging from less than a day to 5 weeks. Because there was too little data, the trials enrolled very different people, and the follow-up times did not match, the authors did not pool them into one overall figure. The results below are all against lenses with no blue-light filter. Where follow-up was shorter than a week, self-reported eye fatigue "may make little or no difference" and the certainty of the evidence is low. In one trial of 120 people the two groups differed by 9.76 units. The credible range of that difference is 95% CI −33.95 to 53.47. A range that crosses both signs means no difference can be made out. Critical flicker fusion frequency is an objective measure of eye fatigue; the two groups "may be almost identical", with low certainty. The best corrected visual acuity "is probably not affected". The two groups differ by 0.00 logMAR (95% CI −0.02 to 0.02). This outcome rests on 1 study and 156 people, with moderate certainty. - Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Singh S, Downie LE, Anderson AJ, Keller PR, White SJ, Ang M, Wolffsohn JS (2023). Blue-light filtering spectacle lenses for visual performance, sleep, and macular health in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD013244. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2>
- Notes: Looking at a screen brings eye strain and dry eye, which show up as soreness, dryness and blurred vision after a while. Resting and blinking more often eases them. That they settle does not mean "you have damaged your eyes". This is the consensus of ophthalmology, not the conclusion of the Cochrane review in the Sources field, so it is graded C only. ### 17. Do not treat blood donation as a health practice: "detox, lower cholesterol, prevent heart attacks" has no support from randomised trials, while donating too often really does run you out of iron
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. One donation, with the forms, the blood draw and the time sitting afterwards, takes about an hour. For weeks afterwards your body is making up the iron you lost.
- In plain language: The claim that donating blood detoxifies, lowers cholesterol and prevents heart attacks does not hold up. People who keep giving blood were already healthier to begin with; take that layer out and the fall in mortality from one extra donation a year shrinks from nearly a fifth to 7.5%. In the trials that actually separated people into groups, there was no difference in deaths. In the other direction, donating too often really does make people weak and sensitive to the cold, and the reason is iron deficiency.
- Benefit: The Kuopio study in Finland followed 2862 men aged 42 to 60 for an average of nearly 9 years. Of the 153 who had given blood in the 24 months before counting began, 1 (0.7%) had an acute heart attack. Among the 2529 who had not given blood, 316 (12.5%) had one (the difference between the two proportions P<0.0001). Once the effect of age and other coronary heart disease risk factors was taken out, those who had given blood had about 88% lower risk of heart attack. Relative risk 0.12 (95% CI 0.02–0.86, P=0.035). But this comes only from follow-up records, with no control group. The Nordic SCANDAT database holds 1,182,495 donors and 9,526,627 person-years of follow-up in total, of whom 15,401 died. Taking out only characteristics such as age and sex, giving blood one extra time a year was associated with 18.6% lower mortality (95% CI 16.8%–20.4%). Taking out also the layer of "you only stay allowed to give blood if you stay healthy", the reduction is only 7.5% (5.7%–9.4%). The authors conclude that it「不能解释为有益健康的确凿证据」. The FeAST randomised controlled trial enrolled 1277 patients with symptomatic lower limb arterial disease and randomised them to two groups, one having blood drawn every 6 months on a fixed schedule to reduce iron, the other as a comparison. The main measure was all-cause mortality, 125/636 (20%) against 148/641 (23%). HR 0.85 (HR is the risk ratio, where 1 means the two groups are the same). 95% CI 0.67–1.08, P=0.17. Taken together, death plus heart attack and stroke among the survivors was 180 (28%) against 205 (32%), HR 0.88 (0.72–1.07, P=0.20). Neither showed a difference. The INTERVAL randomised trial enrolled 45,263 people giving whole blood. They were split into three different intervals between donations. For men they were 12, 10 and 8 weeks, for women 16, 14 and 12 weeks. With the interval shortened, noticeably more blood was collected over two years (the 8-week group of men gave 1.69 units more than the 12-week group, 95% CI 1.59–1.80). Quality of life, physical activity and cognitive function showed no difference between the groups. But there was more discomfort from giving blood, including tiredness, breathlessness, dizziness, lightheadedness and restless legs (legs so uncomfortable at night that you cannot lie still). At the same time the average haemoglobin and ferritin were lower, and there were more deferrals for low haemoglobin (all items P<0.0001). Iron deficiency affects how much heat the body makes: 10 women with iron deficiency anaemia, 8 people whose iron stores were exhausted but who were not yet anaemic, and 12 control women soaked in water at 28 ℃ for 100 minutes. The anaemia group had a rectal temperature of 36.0±0.2 ℃ against 36.2±0.1 ℃ in the control group (P=0.001). Oxygen consumption was 5.28±0.26 against 5.99±0.29 mL·min⁻¹·kg⁻¹ (P=0.04). The two thyroid hormones, thyroxine and triiodothyronine, were also lower (P<0.002). The claim looks sound because people who give blood are healthy people who have been screened again and again, and because the hypothesis that "letting iron out protects the heart" has not been confirmed in a randomised trial

- Notes: What is being rejected is donating blood for the sake of your own health, not blood donation itself. ### 19. Do not start urate-lowering drugs because a check-up found high uric acid and you have never had an attack

- Cost: No money, and it saves the long run of medicine and repeat testing as well as the risk of a dose you did not need.
- In plain language: The American College of Rheumatology recommends that people whose uric acid is above the limit but who have never had an attack of gout and have no gouty tophus should not start urate-lowering drugs. You have to treat 24 people for 3 years to prevent one attack. The hope that it protects the kidney has also fallen through: kidney function fell just as fast in the people taking the drug as in those taking a placebo. Treat it after the first real attack.
- Benefit: The American College of Rheumatology's 2020 gout guideline covers asymptomatic hyperuricaemia. It means a blood urate above 6.8 mg/dL, with no previous attack of gout and no tophus under the skin. The guideline conditionally recommends that these people do not start any urate-lowering drug. The drugs include allopurinol, febuxostat and probenecid. The certainty of the evidence is graded "high". The footnote under the table states「按归因风险计算，需治疗 24 名患者 3 年才能预防一次痛风发作」. ### 20. Do not have a preventive cholecystectomy because a check-up found gallstones you have never felt from (asymptomatic gallstones)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and you also save one operation under general anaesthetic and one hospital stay. What you take on instead is remembering which signals mean you have to go to hospital at once.
- In plain language: Of the people who have stones in the gallbladder and have never felt pain, about 80% stayed without symptoms over nearly nine years. Leave the stones alone and the chance of an emergency such as cholecystitis or pancreatitis each year is about one in a thousand to three in a thousand. Removing the gallbladder is not risk-free either: about 1.5% of people have the bile duct injured. So the European and British guidelines both say that if there are no symptoms, do not operate yet.
- Benefit: The Italian MICOL population survey put 11,229 people aged 29 to 69 through an ultrasound. 856 were found to have gallstones, of whom 580 had no symptoms, and they were followed for 8.7 years on average. ### 21. Do not cut out calcium in order to prevent kidney stones

### 23. Do not expect buying things to improve your mood or your sense of who you are

- Cost: No money. The hard part is not ordering when you feel low.
- In plain language: The more a person values money and material things, the less happy they say they are, but the association is weak and it does not show what leads to what. Other research has found that spending money on an experience makes people happier than spending it on things. So when you feel low, spending on going out or on a meal with someone is more likely to help than filling a shopping basket.
- Benefit: One meta-analysis (pooling many studies) covered 259 independent samples and 753 effect sizes. The result: the more a person values material things, the lower the well-being they report. On the most commonly used multidimensional scale the correlation is r = -0.19 (ρ = -0.24 after correction). On scales that measure only the single aspect "valuing money", the association is even weaker, r = -0.08 to -0.11. Split by which outcome is being examined, the strength differs. The strongest associations are with risky health and consumption behaviour and with negative self-evaluation, r = -0.28 to -0.44. The weakest are with life satisfaction and negative mood, r = -0.13 to -0.15. The authors also ran a mediation analysis (to see what the effect travels through), and the result suggests that this negative association may come from unmet psychological needs. Another study: in two surveys of different populations, respondents said that money spent on an experience made them happier than money spent on things. In the laboratory experiments that followed, the good mood recalled after spending money on an experience was also higher than that recalled after spending it on things (the national standard does not apply; both studies used foreign samples)

- Sources: Dittmar H, Bond R, Hurst M, Kasser T (2014). The relationship between materialism and personal well-being: A meta-analysis. ### 24. Do not spend more to move up one notch among the people around you, by changing house, car or circle
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. All you have to do is ask one more question before you add to your budget: is this extra money buying the thing itself, or your position among these people? The hard part is keeping up when everybody around you has changed.
- In plain language: Comparing people with the same income, the more a neighbour earns, the lower that person reports their own sense of well-being. This only happens with neighbours who have the same education as you and whom you see often. So the extra money you spend to move into a place where everybody is better off than you buys yourself a move down a notch. But when your own income rises, you do feel better off: the absolute level still counts.
- Benefit: Two waves of the US National Survey of Families and Households (NSFH) revisited the same group of people. The author matched this data to local median income. Local median income (PUMA) is estimated from a 5% sample of the 1990 census. After subtracting the person's own income and a set of personal characteristics, the result was this: the higher a neighbour's income, the lower the person's own reported sense of well-being. The effect is large, it holds under a different specification, and it is highly significant. The author puts it this way: the drop in well-being from "a neighbour's income rising" is of the same order as the drop from "your own income falling by the same amount". Changed to comparing one person with themselves before and after (adding individual fixed effects), the sign of the coefficient is unchanged and its size is similar; only the significance falls to the 10% level. Narrowed further to comparisons within one state and one wave of the survey (adding state and wave fixed effects), the result is basically unchanged. That means it is not caused by "people who love moving to poorer places being happier to begin with", nor by some unmeasured factor at state level. The effect is stronger in people who are often in contact with their neighbours and not significant in people whose contact is with friends outside the neighbourhood. There is no difference between renters and people who own their home, which does not support the explanation "prices are high in expensive places, so there is less money to spend in reality". Sense of well-being falls only with the income of neighbours who have the same education, and barely responds to the income of neighbours with a different education. And it is carried mainly by satisfaction with one's own material circumstances, not by satisfaction with family life or anything else. Lastly, if your own income and your neighbour's income rise together by the same proportion, sense of well-being still rises

People who skipped breakfast did not make it up at lunch or dinner. These trials were all short: an average of 7 weeks for weight, an average of 2 weeks for intake.
