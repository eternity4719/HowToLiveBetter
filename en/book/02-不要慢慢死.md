# 2. 不要慢慢死

本节只收两类慢性风险：一类对总死亡率影响大，一类证据硬。条目按性价比从高到低排。有几条标了「随机试验」，意思是把人随机分成两组来比，这种结果最可信。其余数字都来自只跟踪记录、不做分组的研究。这类研究算出的 HR、RR 等风险比里混着两种干扰：这些人本来就不一样，有人是先病了才这么做。所以这些数字只说明方向和大概多大，不代表照做就能降这么多。

### 1. Quit smoking, the earlier the better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it saves you money instead. A pack a day costs 20–30 yuan, and quitting leaves that money in your pocket. The hard part is the withdrawal period, which usually runs from a few weeks to several months.
- In plain language: Smokers live on average more than ten years less than non-smokers. Quitting before 40 wipes out about nine tenths of the death risk that going on smoking carries. The earlier you quit, the more you get back.
- Benefit: A US study that followed a group of people, recording only and not assigning groups, found that people who still smoke have a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than people who have never smoked. Quitting before 40 removes about 90% of the death risk that continuing to smoke carries. People who quit at 25–34 live about 10 years longer, those who quit at 35–44 about 9 years longer, and those who quit at 45–54 about 6 years longer (the source reports these in years of life). Similar Chinese research: in the 2010s, the death risk of urban men who smoke was 1.65 times that of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and of rural men 1.22 times. Ten years after you stop, the risk carried by smoking has almost gone.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Jha P et al. (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128> ; Chen Z et al. (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2> ; Oberg M et al. (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8> (the two second-hand smoke figures)
- Notes: Breathing other people's smoke kills too: about 603,000 people died worldwide from second-hand smoke in 2004, about 1% of all deaths that year. If you do not smoke, stay away from other people's smoke, and above all keep your children out of it. For how to quit, the specific methods are in the next four items: item 3 (cessation drugs), item 4 (set a quit date), item 5 (go to a smoking cessation clinic), item 6 (e-cigarettes)
### 2. Do not smoke at home or in the car, and do not let guests smoke at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time. The hard part is having to ask family and guests not to smoke in the home.
- In plain language: About 600,000 people a year die worldwide from breathing other people's smoke, and close to 30% of them are children. Long-term exposure to second-hand smoke raises the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke by around 30%. What you take in at home does more harm than what you take in outside.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1.0% of all deaths, and 28% of them were children. Pooling 57 studies: the odds of hypertension among people exposed to second-hand smoke were about 1.28 times those of people unexposed (odds ratio). Heart disease was 1.39 times, heart attack 1.50 times, and stroke 1.36 times. The risk of second-hand smoke at home is higher than outside the home
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet: 「603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men」, 「61% of DALYs were in children」. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8> ; (2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research: 57 studies, 「hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)」「Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure」. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Start with the home, because you spend more time there, and pooled studies show that second-hand smoke at home does more harm than second-hand smoke outside. Children carry 61% of the health loss caused by second-hand smoke. For quitting yourself, see item 1 in this section.
### 3. Do not try to quit on willpower alone, get the medicine first: it can more than double the success rate
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Nicotine patches and chewing gum are over-the-counter, and you can buy them at a pharmacy counter. One course runs 8 to 12 weeks and costs a few hundred yuan to a little over a thousand. Varenicline and bupropion are prescription drugs, so you need a smoking cessation clinic or a respiratory medicine department to prescribe them. The money roughly cancels out against the cigarettes you stop buying over the same period.
- In plain language: Most people cannot quit on willpower alone. People who take varenicline succeed at more than twice the rate of people who take a placebo. People who use a substitute such as a nicotine patch or chewing gum succeed about 50% more often than people on no drug at all. Using a patch and chewing gum together adds another 20% or so.
- Benefit: Pooling 41 randomised trials and 17,395 people, the quitting success rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51, which is the credible range; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline also beat bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and a single kind of nicotine substitute (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). For nicotine substitutes against no drug: 133 trials and 64,640 people, the success rate was about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). A patch plus one fast-acting form (chewing gum or lozenge) beat a single form by about 27% (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37, 16 trials and 12,169 people, high-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8> ; Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Nicotine replacement therapy versus control for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD000146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000146.pub5> ; Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2> ; Shanghai Municipal Health Commission (上海市卫生健康委员会) (2021). Choosing the right drug makes quitting easier, 选对药物，让戒烟轻松一点: 「市场上可见的戒烟药物主要有三种，即尼古丁替代疗法药物、安非他酮、伐尼克兰」 — the smoking cessation drugs on sale come in three kinds, namely nicotine replacement therapy drugs, bupropion and varenicline. 「尼古丁替代疗法药物属于非处方药（OTC），可通过药店柜台购买；而安非他酮、伐尼克兰属于处方药，须到医院戒烟门诊或呼吸内科就诊，凭医师处方经药师调配后才能得到」 — nicotine replacement therapy drugs are over-the-counter and can be bought at a pharmacy counter, while bupropion and varenicline are prescription drugs, which you can only get by visiting a smoking cessation clinic or a respiratory medicine department at a hospital, on a doctor's prescription, dispensed by a pharmacist. <https://wsjkw.sh.gov.cn/jtyx/20211119/df50681e01ba49f896d54f771d8176ae.html>
- Notes: The cessation drugs sold in China are the three kinds above, and only the nicotine substitutes are over-the-counter. The common reactions to varenicline are nausea, vivid dreams and poor sleep, so tell your doctor if you have a psychiatric history. A course of nicotine replacement usually runs 8 to 12 weeks, so do not stop suddenly on your own; taper as the doctor says. The medicine only handles the discomfort of the first few weeks of withdrawal. It does not handle the situations where you want to smoke, so use it together with item 4 in this section (set a quit date) and item 5 in this section (go to a smoking cessation clinic).
### 4. Set a quit date and stop in one go on that day, do not taper first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You pick one day on the calendar and tell your family and colleagues in advance.
- In plain language: Many people plan to cut a few cigarettes a day first and taper down to zero. But by the sixth month, 22% of the people who set a date and stopped in one go had not smoked again, against only 15.5% of those who tapered. So set a date and stop in one go on that day.
- Benefit: 697 British smokers were randomly assigned to two groups. One group stopped in one go on the quit date; the other cut their smoking by 75% in the two weeks before the quit date. Both groups had behavioural support from a nurse and used nicotine replacement around the quit date. At week 4, 49.0% of the group that stopped in one go had not relapsed (95% CI 43.8–54.2, which is the credible range), against 39.2% in the tapering group (34.0–44.4), a relative risk of 0.80 (0.66–0.93). At month 6, 22.0% of the one-go group (18.0–26.6) against 15.5% of the tapering group (12.0–19.7), a relative risk of 0.71 (0.46–0.91). Among people who already preferred to taper, the success rate at week 4 was also lower than among those who preferred to stop in one go (38.3% against 52.2%).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805> ; Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308 (the passage on starting nicotine replacement early). <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes:Disputed. Cochrane used to have a review that compared these two methods head to head, and it concluded that they made little difference (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.13, 10 trials and 3,760 people), but that review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best evidence available now is the randomised trial above, and its direction supports stopping in one go. In that trial both groups also started nicotine replacement before the quit date, and that practice has its own evidence: starting early gives a success rate about 25% higher than starting on the quit date itself (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44, 9 trials and 4,395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). A sensible plan is therefore to set the date first, start the patch two weeks ahead, and stop in one go on the day.

### 5. Go to a smoking cessation clinic, or call 12320 to ask whether there is a cessation service near you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The registration fee runs from a few yuan to a few tens of yuan. Behavioural support is usually 4 to 8 sessions, each half an hour to an hour, totalling 5 hours or less. The hotline is free.
- In plain language: The medicine and having someone check in with you regularly are two separate things, and together they work best. People who take the medicine and also have a dedicated person following them up succeed at close to twice the rate of people who hear only one brief piece of advice. Telephone support counts too: a cessation line that calls you back without being asked raises the success rate by another 20% to 30%.
- Benefit: Pooling 52 trials and 19,488 people, the quitting success rate in the drug plus behavioural support group was 1.83 times that of usual care or brief advice (RR 1.83, 95% CI 1.68–1.98, which is the credible range; high-quality evidence). In the 43 trials that recruited in healthcare settings it was 1.97 times (1.79–2.18). Telephone counselling counted on its own: among people who had called a cessation line themselves, those who received several unprompted callbacks succeeded about 38% more often than those who only got materials or a single session (RR 1.38, 95% CI 1.19–1.61, 14 trials and 32,484 people); among people who had not called a line themselves and were offered telephone counselling by the researchers, about 25% more (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.15–1.35, 65 trials and 41,233 people). Both are moderate-certainty evidence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Stead LF, Koilpillai P, Fanshawe TR, Lancaster T (2016). Combined pharmacotherapy and behavioural interventions for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 3, CD008286. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008286.pub3> ; Matkin W, Ordóñez-Mena JM, Hartmann-Boyce J (2019). Telephone counselling for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD002850. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002850.pub4> ; China CDC (中国疾病预防控制中心) (2021). The 'China Smoking Cessation Platform' WeChat mini programme is officially live: 「在健康中国行动控烟行动工作组指导下中国疾病预防控制中心和世界卫生组织驻华代表处联合制作了中国权威戒烟资源库『中国戒烟平台』微信小程序。小程序内容主要包括：戒烟热线、戒烟门诊详细信息，以及线上戒烟服务资源等」 — under the guidance of the tobacco control working group of the Healthy China Action, the China CDC and the WHO Representative Office in China jointly produced the 'China Smoking Cessation Platform' WeChat mini programme, an authoritative library of smoking cessation resources for China. The mini programme mainly carries quitlines, details of cessation clinics, and online cessation services. The footer of the same page gives 「健康咨询电话：12320」 — health consultation number: 12320. <https://www.chinacdc.cn/jkyj/yckz/gzdt/202203/t20220310_296389.html>
- Notes: How to find one in China: search for the 'China Smoking Cessation Platform' mini programme on WeChat, which lists quitlines and cessation clinics by region. You can also call 12320 and ask whether there is a cessation clinic near you. Most of the trials above were done abroad, and the strength of the service varies a lot between clinics in different places, so read the numbers as direction only. For the medicine to go with it, see item 3 in this section (cessation drugs).
### 6. Consider e-cigarettes only if you have already failed to quit, and people who do not smoke should stay away
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The device and the pods cost from a few tens to a few hundred yuan a month. In China you can only buy tobacco flavours, and only in shops that hold a tobacco monopoly retail licence.
- In plain language: People who switch from cigarettes to nicotine e-cigarettes succeed about 60% more often than people who use nicotine patches, and that finding is reliable. But e-cigarettes are not harmless, and there is still no long-term safety data. For people who do not smoke, they bring no benefit at all.
- Benefit: A continuously updated review included 80 randomised trials and 29,861 people. Compared with nicotine replacement therapy, nicotine e-cigarettes raised the quitting success rate by about 61% (RR 1.61, 95% CI 1.23–2.12, which is the credible range; high-certainty evidence, 11 trials and 4,114 people). In absolute terms, that is about 4 more people in every 100 who quit (1 to 7 more). The rate of serious adverse events was about the same in both groups (risk difference 0.01, 95% CI −0.01 to 0.02, moderate-certainty evidence). Compared with behavioural support alone or no support, about 75% higher (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.39–2.20, low-certainty evidence).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lindson N, Livingstone-Banks J, Butler AR, et al. (2026). Electronic cigarettes for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 8, CD010216. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub11> ; State Tobacco Monopoly Administration (国家烟草专卖局) (2022). Measures for the Administration of E-Cigarettes, 电子烟管理办法（国家烟草专卖局公告 2022 年第 1 号，2022 年 5 月 1 日施行）. 第十八条: anyone doing e-cigarette retail must apply to the tobacco monopoly administration for a tobacco monopoly retail licence. 第二十二条: 「禁止向未成年人出售电子烟产品。」 — selling e-cigarette products to minors is prohibited. 第二十三条第二款: 「任何个人、法人或者其他组织不得通过本办法规定的电子烟交易管理平台以外的信息网络销售电子烟产品、雾化物和电子烟用烟碱等。」 — no individual, legal person or other organisation may sell e-cigarette products, e-liquids or nicotine for e-cigarettes over any information network other than the e-cigarette trading platform these Measures provide for. 第二十六条: 「禁止销售除烟草口味外的调味电子烟和可自行添加雾化物的电子烟。」 — the sale of flavoured e-cigarettes other than in the tobacco flavour, and of e-cigarettes you can add e-liquid to yourself, is prohibited. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5697988.htm>
- Notes:Disputed. The World Health Organization does not recommend e-cigarettes as a smoking cessation method. The reason is that long-term safety data is insufficient, and they get young people started on nicotine. Britain has put them into its cessation services instead. China does not ban e-cigarette sales outright, but it regulates them tightly, and the rules are written into the Measures for the Administration of E-Cigarettes. Only tobacco flavour may be sold; fruit flavours and e-cigarettes you can add e-liquid to yourself may not. You can only buy in a physical shop that holds a tobacco monopoly retail licence. Selling on any other website, online shop or social app outside the national e-cigarette trading platform is illegal, and selling to minors is prohibited. So any fruit-flavoured e-cigarette still on sale is coming through an illegal channel and sits outside the regulatory system. That is how the 'heady' e-cigarettes spiked with synthetic cannabinoids got out, see item 4 in section 22 (do not take a pod someone hands you). The products tested in the review are all regulated nicotine products; e-liquid of unknown origin is not covered. In order, try item 3 in this section (cessation drugs) first, because the evidence there is harder and it is cheaper.
### 7. Stop drinking sugary drinks, and switching to sugar-free ones is not a solution
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea even saves money. The hard part is dropping the habit of drinking sweet drinks, sugar-free ones included.
- In plain language: People who drink more than two bottles of sweet drinks a day have about 20% higher death rates in the same period than people who hardly drink them. One to two bottles a day is about 10% higher. Switching to sugar-free did not show a benefit either: people who drink more than two cups of artificially sweetened drinks a day are instead about a quarter higher.
- Benefit: The United States has two large studies that followed a group of people. Both only followed records, with no control group, and together cover about 118,000 people and 36,000 deaths. People drinking more than 2 servings a day had a death risk about 21% higher than people drinking less than 1 serving a month (HR 1.21). People drinking 1–2 servings a day were about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC cohort also only followed records, with no control group, and covers 450,000 people across 10 countries. Comparing people who drink more than 2 cups a day with people who drink less than 1 cup a month: for sugar-sweetened soft drinks the risk was about 8% higher (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16). For artificially sweetened drinks, the sugar-free versions, about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35). For all soft drinks taken together, about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Malik VS et al. (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401> ; Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes:Disputed, and the dispute is about sugar-free drinks. The association with artificially sweetened drinks is stronger than the association with sugary ones, and the cause is probably running the other way: people switch to the sugar-free versions only after they are already overweight or have diabetes. So do not conclude from this that sweeteners are more toxic. It does at least show that the claim 'switching to sugar-free means you are fine' has no evidence behind it. Switching to plain water or unsweetened tea is the safest option. Also, these studies only followed records and did not run a controlled trial, and people who drink a lot of sweet drinks already have worse lifestyles, so the numbers may be too high.
### 8. Do not chew betel quid
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time. You also save the money you spent on betel quid. The hard part is giving up the habit of chewing.
- In plain language: People who chew betel quid have about 20% higher death rates in the same period than people who do not. The risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is nearly 50% higher. The risk of oral cancer and oesophageal cancer has to be counted separately.
- Benefit: Pooling 17 Asian studies and 388,000 people. People who chew betel quid have a death risk about 21% higher than people who do not (relative risk 1.21, P=0.02). The mortality figure covers 179,600 of them. The risk of diabetes is about 47% higher (1.47). The risk of metabolic syndrome is about 51% higher (1.51). Betel quid is itself a known risk factor for oral cancer and oesophageal cancer
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Notes: The longer you have chewed and the more you chew each day, the higher the risk of oral cancer. After you quit, the risk falls over time. Betel quid containing tobacco is more dangerous
### 9. Replace the salt in your home with low-sodium salt (potassium salt)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bag costs a few yuan more than ordinary salt. Switch when you next buy salt and it takes no extra time. The taste barely changes.
- In plain language: For people who have had a stroke, or who are over 60 and have high blood pressure, replacing the salt at home with low-sodium salt lowers the chance of dying within five years by about 12% and the chance of stroke by about 14%.
- Benefit: A randomised trial done in rural China assigned 20,995 people to two groups, all of them people who had had a stroke or hypertensive patients over 60, and followed them for 4.74 years. Result: the group using low-sodium salt had a death risk about 12% lower than the group using ordinary salt (RR 0.88). Stroke was about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events were about 13% lower (RR 0.87). For events of high blood potassium, the two groups showed no statistically significant difference.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Neal B et al. (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675> ; O'Donnell M et al. (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889> (the PURE side of the dispute)
- Notes:Disputed. The PURE study only followed records over the long term, with no control group. It reports that people excreting less than 3 g of sodium a day have about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events, and people excreting more than 7 g a day about 15% higher. On its results, both too little and too much are bad, and the middle is best. But in the Chinese rural randomised trial cited in this item, low-sodium salt only replaced part of the salt, so sodium intake did not come down that far. Note also that the trial subjects were high-risk older people. Healthy young people get much less from switching salt. People with impaired kidney function, or people taking potassium-sparing drugs, should ask a doctor before switching.

### 10. Brush your teeth properly, clean between the teeth once a day, and replace missing teeth promptly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Dental floss or interdental brushes cost a few tens of yuan a year, and a professional cleaning costs a hundred or two hundred yuan each time. It adds two or three minutes a day. The hard part is making interdental cleaning a daily habit.
- In plain language: People who use floss or interdental brushes every day have about 10% lower death rates in the same period. Older people who have lost all their teeth have about 90% higher death rates. People left with fewer than 20 teeth are about double.
- Benefit: One study in Japan covered 9,676 people and followed them for 6 years. People who used interdental cleaning tools had a death risk about 11% lower (risk ratio 0.89). People who used tongue-cleaning tools had one about 23% lower (0.77). A separate pooling of several studies looked at older people living in the community. People with not one tooth left had about 1.87 times the chance of dying (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.35–2.59, which is the credible range). People with fewer than 20 teeth had about 2.04 times (1.67–2.49)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789> ; Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Notes:Disputed. These studies only followed records. People with bad teeth often have worse health and worse finances across the board. Whether bad teeth drag the body down, or poor health drags the teeth down, is not clear. But this costs almost nothing, and periodontal disease and missing teeth affect eating on their own
### 11. Walk 7000–8000 steps a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Walking for 60–90 minutes a day can be split into the commute and the shopping trip. The hard part is doing it every day.
- In plain language: People who walk 5800 steps a day have about 40% lower death rates in the same period than people who walk only 3500. At 7800 steps the fall is about 45%. Past that the figure drops a little further, then flattens out. For people over 60, 6000–8000 steps is enough.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 studies that followed a group of people, all of them only followed records with no control group. The 15 studies cover 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths. People are split into four bands by daily steps, and the median of the lowest band is 3553 steps a day. The medians of the other three bands are 5801, 7842 and 10,901 steps a day. The death risk of those three bands is about 40%, 45% and 53% lower than the lowest band (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). Among people over 60, the benefit flattens out after 6000–8000 steps a day. Among people under 60 it flattens out after 8000–10000 steps. A separate pooled analysis of several studies: the effect starts at about 3867 steps a day. After that, every extra 1000 steps a day cuts the death risk by about 15%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Paluch AE et al. (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9> ; Banach M et al. (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>
- Notes: The stretch from 4000 steps to 7000 steps is the best value, and you do not have to reach ten thousand. Note that these studies only followed records: the people who walked least include some who were too ill to walk at all. They walked little because they were about to die, not the other way round. So the gap these studies report is often too large. The direction is clear all the same. This item and item 14 (150–300 minutes of moderate exercise a week) are the same thing measured two ways, and hitting either one is enough.
### 12. If you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids, take the medicine as prescribed and do not stop on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Blood pressure pills and statins bought through the volume-based procurement scheme cost from a few yuan to a few tens of yuan a month. One dose a day takes very little time. The hard part is carrying on with them when you feel fine and think you no longer need them.
- In plain language: For every 10 mmHg that blood pressure falls, the chance of dying falls about 13% and the chance of stroke falls about 27%. For every unit that statins take off LDL cholesterol, the chance of dying falls about 10%. People who take their medicine on schedule have a death rate 30% to 45% lower than people who often miss doses.
- Benefit: Pooling several randomised trials: for every 10 mmHg that systolic blood pressure falls, the death risk is about 13% lower (RR 0.87). Systolic blood pressure is the higher of the two numbers in a reading. Major cardiovascular events are about 20% lower (RR 0.80). Stroke is about 27% lower (RR 0.73). Heart failure is about 28% lower (RR 0.72). Statins bring LDL cholesterol down. For every 1.0 mmol/L that LDL falls, the death risk is about 10% lower (RR 0.90). For the same fall, major vascular events are about 22% lower (RR 0.78). A separate pooling of several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: people who take at least 80% of their doses as prescribed have a lower death risk than people who often miss doses. For statins the figure is about 45% lower (RR 0.55). For blood pressure pills it is about 29% lower (RR 0.71).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ettehad D et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5> ; Chowdhury R et al. (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Notes: Only do this if the doctor has judged that you need the medicine. Healthy people do not. The figures for the group that keeps taking the medicine come from studies that only followed records, and people who manage to keep taking it are more disciplined to begin with, so the benefit is overstated. The figures from the two randomised trial pools above are more reliable. This section has no separate item on blood glucose: the evidence that pushing glucose lower lowers all-cause mortality is not as consistent as it is for blood pressure and blood lipids.
### 13. Sleep about 7 hours a night and keep a fixed routine
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You have to set aside the time to sleep, and for most people that means giving up some phone time. The hard part is fixing your bedtime and your wake-up time.
- In plain language: People who sleep under 7 hours over the long run have about 10% higher death rates in the same period. The more irregular the routine, the higher the risk. The most regular sleepers have death rates 20% to nearly 50% lower than the least regular. How regular your routine is therefore says more about your death risk than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Pooling 16 follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group, covering 1,380,000 people and 113,000 deaths. Short sleepers have a death risk about 12% higher (RR 1.12). Long sleepers about 30% higher (RR 1.30). A separate summary bands people by how long they sleep, and the 7-hour band has the lowest risk. Below 7 hours, every 1 hour less raises the risk about 6% (RR 1.06). Above 7 hours, every 1 hour more raises it about 13% (RR 1.13). UK Biobank has wristband records for about 61,000 people, again only followed records with no control group. People are split into five bands by how regular their routine is. The four more regular bands have a death risk 20%–48% lower than the least regular band. How regular the routine is predicts death better than how long you sleep. A separate UK Biobank study measured bedtimes with wristbands in 51,562 people, and 3,853 of them developed cardiovascular disease during the study. Social jet lag is the gap between the midpoint of sleep on a weekday and the midpoint at the weekend. People with a social jet lag of 2 hours or more have a cardiovascular risk about 30% higher (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). The same association holds among people whose sleep duration is normal.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP et al. (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585> ; Yin J et al. (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947> ; Windred DP et al. (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253> ; Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: What needs changing is sleeping too little and having an irregular routine. There is no need to deliberately cut your sleep short. Long sleepers having a higher risk is most likely the cause running the other way: depression, chronic illness and sleep apnoea make people sleep more. Regularity of routine for now rests on two follow-up studies, both only followed records with no control group, so on its own it is grade B evidence. How to make up for a night up late is in item 39 in this section (sleep earlier the next night).
### 14. Build up 150–300 minutes of moderate exercise a week, and brisk walking counts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is keeping it up over the long run.
- In plain language: People who brisk-walk for 150–300 minutes a week have about 30% lower death rates in the same period than people who do not exercise. Even if you fall short of the target, the fall is about 20%. Three to five times the target brings the figure down to about 40% lower. Going further will not lower it more, but it does no harm.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: people whose activity reaches 1 to 2 times the guideline minimum have a death risk about 31% lower than people who do not exercise (HR 0.69). That amount is 7.5–15 MET·h a week, which is about 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week. People who fall short of the minimum are still about 20% lower (HR 0.80). People who reach 3 to 5 times the minimum are about 39% lower (HR 0.61), and that is where the reduction tops out. Going further will not lower it more, but it does no harm (above 10 times, HR 0.69). A separate pooled study using accelerometers: the most active quarter of people by moderate-to-vigorous activity have a death risk about 48% lower than the least active quarter (HR 0.52). An accelerometer is a device you wear that measures activity directly.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Arem H et al. (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533> ; Ekelund U et al. (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: This item and item 11 (7000–8000 steps a day) only need one of the two. Note that these studies only followed records. The ones that measured activity with a device tracked people for a short time and the participants were mostly older, so the figures include an element of "became inactive after falling ill". The real benefit is not as large as a risk ratio of 0.52.

### 15. Play a racket sport three times a week, 45 minutes each time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Court hire costs a few tens of yuan each time. About 2 hours a week. The hard part is getting enough people and a court together, and turning up every week.
- In plain language: People who regularly play tennis, badminton or table tennis have about half the death rate in the same period as people who do not, and cardiovascular death is about 56% lower. Swimmers are about 28% lower. Running and football showed no difference in this dataset.
- Benefit: A UK follow-up study of 80,300 people compared people who did a given sport with people who did not. People who played a racket sport had a death risk about 47% lower (risk ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.40–0.69, credible range). Cardiovascular death was about 56% lower (0.44, 0.24–0.83). Racket sports means tennis, badminton and table tennis. For swimmers the two figures are 0.72 and 0.59, for aerobics 0.73 and 0.64, and for cycling 0.85. Running and football showed no statistically significant difference
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Oja P, Kelly P, Pedisic Z, et al. (2017). Associations of specific types of sports and exercise with all-cause and cardiovascular-disease mortality: a cohort study of 80 306 British adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine, 51(10), 812-817. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096822>
- Notes:Disputed. These studies only followed records. People who take up a sport are healthier to begin with and have more social contact. The fact that running showed no difference also suggests that what matters may be which kind of person picks which sport. Do not read this item as saying running is useless, because this section has other items that go by total activity.
### 16. Add up the short bursts of effort, such as stairs and walking fast, to four or five minutes a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and no need to carve out extra exercise time. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and to walk faster instead of taking the easy way.
- In plain language: People who do no exercise at all still have a death rate about 40% lower in the same period than people who do none at all, as long as they have three one- or two-minute bursts of effort a day. Cardiovascular death is nearly half lower. Effort here means things like taking the stairs and hurrying to get somewhere.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study of 25,200 people who did no deliberate exercise in ordinary life. The average follow-up was 6.9 years, and 852 of them died. Some people had 3 bouts a day of short vigorous activity lasting 1–2 minutes each. Compared with people who had none, their death risk and cancer death risk were 38%–40% lower. Cardiovascular death risk was 48%–49% lower. People who totalled 4.4 minutes a day of this activity had a death risk and cancer death risk 26%–30% lower. Cardiovascular death risk was 32%–34% lower
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes:Disputed. This study measured activity with wearable devices, which is more reliable than asking people to fill in a questionnaire from memory. But it still only followed records, it did not run a controlled trial, and it tracked people for 6.9 years. The subjects were people who did no exercise at all, so this item does not apply to people who already exercise regularly
### 17. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and press-ups cost nothing. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each time. The hard part is fitting it into the week's schedule.
- In plain language: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week have a death rate 10% to 20% lower in the same period than people who do none. Doing more does not make it better. Strength work and aerobic work together work best.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: people who do strength training have a death risk 10%–17% lower than people who do not. More is not better: the fall is largest at about 30–60 minutes a week, about 10%–20%, and training beyond that adds nothing. People who do both strength and aerobic work have a lower death risk than people who do neither.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Momma H et al. (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: These studies only followed records, and the activity level was self-reported. The evidence that training too much makes matters worse is very weak, so there is no reason to hold back on that account. Strength training also helps older people avoid falls and keep their muscle, and those points are in section 1.
### 18. Do not sit for long stretches, get up and move from time to time
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and getting up a few times takes very little time. The hard part is that once you sit down you lose track of time, so set a reminder.
- In plain language: The people who sit longest have a death rate in the same period 2.6 times that of the people who sit least. Sitting for a long unbroken stretch carries a separate risk on top of that. But as long as you get 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day, most of the extra risk from sitting is cancelled out.
- Benefit: A US follow-up study that only followed records, with no control group, measured activity with accelerometers. People were split into four groups by total daily sitting time. The group that sat the most had a death risk 2.63 times that of the group that sat least (HR 2.63). People were then split into four groups by how long they sat in one unbroken stretch: the group with the longest unbroken sitting had a figure 1.96 times that of the group with the shortest (HR 1.96). So total sitting time and the length of a single unbroken stretch are each separately associated with the risk. A separate pooled analysis covering more than a million people, only followed records with no control group: people who sit more than 8 hours a day and barely exercise have a death risk 1.59 times that of another group (HR 1.59). That other group sits under 4 hours a day and is the most active. About 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day cancels out the extra risk from sitting. The most active group has an HR of only 1.04 even when sitting more than 8 hours a day, which is not statistically significant.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Diaz KM et al. (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212> ; Ekelund U et al. (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable conclusion is that how much you move matters more than how long you sit, and that when activity is enough the risk from sitting largely disappears. Note that these studies only followed records, and the people who sit the most include a large number who were already ill, so a risk as high as 2.63 times clearly contains an element of "became sedentary after falling ill". In the same pooled analysis, the risk of watching more than 3 hours of television a day is present at every activity level, and the most active group only pushes that starting point out to 5 hours.
### 19. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausage, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it may even save money, and it takes no time. The hard part is cutting back on the mouthful you actually like.
- In plain language: People who eat the most ham, bacon and sausage have a death rate about 20% higher in the same period than people who eat the least. Every extra daily serving of processed meat adds 20% to the figure.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: the group that eats the most processed meat has a death risk about 23% higher than the group that eats the least (RR 1.23). Red meat of all kinds is about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat is about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but that one is not statistically significant. A separate pooled analysis works from the amount eaten per day. Every extra daily serving of processed meat, so 1 more serving a day, raises the death risk about 23% (RR 1.23). Every extra daily serving of red meat, 1 more serving a day, raises it about 10% (RR 1.10).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261> ; Schwingshackl L et al. (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148> ; Johnston BC et al. (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621> (the NutriRECS guidelines, the other side of the dispute)
- Notes:Disputed. The NutriRECS guidelines use a standard evidence grading system (GRADE) and rate these studies as not very reliable. They give one very weak recommendation: carry on eating the way you do now. The two sides disagree about whether the evidence is hard enough, not about the direction. No study says processed meat is good for you. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect and shows no statistically significant difference. What needs controlling is the processed meat.

### 20. Drink less alcohol, or none
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it saves money, and it takes no time. The hard part is the awkward moment when people at a gathering push a drink on you.
- In plain language: Past 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink the earlier you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. People drinking 100–200 g a week at 40 live about half a year less; people drinking 200–350 g lose one to two years; people drinking more than 350 g lose four or five years. Judged by overall health, the best value is not drinking.
- Benefit: Pooling 83 follow-up studies, only followed records with no control group, covering 600,000 people who drink alcohol. The lowest death risk is at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol a week. That amount is about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40-proof baijiu. People drinking 100–200 g a week at 40 have a life expectancy about 6 months shorter. People drinking 200–350 g a week lose 1–2 years. People drinking more than 350 g lose 4–5 years. The source reports all three in years of life. The Global Burden of Disease study GBD 2016 adds up all kinds of health loss and puts the lowest-risk amount at 0 drinks a week. A separate pooled analysis corrected for bias in study design. People drinking 1.3–24 g a day have a death risk about 7% lower than people who never drink (RR 0.93), but that one is not statistically significant. People drinking 45–64 g a day are about 19% higher (RR 1.19). People drinking above 65 g a day are about 35% higher (RR 1.35).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wood AM et al. (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X> ; GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2> ; Zhao J et al. (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185> ; Di Castelnuovo A et al. (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437> (the other side of the dispute)
- Notes:Disputed. The side that argues drinking less is good points to a pooled analysis of 34 long-term follow-up studies. It reports that light drinkers have an all-cause mortality up to 17%–18% lower. The amount it gives is no more than 4 drinks a day for men and 2 for women. Within that amount, the more people drink the lower their mortality. The other side argues that the "non-drinkers" group includes people who gave up because of illness and people who were in poor health to begin with. One pooled analysis adjusted specifically for those confounders, and once that was done the protective effect disappeared. The Global Burden of Disease study also puts 0 as the safest. The safe way to read it: drinking less will not clearly shorten your life, and drinking more certainly will. Starting to drink for health reasons has no basis. What to do if you want to drink less is in item 22 in this section (count the drinks first, then see a doctor). People who drink every day should not stop abruptly on their own, see item 21 in this section (do not stop abruptly on your own).
### 21. If you drink every day and shake or feel anxious when you stop, do not stop abruptly on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The registration fee for psychiatry or an addiction medicine clinic runs from a few yuan to a few tens of yuan. Severe cases need a few days in hospital.
- In plain language: Someone who drinks a lot every day and stops suddenly goes into withdrawal. Mild withdrawal means shaking, sweating, anxiety and sleeplessness. Severe withdrawal means seizures or delirium tremens, which is a medical emergency that kills. Norway followed more than thirty thousand people, and of those who had delirium tremens, 8% died each year afterwards. If you want to quit, go to hospital first and say how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states that people who drink heavily over the long run develop withdrawal syndrome after stopping suddenly or cutting down sharply, with tremor, autonomic overactivity, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures and delirium tremens, and that in ordinary internal medicine this is 「常见且可能危及生命」 — common and potentially life-threatening. A Norwegian national register cohort of 36,287 people diagnosed with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state or delirium tremens between 2009 and 2015: the annual mortality in the delirium tremens group was 8.0%, in the withdrawal state group 5.0%, and in the alcohol dependence group 3.6%. The standardised mortality ratio for the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, the credible range), which means the death risk is about 9.8 times that of the general population of the same age.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103> ; Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to judge it yourself: if you drink almost every day, and missing a day brings shaking, sweating, anxiety and sleeplessness, and you need a drink in the morning to feel right, then any one of those means you should not stop on your own. What hospitals normally do is give benzodiazepines to get you through those few days and add thiamine (vitamin B1) at the same time. The grade is B because the Norwegian cohort says what happened later to people who had delirium tremens, not what happens to people who stop abruptly on their own, and getting from one to the other is an inference. You still need to quit, but quit in hospital instead. How much a week counts as too much is in item 20 in this section (drink less or none), and what to do if you want to drink less is in item 22 in this section (count the drinks first, then see a doctor).
### 22. To drink less, first count how much you drink in a week, then talk to a doctor for a few minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Counting the drinks takes one line a day. Asking the question while you are at the clinic costs nothing extra.
- In plain language: A doctor who spends a few minutes looking at how much you drink, explaining the risk and setting you a target leaves you drinking 20 grams of pure alcohol less a week on average a year later, which is about half a litre of beer. Talking for longer brings no further benefit. For people who cannot stop on their own, two drugs proved effective abroad, and for every 12 people treated about 1 more does not go back to drinking as much as before.
- Benefit: Pooling 34 randomised trials and 15,197 people, people who received a brief intervention drank 20 grams of pure alcohol less per week a year later than people who received only the minimum intervention or none (95% CI 12 to 28 grams less, the credible range; moderate-quality evidence). The baseline drinking of the participants in these studies averaged 244 grams a week. A brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counselling of no more than 5 sessions and under 60 minutes in total; talking for longer brought no extra benefit. For the drugs: pooling 122 randomised trials and 22,803 people, acamprosate means that for every 12 people treated (95% CI 8 to 26) about 1 more does not return to drinking at all. Oral naltrexone at 50 milligrams a day means that for every 12 people treated (8 to 26) about 1 more does not return to heavy drinking.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4> ; Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit is graded "small" because both of these bodies of evidence measure how much people drink, not mortality, and by the rule of this book a surrogate endpoint alone gets "small". Most of the drug trials were done abroad, most participants already met the diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence, and every trial also gave psychosocial support, so the figures are the extra benefit on top of that. Whether these two drugs are approved in China and whether you can be prescribed them is up to your doctor and the drug's own package information, so do not buy them yourself online. If you already have withdrawal symptoms, see item 21 in this section (do not stop abruptly on your own).
### 23. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: 28 grams a day costs two or three hundred yuan a year. You eat them in passing, and it takes no time.
- In plain language: People who regularly eat nuts have about 20% lower death rates in the same period than people who do not. Eating them once a week already shows a difference, about 10%. Eating a handful a day shows the clearest difference of all.
- Benefit: Two US follow-up studies, 119,000 people, more than 300 ten-thousand person-years. A person-year is the number of people multiplied by the number of years each was followed. Compared with people who ate no nuts: people who ate less than 1 time a week had a death risk about 7% lower (risk ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, the credible range). People eating 1 time a week were about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93). People eating 2–4 times a week were about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90). People eating 5–6 times a week were about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91). People eating 7 or more times a week were about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy them plain, with no added salt. Nuts are not low in calories, so do not treat them as a snack to eat freely. Note that these studies only followed a group of people over the long term, and nut eaters have better lifestyles across the board, so the figures need discounting.
### 24. Replace some of your red meat with fish and poultry
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time. This is swapping one kind of meat for another, not buying more. The hard part is shifting what you prefer.
- In plain language: Eating two more servings a week of processed meat or red meat raises the death rate in the same period by about 3%. Swapping the same number of servings for fish or poultry shows no rise. The effect in this section is small, which makes this an in-passing swap rather than something to fight over.
- Benefit: Pooling 6 US follow-up studies and close to 30,000 people. Two extra servings of processed meat a week raise the death risk about 3% (risk ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05, credible range). Two extra servings of unprocessed red meat a week also raise it about 3% (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same amount eaten as poultry gives 0.99 (0.97–1.02), and as fish 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither is statistically significant
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: Two servings a week makes very little difference, so do not expect to live longer by swapping meat. When you are spending the same money on meat, you can favour fish and poultry

### 25. Replace some of your white rice and white flour with whole grains
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brown rice, oats and wholemeal bread cost a little more than white rice and white flour, and take no time. The hard part is getting used to the taste.
- In plain language: People who eat 90 g more whole grains a day have about 17% lower death rates in the same period. 90 g is about 3 servings. At about 200 g a day the figure is still falling.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: every 90 g more whole grains a day lowers the death risk about 17% (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 servings. The benefit continues to 210–225 g a day. In a separate pooled analysis, every 1 extra serving a day lowers it about 8% (RR 0.92).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D et al. (2016). Whole grain consumption and risk of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and all cause and cause specific mortality: systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.i2716> ; Schwingshackl L et al. (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>
- Notes: You do not have to switch everything to whole grains, because half of it already puts you in the stretch where the benefit is most visible. Note that these studies only followed records, and people who eat whole grains were healthier to begin with, so the figures are too large. Results also differ a lot between studies (I² 83%, and the higher that number, the less the studies agree).
### 26. Drink tea three or more times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few tens to a few hundred yuan a year. Making a cup takes a few minutes and takes very little time.
- In plain language: People with a habit of drinking tea have about 15% lower death rates in the same period. Measured from 50, they live 1.26 years longer on average, and they spend 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: A Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR), half of whom were followed for the full 7.3 years. People with a habit of drinking tea had a death risk about 15% lower (risk ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90, the credible range). Measured from age 50: people with a habit of drinking tea spend 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and their life expectancy is 1.26 years longer
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685> ; mycotoxins in tea: Cui P et al. (2020). Quantitative analysis and dietary risk assessment of aflatoxins in Chinese post-fermented dark tea. Food and Chemical Toxicology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2020.111830> ; Zhou H et al. (2022). Mycotoxins in Tea (Camellia sinensis (L.) Kuntze): Contamination and Dietary Exposure Profiling in the Chinese Population. Toxins. <https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins14070452> ; green tea extract and liver damage: Hu J et al. (2018). The safety of green tea and green tea extract consumption in adults - Results of a systematic review. Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yrtph.2018.03.019>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: these studies only followed records, and Chinese men who drink tea also have a high rate of smoking and drinking. The authors have already subtracted part of that effect, but not all of it. One more thing: do not drink it scalding hot, and for the temperature of hot drinks see the item in this section on that. Some people worry about mycotoxins in tea. In China, 158 samples of dark tea have been tested and 2 of them came back with aflatoxin, and worked out against the amount of tea people drink, the intake is below the internationally acceptable level. Another study tested 352 samples of tea for 16 mycotoxins, and only ochratoxin A in dark tea came out above the limit on average. Worked out against how much tea people in China drink, neither study found a dietary risk. Keep tea somewhere dry, and do not drink it if it has got damp and gone mouldy. Green tea extract capsules are a different thing: a large dose of concentrated catechins swallowed at once can damage the liver, and brewed tea does not have that problem. So this is about brewed tea only, and do not substitute a green tea extract supplement for it.
### 27. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, with no sugar and no cream topping
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Brewing it yourself costs a yuan or two a day. Making a cup takes a few minutes and very little of your time.
- In plain language: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day have about 17% lower death rates in the same period than people who do not.
- Benefit: A review pooled the results of 201 summary studies on top of each other, and the original studies underneath those summaries all only followed records with no control group. Result: people drinking 3–4 cups of coffee a day had a death risk 17% lower than people who do not drink coffee (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88, the credible range)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: the original authors state that the evidence comes almost entirely from follow-up records, and they also write that 「需要可靠的随机对照试验才能判断是否为因果」 — reliable randomised controlled trials are needed to tell whether the relationship is causal. In plain terms, people have to be randomly split into two groups and compared, because records alone cannot establish cause. Drink it black, because sugar and a cream topping cancel the benefit out. This does not apply to pregnant women, or to people with arrhythmia, anxiety or insomnia.
### 28. Eat 5 servings of fruit and vegetables a day (about 400 g)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan to a dozen-odd yuan a day. Washing and cutting takes some time each day. The hard part is buying every day and eating enough of it every day.
- In plain language: People who eat 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day have about 10% lower death rates in the same period. People who eat 5 servings a day have about 13% lower rates than people who eat only 2. Eating more than that does not lower it further.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group: every 200 g more fruit and vegetables a day lowers the death risk about 10% (RR 0.90). The benefit continues to 800 g a day. Two large US follow-up studies plus a pooled analysis of 26 studies: people eating 5 servings a day had a death risk about 13% lower than people eating only 2 (HR 0.87). The best combination is 2 servings of fruit and 3 of vegetables, and going beyond that does not lower the risk further.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Aune D et al. (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319> ; Wang DD et al. (2021). Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality: Results From 2 Prospective Cohort Studies of US Men and Women and a Meta-Analysis of 26 Cohort Studies. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.120.048996>
- Notes: 5 servings is enough, there is no need to reach 10, and the two studies agree on that point. Note that these studies only followed records and the confounding is obvious: people who eat more vegetables differ from other people in income, education and exercise. So "about 10% lower risk" is already the ceiling.
### 29. Eat less ultra-processed food (crisps, instant noodles, cakes, instant meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, you only have to shop around. Either you cook yourself, or you spend time picking out food that has not been much processed, and either way it takes time every day. The hard part is fighting the habit that convenience is better.
- In plain language: People who eat the most ultra-processed food have about 20% higher death rates in the same period than people who eat the least, and cardiovascular death is 50% higher. That evidence is graded not very certain, so read the figures with a discount.
- Benefit: A review pooled several meta-analyses on top of each other, and the original studies underneath them all only followed records with no control group. Result: people who eat a lot of ultra-processed food have a death risk about 21% higher than people who eat little of it (RR 1.21), and cardiovascular death about 50% higher (RR 1.50). The evidence grades for these two are "strong indication" and "convincing" respectively. But judged by the standard evidence grading system (GRADE), the certainty is low or very low.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lane MM et al. (2024). Ultra-processed food exposure and adverse health outcomes: umbrella review of epidemiological meta-analyses. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-077310>
- Notes:Disputed on three points. First, this classification (NOVA) lumps together foods that differ a lot in nutrition. Second, it overlaps heavily with sugary drinks and processed meat (items 7 and 19), so it is not possible to tell which of them is doing the work. Third, judged by the standard evidence grading system (GRADE), the grade is low as well. So once you have done those two items, how much extra this one adds is anyone's guess. The other side's evidence is the low rating in that same review, because no original study has yet reached the opposite conclusion. So do items 7 and 19 first (stop drinking sugary drinks, eat less processed meat), then come back to this one.
### 30. Cook and heat with electricity or gas instead of coal and firewood
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A rural household has to replace the stove and add the fuel bill, which comes to a few hundred yuan a year and sometimes over a thousand. It is a one-off change and does not take time in the long run. City dwellers are basically already on electricity or gas.
- In plain language: People who cook with coal or firewood have about 10% higher death rates in the same period; people who burn coal and wood for heating are about 14% higher. People who have already switched to electricity or gas have a death rate 13%–33% lower than those still burning coal and wood.
- Benefit: A Chinese study that followed people with chronic disease over the long term, only followed records with no control group, covering 271,000 adults with no cardiovascular disease. People who used solid fuels such as coal and firewood for cooking had a death risk about 11% higher than people who used electricity or gas (HR 1.11). People who used solid fuels for heating were about 14% higher (HR 1.14). Among people who had already switched from solid fuel to clean fuel, compared with those still burning, cooking was about 13% lower (HR 0.87) and heating about 33% lower (HR 0.67). Outdoor PM2.5 is a separate account. Pooling 104 follow-up studies, every 10 µg/m³ of long-term exposure raises the risk of natural-cause death about 8% (RR 1.08). Natural causes means deaths that were not accidents.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yu K et al. (2018). Association of Solid Fuel Use With Risk of Cardiovascular and All-Cause Mortality in Rural China. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.2151> ; Chen J, Hoek G (2020). Long-term exposure to PM and all-cause and cause-specific mortality: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Environment International. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2020.105974>
- Notes: These studies only followed records. Households that switched fuel are often wealthier as well, so the figure of a risk ratio of 0.67 has the family's income mixed into it. As for outdoor PM2.5, what one person can do is limited, and it comes down to moving house, wearing a mask and using a purifier. There is no research with mortality as the endpoint for air purifiers, so this section gives no figure for them.

### 31. Let hot things cool before you drink them, and skip tea, soup and coffee that are scalding
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, just two or three minutes of waiting each time. The hard part is holding back when you want a drink.
- In plain language: Drinking tea that is very hot gives you 8 times the chance of oesophageal cancer that drinking warm tea does. Even at just "hot" it is 2 times. Drinking it less than two minutes after it is poured carries 5 times the risk of waiting more than four minutes.
- Benefit: In a high-incidence area of oesophageal cancer in northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people without the disease. Everything below is compared with people who drink warm tea. People who drink "hot" tea had about 2.07 times the odds of squamous oesophageal cancer (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.28–3.35, credible range). People who drink "very hot" tea had about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). People who drink it less than 2 minutes after pouring had about 5.41 times the odds of people who wait more than 4 minutes (2.63–11.1)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929> ; Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies hot drinks above 65 ℃ as group 2A, which means probably carcinogenic. The same assessment says coffee itself is not carcinogenic. In China, oesophageal cancer is common in the Chaoshan area of Guangdong and around the Taihang Mountains, and that is linked to the habit of drinking while it is still scalding
### 32. Get out in the daylight, and do not avoid the sun all day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. A dozen minutes a day, on the way to work and at the lunch break, is enough.
- In plain language: Women who deliberately avoid the sun have a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter than the women who get the most sun. People who do not smoke but avoid the sun live about as long as the heaviest smokers in the group that gets the most sun.
- Benefit: A Swedish study of 29,500 women followed for 20 years. Compared with the group that got the most sun, people who deliberately avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter. The authors write that 「不吸烟但回避晒太阳的人，预期寿命与晒太阳最多组里的吸烟者相当」 — people who do not smoke but avoid sun exposure have a life expectancy on a par with the smokers in the group that gets the most sun
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: Sweden lies at a high latitude with little sunshine, so the conclusion cannot be carried straight over to China. And people who avoid the sun may have been frail and housebound to begin with. Also, do not go as far as getting sunburnt. You still need to cover up under the strong midday sun, because sun exposure raises the risk of skin cancer.
### 33. Keep your BMI between 20 and 25, and lose weight if you are overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Diet and exercise both take time every day. This is the item in the whole section that demands the most willpower: controlling what you eat and moving your legs over the long run, and keeping the weight off afterwards is harder than losing it.
- In plain language: The death rate is lowest in the BMI 20–25 band. At a BMI of 27.5–30 it is about 20% higher, at 30–35 about 45% higher, and at 35–40 close to double. East Asian people are more sensitive to excess weight, and every 5 points of BMI raises the risk about 40%.
- Benefit: Individual participant data from 239 follow-up studies pooled together, all only followed records with no control group. The analysis kept only three kinds of people: those who had never smoked, those with no chronic illness when they entered the study, and those who were still alive 5 years later. Result: the death risk is lowest at a BMI of 20–25. At 25–27.5 it is about 7% higher (HR 1.07). At 27.5–30 about 20% higher (HR 1.20). At 30–35 about 45% higher (HR 1.45). At 35–40 about 94% higher (HR 1.94). At 40–60 it is 2.76 times (HR 2.76). Among people above a BMI of 25, every extra 5 kg/m² raises the risk in East Asian populations about 39% (HR 1.39).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1> ; Flegal KM et al. (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905> (the other side of the dispute)
- Notes:Disputed. One side argues that being a little heavier means living longer. Their pooled figures are that overweight people have a death risk about 6% lower, and mildly obese people about 5% lower. The two sides differ mainly over whether to drop three kinds of data: people who smoke, people who are already ill, and the first few years after entry into a study. The reason to drop them is that people who are seriously ill start to lose weight first. The analysis of the 239 studies above dropped those three groups, and the risk from being overweight came back. Note also that no randomised trial has shown that losing weight by itself lowers all-cause mortality. The figures above come from comparing people of different weights side by side, so they do not mean that losing weight lowers your risk by that much. If you want to lose weight there is no need to work on mealtimes, because breakfast and 16:8 intermittent fasting bring no extra benefit, see item 26 in section 6 (do not expect breakfast or 16:8 to help you control your weight)
### 34. Eat chillies four or more times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time.
- In plain language: People who eat chillies four or more times a week have about 23% lower death rates in the same period than people who rarely eat them, and cardiovascular death is about a third lower.
- Benefit: An Italian study of 22,800 people, half of whom were followed for the full 8.2 years, with 1,236 deaths in total. People who ate chillies 4 or more times a week had a death risk about 23% lower than people who rarely ate them (risk ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90, credible range). Cardiovascular death was about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: this is a follow-up record study done in the context of a Mediterranean diet, and people who eat hot food may have an overall diet closer to the local tradition. Note too that people with reflux, haemorrhoids or irritable bowel will find that hot food makes the symptoms worse, so there is no need to force yourself.
### 35. Have one or two servings of milk or yoghurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A few yuan a day, and it takes no time.
- In plain language: People who drink 2 or more servings of milk or yoghurt a day have about 17% lower death rates in the same period than people who do not.
- Benefit: The PURE follow-up study covers 21 countries and 136,000 people, with an average follow-up of 9.1 years and 6,796 deaths. People who ate two or more servings of dairy a day had a death risk about 17% lower than people who ate none (risk ratio 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96, credible range), trend P=0.0052
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: PURE included a large number of middle-income and low-income countries, and in those countries being able to drink milk every day already says the family has some money, and that confounding is hard to remove cleanly. People who cannot digest lactose can switch to yoghurt or low-lactose milk instead, and there is no need to force themselves to drink ordinary milk.
### 36. You do not need to give up eggs, but do not eat three or four a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and it takes no time.
- In plain language: Every extra half a whole egg a day raises the death rate in the same period by about 7%. Swap that half for egg white, fish, poultry or nuts and the death risk for each of those is lower instead. There is no need to give up eggs, only not to eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: A US follow-up study of 521,000 people, with 129,000 deaths. Every extra half a whole egg a day raised the death risk about 7% (risk ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08, the credible range). Swapping that half a whole egg for the same amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy or nuts and beans gives a lower death risk, and the death risk is also lower for cardiovascular disease, cancer and respiratory disease
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Notes:Disputed, and the dispute is a large one: there are also long-term follow-up studies of large groups of people, and pooled analyses of several studies, that found no link between eggs and mortality. The US dietary guidelines have already dropped the daily limit on cholesterol. What needs avoiding is only three or four eggs a day, and one a day is nothing to worry about.
### 37. Take a bath if you can, and do not only use a shower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A bit more on water and electricity. Half an hour each time.
- In plain language: People who bathe almost every day have a cardiovascular event risk about 28% lower than people who bathe fewer than twice a week, and brain haemorrhage about 46% lower.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people followed for 19 years, with 2,097 cardiovascular events. People who bathed almost every day had a cardiovascular event risk about 28% lower than people who bathed no more than twice a week (risk ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84, the credible range). Coronary heart disease was about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke was about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Brain haemorrhage was about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Notes:Disputed, and the reason is this: the result comes from a Japanese population with a bathing culture, and most families in China have no way to bathe every day. Also, do not make the water too hot and do not stay in too long. For older people and people with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, water that is too hot and a bath that is too long are dangerous in themselves, and every year a large number of older people in Japan die suddenly in the bath.

### 38. Keep the nap under half an hour, do not go past an hour, and if you need one or two hours to get through the day, find out why
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Just set an alarm before you lie down, and it takes almost no willpower.
- In plain language: People who nap for no more than an hour have no higher risk of death or chronic illness, and their minds are a little sharper. People who sleep past an hour have a coronary heart disease risk about 30% higher, and diabetes and obesity about 20% higher. So set an alarm and wake yourself at half an hour. If you genuinely need one or two hours of napping to get through the day, that is a signal to have your body checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses covering 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review is a summary of summaries. The conclusion is that people who nap for less than 60 minutes have no higher all-cause mortality or chronic illness risk, and the improvement in thinking is clearest (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00, the credible range). A short nap of 20 to 30 minutes gives the biggest improvement in athletic performance (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). People who nap for more than 60 minutes have a coronary heart disease risk about 30% higher and a diabetes and obesity risk about 20% higher. A separate study had 1,338 people over 56 wear wristbands to measure naps directly instead of filling in a questionnaire. The longest follow-up was 19 years, and 926 people died during it. Every 1 hour more of napping raises the death risk about 13% (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). One extra nap a day adds about 7% (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). People who nap in the morning have a risk about 30% higher than people who nap in the early afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, et al. (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013> ; Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, et al. (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938> ; Dashti HS et al. (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes:Disputed. The two studies above both only followed records, with no control group. People who nap for a long time are more likely to have sleep apnoea, anaemia, an underactive thyroid, depression or some other chronic illness to begin with. So how much of the higher death rate among long sleepers is caused by the nap itself is not clear. Causal inference using genes (Mendelian randomisation, 453,000 plus 541,000 people) supports only one thing: the more often you nap, the slightly higher your blood pressure and waist. It did not find a causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So do not treat "nap less" as a way to lower your death rate. The "find out why" half of this item is the author's advice and is grade C: if you are sleepy in the daytime to the point where you must sleep for a long time, check your night-time sleep first, and check for sleep apnoea if you snore or stop breathing during sleep, then check a full blood count and your thyroid. How to get more out of a short nap is in item 11 in section 3 (sleep 10 minutes when you feel sleepy in the afternoon). How long to sleep at night is in item 13 in this section (about 7 hours a night).
### 39. Make up for a night up late the next night, do not save it up for the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. The hour or two you make up is time you wanted to sleep anyway.
- In plain language: People who did not make up their sleep the next night after a short night have a death risk about 15% higher than people with a regular routine, and those who cut their sleep a lot and did not make it up are about 42% higher. People who did make it up show no rise in risk. So after a night up late, go to bed early the next night.
- Benefit: UK Biobank, 85,618 people, average age 61.8, with wristbands recording sleep day by day and a median follow-up of 8 years. The study first worked out each person's own sleep need, and called a night on which they slept at least 2.5 hours less than that a "short night". The first night after a short night is a "make-up night", and sleeping more than they needed that night counts as having made it up. That gives five patterns. Compared with people who kept a regular routine, people who cut their sleep and did not make it up had an all-cause death risk about 15% higher (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31, the credible range). People who cut their sleep by more than 3.5 hours in total and did not make it up were about 42% higher (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). Neither of the two groups that made it up showed a significant association, and the figure for making up after a short night was HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among people who already slept short, cutting sleep without making it up was about 19% higher (1.19, 1.01–1.40), and cutting it a lot without making it up was about 38% higher (1.38, 1.17–1.63). The same result came out of a separate US health survey of 4,586 people.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, et al. (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This study only followed records, with no control group. People who cannot make up their sleep may have been busier and in worse health to begin with, and that layer cannot be removed cleanly. "Neither of the two groups that made it up showed a rise in risk" only means no difference was found: their point estimates are still above 1, and making it up does not mean nothing happened. So pay back a night up late as soon as you can, and do not use that as a licence to stay up late. The make-up in this study means sleeping a little extra on the first night after a short night, and it does not include paying everything back in one go at the weekend. Turning "stay up late on weekdays, catch up at the weekend" into a weekly habit is called social jet lag, and it is itself linked to cardiovascular disease, see item 13 in this section (about 7 hours a night and a fixed routine). The rule about a fixed wake-up time at the weekend as well is in item 2 in section 3 (fixed wake-up time, at the weekend too), and it does not clash with this item: this one tells you to go to bed early that night, not to sleep until midday the next day.
### 40. The longer you work night shifts, the higher your cardiovascular risk, so change to a day post as early as you can
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Moving post or changing job may mean less money, and you lose the night shift allowance. Job hunting takes time and the decision is hard. If you do this arithmetic while you are choosing a job, the cost is zero.
- In plain language: People who work night shifts have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than people who do not, and about 27% more cardiovascular death. And it grows with the years: every extra 5 years of night shifts adds about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So this is an argument counted in years, and the earlier you change post the better. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much weaker than the stories say.
- Benefit: Pooling 23 follow-up studies, all only followed records with no control group. People who work night shifts have a cardiovascular event risk about 13% higher (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16, the credible range). Cardiovascular death is about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). Counted in years: every extra 5 years of night shifts raises the incidence of cardiovascular disease about 7% more (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09), and cardiovascular death about 5% more (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By disease, the incidence of coronary heart disease is about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28), death from coronary heart disease about 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36), death from ischaemic heart disease about 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84), and death from stroke about 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). The stroke incidence figure is not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, et al. (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1668848> ; Esposito G, Bravi F, Santucci C, et al. (2025). Night shift work and breast cancer risk in healthcare workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Occupational Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqaf040> ; Shen QM, Li ZY, Tan YT, et al. (2026). Night shift work and risk of total and site-specific cancer: results from a prospective cohort study among Chinese men. Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health. <https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4290> ; Czeisler CA, Johnson MP, Duffy JF, et al. (1990). Exposure to bright light and darkness to treat physiologic maladaptation to night work. New England Journal of Medicine, 322(18), 1253-1259. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199005033221801>
- Notes: These are all studies that only followed records, with no control group. People who work night shifts already differ from others in smoking, weight and income, and that cannot be fully adjusted away. On night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much weaker than the stories say. In a pooled analysis of 12 studies and 12,132 cases of breast cancer, people who have worked night shifts have a risk only about 5% higher, and the credible range crosses 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The group with a full 20 years is about 25% higher (1.25, 1.01–1.55), but the authors' sensitivity analysis makes that one not significant, and after correcting for possible publication bias it comes out close to 1, in their words the association is 「远未确立」 — far from established. A separate study that followed Chinese men for 16.1 years and recorded 8,202 cancers found no association between night shifts and total cancer or most common cancers, with pancreatic cancer the only exception at about 59% higher in the group with 11 to 20 cumulative years (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this item does the arithmetic on the cardiovascular side, not on cancer. Not everyone should leave a night shift post, and the calculation in years is one you have to do for yourself. If you are already on nights, change the things you can change: for quitting smoking see item 1 in this section (quit smoking, the earlier the better), for blood pressure and blood lipids see item 12 in this section (take the medicine as prescribed if you have high blood pressure or high blood lipids), and for how to make up the sleep after a night shift see item 39 in this section (make up for a night up late the next night). There are two more countermeasures worth trying: concentrating your eating in the daytime, and moving your body clock across with strong light plus strict darkness during the day. The second is the only one shown to actually move the clock. Under ordinary indoor lighting, six night shifts in a row leave the clock completely unmoved. Switch to strong light of 7000 to 12000 lux plus near-total darkness during the day, and four days will turn it fully around. **The light at night has to be bright enough, the sleep during the day has to be properly dark, and you have to wear sunglasses on the way home to block the morning light. All three are required**, and leaving out the last one makes the effort pointless. Both countermeasures only reach metabolic measures and sleep; no trial has tested whether doing them lowers your cardiovascular risk. Spending your days off living a normal daytime routine pushes the clock back. For the doses, the original figures and the rest of the sources, and for how the body tells the time, see [docs/生物钟和夜班.md](../docs/生物钟和夜班.md).

### 41. Buy prepackaged cooking oil with an SC number, not loose oil pressed at home in a small workshop
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The same money, just a different shop. All you have to do is check whether the package carries a food production licence number beginning with SC. The hard part is persuading older relatives who are used to buying home-pressed oil.
- In plain language: Peanuts that go damp and mouldy grow aflatoxin, which is a proven carcinogen and does most of its damage to the liver. After Guangzhou brought small workshops under control, the share of residents who often eat home-pressed oil with abnormal liver function fell by about 35%. In Guangxi, pregnant women who ate home-pressed peanut oil had a risk about nine tenths higher of having a baby with a low birth weight, and about four fifths higher of giving birth early.
- Benefit: Aflatoxin is a group 1 carcinogen designated by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, meaning it certainly causes cancer, and it mainly causes liver cancer. A study in Guangzhou measured aflatoxin B1 in home-pressed peanut oil from small workshops between 2010 and 2022. The median was 1.29 μg/kg, and half of the samples fell between 0.12 and 6.58. After the city brought management rules for small workshops into force, the toxin in the oil fell immediately by 2.865 μg/kg, and then by a further 2.593 each year. The share of residents in the area who often eat home-pressed oil with abnormal liver function fell accordingly (PR 0.650, 95% CI 0.469–0.902, credible range), by about 35%. A study in Guangxi that followed 1,611 pregnant women found that 81.7% ate home-pressed peanut oil. Those who ate it had a risk about nine tenths higher of a baby with a low birth weight (aOR 1.9, 1.1–3.2), and about four fifths higher of preterm birth (aOR 1.8, 1.1–3.0).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lei J, Li Y, Wang Y, et al. (2024). The impact of small food workshops management regulations on aflatoxin B1 in home-made peanut oil and the liver function of high-consumption area residents: an interrupted time series study in Guangzhou, China. Frontiers in Public Health, 12. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2024.1484414> ; Zhong Y, Lu H, Jiang Y, et al. (2024). Effect of homemade peanut oil consumption during pregnancy on low birth weight and preterm birth outcomes: a cohort study in Southwestern China. Global Health Action, 17. <https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2024.2336312> ; IARC (2012). Chemical Agents and Related Occupations. IARC Monographs on the Evaluation of Carcinogenic Risks to Humans, Vol 100F. <https://publications.iarc.fr/123> ; National Health and Family Planning Commission (国家卫生计生委), State Food and Drug Administration (国家食品药品监督管理总局) (2017). National Food Safety Standard, Limits for Mycotoxins in Food (GB 2761-2017)
- Notes: Both studies only followed records and had no control group. The endpoint in the Guangzhou study is a liver function test, not liver cancer or death. The median toxin level in home-pressed oil is in fact below the national limit for peanut oil of 20 μg/kg, and the risk comes from eating it over many years and from occasional high batches. The national limit applies to all peanut oil, but prepackaged oil has to pass production licensing and a factory release test, while small workshops are governed by the management rules of each province and are sampled far less often. The people who benefit most from this item are you and the family you eat with.
### 42. Cook with vegetable oil instead of lard and butter, you do not have to shop around between oils for health reasons, and do not expect flaxseed oil to protect your heart
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It does not cost much more. Vegetable oil is already the main cooking fat in most households. The hard part is dropping the habit of lard for frying and for putting over rice.
- In plain language: Cutting back on saturated fats such as lard and butter and switching to vegetable oil cuts heart disease, stroke and events like them by about 17%, but shows no change in overall death risk. There is no evidence that soybean oil, corn oil and rapeseed oil differ from each other. Eating more flaxseed oil showed no drop in heart disease risk either.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 15 randomised trials and 56,675 people, each lasting at least two years. Cutting back on saturated fat lowered cardiovascular events by about 17% (RR 0.83, 0.70–0.98). All-cause mortality was almost unchanged (RR 0.96, 0.90–1.03), and so was cardiovascular death (RR 0.95, 0.80–1.12). Switching to polyunsaturated fat, the main component of soybean oil and corn oil, and switching to carbohydrate made no difference. For switching to monounsaturated fat, the main component of rapeseed oil and olive oil, there is too little data to say anything. A second Cochrane review pooled 19 randomised trials and 6,461 people: eating more omega-6, which is linoleic acid, gave an all-cause mortality of RR 1.00 (0.88–1.12) and a cardiovascular event risk of RR 0.97 (0.81–1.15), showing neither benefit nor harm. A third review pooled 86 randomised trials and 162,796 people: eating more plant-source omega-3, the main component of flaxseed oil and perilla oil, gave an all-cause mortality of RR 1.01 (0.84–1.20) and a coronary heart disease event risk of RR 1.00 (0.82–1.22), which is no measurable effect either way.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, et al. (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (5), CD011737. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011737.pub3> ; Hooper L, Al-Khudairy L, Abdelhamid AS, et al. (2018). Omega-6 fats for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11), CD011094. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011094.pub4> ; Abdelhamid AS, Brown TJ, Brainard JS, et al. (2020). Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (3), CD003177. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub5> ; Chinese Nutrition Society (中国营养学会) (2022). Dietary Guidelines for Chinese Residents (2022). People's Medical Publishing House
- Notes:Disputed: some people argue that the amount of omega-6 in soybean oil and corn oil promotes inflammation, so you should eat less and switch oils. The randomised trials in the omega-6 review above found no harm, and the authors rated the quality of that evidence low themselves. The benefit is graded "medium" on the basis of the 17% drop in cardiovascular events, since all-cause mortality is unchanged. Whatever oil you use, control the quantity: the dietary guidelines recommend 25 to 30 grams of cooking oil per person per day.
