# 22. Relaxing: venues and stress relief

The first half of this section covers which money in an entertainment venue is wasted and where the emergency exits are, measured in money and personal freedom. The second half covers how to take the pressure off, measured in energy and all-cause mortality. The numbers in the two halves are not converted into each other. Section 3 has already set out what to do first when your mood is low, and this section gives the more specific methods.

### 1. Before you go into a KTV room, a bar or an escape room, look for the emergency exit, and change venue if it is locked or blocked
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Look up for 10 seconds when you walk in.
- In plain language: By law, while a venue is trading, the escape routes and emergency exits must be kept clear. They may not be blocked, may not be locked, and may not be piled with things in the way, and the signs may not be covered up. Internet cafés may not seal the doors or bar the windows either. A venue that does not comply is breaking the law, and you can report it to the authorities. Change venue if the private room is underground, if there is only one exit, or if the corridor is full of junk.
- Benefit: This is a statutory duty of the operator. A venue that does not comply is breaking the law, and you can report it to the authorities. The regulation reads: 「营业期间，娱乐场所应当保证疏散通道和安全出口畅通，不得封堵、锁闭疏散通道和安全出口，不得在疏散通道和安全出口设置栅栏等影响疏散的障碍物」, that is, while trading, a venue must keep escape routes and emergency exits clear, may not block or lock escape routes and emergency exits, and may not put up railings or other obstacles that impede escape. The regulation also requires that the venue 「应当在疏散通道和安全出口设置明显指示标志，不得遮挡、覆盖指示标志」, that is, put up clear signs at escape routes and emergency exits and not cover or obscure them. Internet cafés are the same. They are told 「营业期间禁止封堵或者锁闭门窗、安全疏散通道和安全出口」, that is, while trading, blocking or locking doors, windows, escape routes and emergency exits is forbidden, and 「禁止明火照明和吸烟」, that is, open-flame lighting and smoking are forbidden.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2006). Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号, Articles twenty and twenty-one）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>; State Council (国务院) (2002). Regulations on the Administration of Business Premises for Internet Access Services, 互联网上网服务营业场所管理条例（国令第 363 号, Article twenty-one）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>
- Notes: If any one of these is true, change venue: the private room is underground, there is only one exit, the corridor is piled with junk, or the emergency exit sign is unlit. A fire in a place like this means heavy smoke, narrow corridors and many people, and most of them have been drinking. The time you have to get out is measured in seconds. The regulation says the number to report to must be on the warning signs inside the venue.

### 2. Ask for the price list before you order, which venues have to display, and refuse 「play first, pay later」
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask for the list once. The hard part is asking the price in front of other people, which is embarrassing.
- In plain language: By law, a venue selling services and selling goods must display its prices and must show you the price list itself. So-called 「天价酒水」, sky-high drink prices, almost always happen where nobody looked at a price list and the bill was settled on a spoken quote afterwards. Ask for the list when you enter the private room and photograph it. Ask for a confirmed bill after you order. If there is a dispute about the bill, call 12315 there and then.
- Benefit: The regulation reads: 「娱乐场所提供娱乐服务项目和出售商品，应当明码标价，并向消费者出示价目表；不得强迫、欺骗消费者接受服务、购买商品。」, that is, where a venue provides entertainment services and sells goods, it must mark prices clearly and show consumers the price list, and may not force or deceive consumers into accepting services or buying goods. Breaches of price laws and regulations are punished by the competent departments under the law. Disputes over so-called 「天价酒水」 happen for the most part in exactly these settings: no price list shown, and the bill settled on a spoken quote afterwards.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2006). Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号, Articles twenty-nine and fifty-three）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>
- Notes: How to do it: ask for a price list when you enter the private room, paper or electronic, and photograph it as soon as you have it. After ordering, ask for a confirmed bill. If a waiter recommends a 「特调」, a special mix, or a 「果盘」, a fruit platter, always ask the price before ordering. Call 12315 there and then if there is a dispute about the bill. Do not pay first and defend your rights afterwards, because once you have paid, producing the evidence is much harder.

### 3. If someone offers you 「something」 inside the venue, walk out at once; both letting someone use it and supplying it are not doing a friend a favour
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Stand up and walk out. The hard part is leaving the table in front of everybody.
- In plain language: A venue may not sell drugs, may not supply drugs, and may not let anyone use them inside. Breaking that means a suspension of business for 3 to 6 months, and in serious cases the licence is revoked. Using drugs yourself carries administrative punishment. Letting someone 「use it for a moment」 in a private room you have booked, or in your own home, is harbouring another person for drug use, and that is a crime. If somebody produces an unidentified powder, a tablet or a cigarette cartridge and asks you to try it, walk out at once.
- Benefit: The regulation expressly forbids a venue to 「贩卖、提供毒品，或者组织、强迫、教唆、引诱、欺骗、容留他人吸食、注射毒品」, that is, sell or supply drugs, or organise, force, induce, lure, deceive or harbour other people to take or inject drugs. Staff may not use drugs themselves, and may not provide the conditions for any of the above. A breach leads the public security authority to confiscate the illegal gains and illegal property and to order the venue to suspend business for 3 to 6 months. In serious cases the entertainment business licence is revoked. The regulation also requires venues to display, at a prominent place in the hall, in private rooms and in booths, warning signs against drugs, gambling, prostitution and solicitation, and to state the number to report to.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2006). Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, 娱乐场所管理条例（国令第 458 号, Articles fourteen, thirty and forty-two）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>
- Notes: If somebody takes out an unidentified powder, a tablet or an electronic cigarette cartridge and asks you to 「试试」, try it, leave at once and do not stay to watch. The risk you carry personally is heavier than the venue's. Using drugs yourself is in itself subject to administrative punishment. Letting a friend 「use it for a moment」 in a private room you have booked, or in your own home, is harbouring another person for drug use, and that is a crime.

### 4. Do not eat sweets and snacks handed to you by strangers, do not drink a drink that has been out of your sight, and do not take a cigarette cartridge passed to you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money. Turn down everything handed to you. If your own glass has been out of your sight, get a new one.
- In plain language: Drugs today do not look like drugs. A hit-producing electronic cigarette laced with synthetic cannabinoids looks much like an ordinary e-cigarette, and people go round handing them out to try in bars, in KTV rooms and in hotels. Inducing someone to take them carries 10 to 15 days of detention and a fine of 1000 to 5000 yuan, and the person who was deceived into taking them is detained for 10 to 15 days as well. If a drink has been out of your sight, get a new one.
- Benefit: Drugs today do not look like drugs. Synthetic cannabinoids have been listed as a whole class since 2021-7-1, and this large group is now handled as drugs. Some people mix them into the e-liquid to make a hit-producing electronic cigarette. In a case reported by the Supreme People's Procuratorate, investigators described this e-liquid as something that 「可以通过添加食用颜料变成各种颜色、各种口味」, that is, can be turned into any colour and any flavour by adding food colouring, and 「在外观上和普通电子烟基本一致」, that is, looks much the same as an ordinary electronic cigarette. Sellers gather a group and push them in bars, in KTV rooms and in hotels, inducing others to take them. In that report, among the cases of drug use handled locally that year, people who were minors came to nearly forty per cent. The dissociative anaesthetic 依托咪酯 has been in the catalogue of second-category psychotropic drugs since 2023-10-1. Inducing someone to take drugs is in itself unlawful: 「引诱、教唆、欺骗或者强迫他人吸食、注射毒品的，拘留 10 到 15 日并罚 1000 到 5000 元」, that is, anyone who induces, incites, deceives or forces another person to take or inject drugs is detained for 10 to 15 days and fined 1000 to 5000 yuan. Where it amounts to a crime, the sentence is up to 3 years, 3 to 7 years in serious cases and 3 to 10 years where there is coercion, with heavier punishment where the victim is a minor. The consequences fall on the deceived person as well: 「吸食、注射毒品的，处 10 到 15 日拘留并可罚 3000 元以下」, that is, anyone who takes or injects drugs is detained for 10 to 15 days and may be fined up to 3000 yuan. In the lighter cases the penalty is up to 5 days of detention or a fine of up to 1000 yuan. A person may also be ordered not to enter entertainment venues for 6 months to 1 year. An addicted person may be ordered into community detoxification for 3 years. Anyone who refuses community detoxification, or who takes drugs again during it, is sent to compulsory isolated detoxification for 2 years, which may be extended by a further 1 year (nationwide, from month 1 of 2026 under the Public Security Administration Punishments Law).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Procuratorate (最高人民检察院) (2021). Wujiang District, Suzhou, Jiangsu: prosecutors reveal new drugs dressed in trendy clothing. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>; National Medical Products Administration, Ministry of Public Security and National Health Commission (国家药监局、公安部、国家卫生健康委) (2023). Announcement on Adjusting the Catalogue of Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Drugs (2023 No. 120). <https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/qtgzwj/20230911/3b612c94e35b4b63abba314c972a2593.html> (reprinted by the Shanghai Municipal Drug Administration, which records that the original page on nmpa.gov.cn returned 412 on this machine); Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2025). Public Security Administration Punishments Law (2025 revision, Articles eighty-four and eighty-five). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; National People's Congress (全国人大) (1997). Criminal Law (Article three hundred and fifty-three). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2007). Narcotics Control Law (Articles thirty-three, thirty-eight and forty-seven). <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2520706.html> (reprinted by the Guangdong Provincial People's Government).
- Notes: This is not only about entertainment venues. School gates, gatherings with friends and long-distance coaches are the same. Whether what is handed to you is a sweet, a drink or a cigarette cartridge makes no difference. Being drugged is often followed by robbery, sexual assault or blackmail, and there are no published official figures for that, so this is a judgement from experience. Replacing a drink the moment it has been out of your sight saves far more trouble than arguing afterwards. For what to do when somebody in the venue produces an unidentified powder, tablet or cigarette cartridge, see item 3 in this section. For carrying things for other people, see item 29 in section 8.

### 5. Use your own identity card at an internet café, and neither lend yours nor use someone else's
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money.
- In plain language: By law, an internet café must check and register the identity card of each person who gets online, and must record the details of the session, keeping them for at least 60 days. The public security and culture departments can ask for them at any time. While that period runs the café may not alter or delete the records. Whatever you use to get online, what was done on that machine in that period is recorded under that name.
- Benefit: The regulation reads: 「互联网上网服务营业场所经营单位应当对上网消费者的身份证等有效证件进行核对、登记，并记录有关上网信息。登记内容和记录备份保存时间不得少于 60 日」, that is, an operator of a business premises providing internet access services shall check and register the valid documents of the consumers using it, such as their identity cards, and shall record the relevant details of their use. The registration and the backup of the records must be kept for not less than 60 days. When the cultural administration department or the public security authority queries them lawfully, the café must supply them. During the retention period they may not be altered or deleted. So whatever you use to get online, what was done on that machine in that period is recorded under that name.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (国务院) (2002). Regulations on the Administration of Business Premises for Internet Access Services, 互联网上网服务营业场所管理条例（国令第 363 号, Article twenty-three）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>
- Notes: This is the same thing as 「身份证不借人」 in section 9. Internet cafés may not admit minors, and a sign barring minors has to hang at a visible place by the entrance. Entertainment venues likewise do not admit minors. If you help a minor 「刷卡进网吧」, get into an internet café with your own card, the café is fined, but the record stays on your document.

### 6. Do not book murder mystery games or escape rooms set inside a residential building or below the second basement level
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Ask which floor the address is on before you book.
- In plain language: A 2022 notice from five departments states that a script entertainment venue may not be set up inside a residential building, nor below the first underground level. These places are dark, full of partitions and full of flammable props, and the door often only opens once you have solved a puzzle. Once there is a fire, getting out is much harder than from an ordinary shop. Look at the address and the floor on the review page before you book, and change venue if it does not comply.
- Benefit: The dedicated 2022 notice from the five departments is specific: 「剧本娱乐经营场所不得设在居民楼内、建筑物地下一层以下（不含地下一层）等地」, that is, a script entertainment venue may not be set up inside a residential building, below the first underground level of a building (excluding the first underground level itself), or in similar places. The notice also requires venues to meet the requirements of the Work Safety Law and the Fire Protection Law, to 「常态化开展火灾风险自知、自查、自改，提高紧急情况下的组织疏散逃生和初起火灾扑救能力」, that is, routinely identify, inspect and correct fire risks themselves, and improve the ability to organise evacuation and escape in an emergency and to put out a fire in its early stage. The same notice also requires scripts to carry an age suitability notice. A scene unsuitable for minors may not admit minors, and apart from statutory holidays, rest days and the winter and summer holidays, script entertainment activities may not be offered to minors.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, Ministry of Emergency Management and State Administration for Market Regulation (文化和旅游部、公安部、住房和城乡建设部、应急管理部、市场监管总局) (2022). Notice on Strengthening the Management of Script Entertainment Venues, 关于加强剧本娱乐经营场所管理的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-06/27/content_5698021.htm>
- Notes: These places are dark, full of partitions and full of flammable props, and the door often needs a puzzle to be solved before it opens. Once there is a fire, getting out is much harder than from an ordinary shop. You can see the floor, and whether it is a residential building, on the review page before you book. If it does not comply, change venue.

### 7. When your mood is bad, walk or run: the effect size of exercise against depression rises with the intensity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. Half an hour each time. The hard part is that when your mood is worst is exactly when you least want to move, so you have to push yourself.
- In plain language: When your mood is bad, brisk walking or slow running shows the clearest improvement. Next come yoga, strength training and tai chi or qigong, and the differences between them are small. The higher the intensity, the better the effect, and strength training and yoga are the easiest to keep up. The evidence here is of low quality. What can be said is that moving beats not moving, so pick the kind you can keep up.
- Benefit: This is a pooled result across 218 randomised controlled trials. It covers 495 groups and 14170 participants meeting the clinical criteria for severe depression. The method lets the different kinds of exercise compete with one another, in a Bayesian network meta-analysis. The comparison is against usual care and against a placebo tablet as an active control. Effect size is expressed as Hedges' g, and the more negative the number, the greater the improvement in symptoms. Brisk walking or slow running −0.62 (95% confidence interval −0.80 to −0.45, which means the true value falls roughly within this range). Yoga −0.55 (−0.73 to −0.36). Strength training −0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29). Mixed aerobic work −0.43 (−0.61 to −0.24). Tai chi or qigong −0.42 (−0.65 to −0.21). The text says 「运动的效果与所处方的强度成正比」, that is, the effect of exercise is proportional to the intensity prescribed, so the higher the intensity the better the effect. Strength training and yoga were rated the most acceptable and the easiest to keep up.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Noetel M, Sanders T, Gallardo-Gómez D, et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ, 384, e075847. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>
- Notes:Disputed. The authors say so themselves: of the 218 studies, only one was clean enough not to be swayed by its design. On the standard rating scale, brisk walking and slow running count only as "low" certainty, and the rest as "very low". Participants cannot be blinded to exercise, and knowing they are exercising makes them feel better; that expectation cannot be removed. The direction of the conclusion still holds: moving beats not moving, keep the intensity up, and pick the kind you can keep up. This is the expansion of 「情绪低落时先动起来」 in section 3, and it does not replace proper treatment after a diagnosis.

### 8. When anxiety is affecting your life, a mindfulness-based stress reduction course is no worse than the usual anti-anxiety drug
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The course costs a little money. It runs 8 weeks, one session a week, and you practise at home every day. The hard part is practising every day for 8 weeks running.
- In plain language: In people diagnosed with an anxiety disorder, 8 weeks of mindfulness-based stress reduction improves symptoms almost as much as the anti-anxiety drug escitalopram. Nearly eighty per cent of the people on the drug had an adverse reaction, and some dropped out because of it. About fifteen per cent of the people on the course had one, and nobody dropped out. The course has to be a standardised 8-week course; an ordinary meditation app does not count.
- Benefit: The trial was run by academic medical centres in 3 US cities, to see whether the course comes out worse than the drug, in a non-inferiority randomised controlled trial. 276 adults diagnosed with an anxiety disorder were randomised 1:1 into two equal groups. One group took an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course. The other took escitalopram at a flexible dose of 10 to 20 mg. 208 people completed it. At the end, scored on the clinician's rating of anxiety severity (Clinical Global Impression Severity Scale), the mindfulness group fell by 1.35 and the drug group by 1.43. The difference between the two groups is −0.07 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.23, which means the true difference falls roughly within this range; P=0.65, which means this difference cannot be told apart from no difference). The lower end of this range did not cross the line drawn in advance for "not worse" (the non-inferiority margin −0.495). The difference in side effects is large: 78.6% of the drug group had at least one adverse reaction related to the trial, and 10 dropped out because of it. In the mindfulness group the figure was 15.4%, and nobody dropped out.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023). Mindfulness-based stress reduction vs escitalopram for the treatment of adults with anxiety disorders: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13-21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>
- Notes: The trial used the standardised 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course (MBSR), and an ordinary meditation app is not the same thing. It applies to people who already meet the diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder, and if you need a doctor you still need one. If you have been putting off treatment out of worry about drug side effects, this is an option with comparable strength of evidence.

### 9. To settle down in the moment, do 5 minutes of 「cyclic sighing」: a two-stage inhale and a lengthened exhale
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. 5 minutes a day.
- In plain language: 5 minutes of breathing practice a day, kept up for a month, improves mood and slows breathing more than mindfulness meditation of the same length. Of the breathing methods, 「循环叹息」, cyclic sighing, which lengthens the exhale, is the best one. To do it, breathe in through the nose, then take a short top-up breath to fill the lungs, then breathe out slowly through the mouth. This rests on a single trial, where the rating comes from the participant himself, and it costs nothing.
- Benefit: This is a randomised controlled trial carried out at home, comparing three kinds of 5-minute daily breathing practice with mindfulness meditation of the same length over 1 month. The three breathing methods are cyclic sighing, which emphasises a lengthened exhale, box breathing, in which inhaling, holding and exhaling are equal, and cyclic hyperventilation with a hold, in which the inhale is long and the exhale short. Pooling the data across people and across sessions in a mixed-effects model, the breathing practices all did better than mindfulness meditation, and cyclic sighing, which puts the weight on the exhale, did best of all. It was better on mood improvement (P<0.05, which means the difference does not look like it came out by chance) and on the fall in breathing rate (P<0.05).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>
- Notes: To do it, breathe in through the nose, take a short top-up breath to fill the lungs, then breathe out slowly and fully through the mouth, and repeat for 5 minutes. There is only this one trial. It is done at home, whether it works is reported by the person filling in the questionnaire, and the number of people is small. The effect is only a little better than meditation and it does not solve problems. But it costs nothing, takes 5 minutes and can be done anywhere, so it is good value for money.

### 10. Treat 「meeting people regularly」 as a health expense, and do not only reach out when your mood is bad
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A little money, plus the time spent meeting people. The hard part is arranging to see people when your mood is good.
- In plain language: People who rarely see other people have a risk of death over the same period about thirty per cent higher. People who feel lonely are about a quarter higher, and people who live alone about thirty per cent higher, and the pattern is more marked among people under 65. All of these come from following people over time, and it may be that people who are already in poor health are the ones more likely to live alone.
- Benefit: This is a pooled result across 70 studies that first recorded a situation and then followed people forward, a meta-analysis. It covers 3,400,000 participants followed for an average of 7 years. Social isolation, loneliness and living alone are all associated with higher mortality. After taking out confounders such as age and health status, the weighted mean odds ratios are 1.29 for social isolation, 1.26 for loneliness and 1.32 for living alone. Converted, that is a risk of death over the same period roughly thirty per cent, a quarter and thirty per cent higher respectively. The effect sizes of the three are close to one another, and larger among people under 65 than among older people.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227-237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>
- Notes:Disputed. All of this comes from associations observed in groups of people rather than from a controlled trial, so cause and effect cannot be separated. People who are already in poor health are more likely to live alone and to have fewer social contacts. The authors treat social relationships as a health factor of the same order as smoking and obesity, but no randomised trial has shown that increasing social contact lowers mortality. This does not conflict with 「减少让你消耗的人际关系」 in section 3: that one cuts the relationships that drain you, and this one protects the few that restore your energy.

### 11. If you live near green space, make more use of it: walking, a stroll and sitting down all count
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. You do it on the way past.
- In plain language: The better the greenery within 500 metres of your home, the lower the risk of death over the same period. For each 0.1 rise in the vegetation index it is about 4% lower. What is measured is the greenery around the home, not how many times you go to a park. People who live in greener neighbourhoods are often richer, and the air is better as well. So do not read this as going to a park extending your life; it is enough to take the green space around your home into account when you choose where to live.
- Benefit: This is a pooled result across 9 studies that followed groups of people for years, a meta-analysis of cohort studies. It covers 7 countries and 8,320,000 people. What it looks at is the greenery within 500 metres of the home, measured with the Normalised Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI, a single number that converts ground greenery into a figure). For each 0.1 rise in the index, the pooled risk ratio for all-cause mortality is 0.96 (95% CI 0.94 to 0.97), a risk of death over the same period about 4% lower, with the true value falling roughly between 3% lower and 6% lower. 7 of the 9 studies found that greener meant lower mortality, and the difference did not look like chance. The other 2 found no association.
- Evidence grade: A
