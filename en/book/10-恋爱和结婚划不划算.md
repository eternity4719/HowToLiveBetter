# 10. Is dating and marriage worth it

This section breaks 「whether marriage is worth it」 into separate accounts for money, time, health, legal risk and the quality of the relationship, and works them out one at a time. Each account uses only figures whose source can be looked up, and the sources are limited to official statistics or a meta-analysis, which pools the data from several studies and recalculates it. No moral judgement is made here, and no conclusion is drawn on your behalf. The health accounts are worked out on the basis of the risk of death, the time accounts on the basis of minutes per day, and the legal accounts on the basis of what the articles of law say. These three ways of calculating are not converted into one another. For the method of breaking the accounts down, the list to fill in and the common mistakes, see [docs/结婚划不划算.md](../docs/结婚划不划算.md).

### 1. Meet more people rather than fixating on one: whether two people get on cannot be predicted before you meet
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. The time goes on meeting more people, and not on going over the same person again and again. The hard part is holding out against fixating.
- In plain language: Two speed-dating studies had people fill in more than 100 mate-preference questionnaires before they met. The questionnaires can work out who on the whole finds it easy to like other people and who on the whole is well liked; they cannot work out whether there is chemistry between these two particular people. Whether you get on is only known once you have met. So meeting more people is better value than betting everything on one person.
- Benefit: In two speed-dating studies, participants filled in more than 100 self-report scales related to mate preferences before they met, that is, questionnaires they scored themselves on. The researchers used a random forest model, which is one kind of algorithm that looks for patterns. The model can work out 「how easy it is on the whole for a person to like other people」, and these questionnaires account for 4% to 18% of the variance in that, where variance means the difference between one person and another, so the questionnaires only account for a small part of it. The model can also work out 「how popular a person is on the whole」, accounting for 7% to 27%. But it cannot work out 「whether there is chemistry between these two particular people」, and no combination of traits and preferences before the meeting will do it either. The same group of researchers had earlier run a speed-dating experiment. Before the event participants reported their own ideal-partner preferences, such as whether they weigh looks more than economic prospects. That preference does not predict the interest they feel on the day for a particular person
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478-1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580> ; Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245-264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: The two studies only show that it cannot be worked out before you meet. They do not show that no amount of effort is of any use. Whether you get on is only known once you have met, so the time is better spent meeting more people and really seeing each one of them than putting it all on one person. The people in the studies were all university students in Europe and North America. The dating environment in China is different, so discount the figures before applying them to yourself.
### 2. Stop once the other person has clearly refused, because going on pestering them is an administrative offence, not 「sincerity」
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No money, and no time. The hard part is holding out: no more messages, and no more waiting outside.
- In plain language: The other person has clearly said no, and you go on sending messages and going to wait for them, and from 2026-1 that is an administrative offence. It carries up to 5 days of detention, or a fine of up to 1000 yuan. Where the circumstances are relatively serious, 5 to 10 days of detention. The police can also order you not to have contact with the other person, and breaching that order carries a further 5 to 10 days of detention.
- Benefit: The Law on Penalties for Administration of Public Security, in force from 2026-1-1, lists pestering and following as a separate offence. The article says: 「多次发送淫秽、侮辱、恐吓等信息或者采取滋扰、纠缠、跟踪等方法，干扰他人正常生活的」. This conduct is punishable by up to five days' detention, or a fine of up to one thousand yuan. Where the circumstances are relatively serious, it is punishable by five to ten days of detention, and a fine of up to one thousand yuan may be added. This item has one more layer: with the approval of the person in charge of the public security organ, the behaviour may be ordered to stop and the person may be barred from having contact with the victim for a set period. Breaching the no-contact order carries a further five to ten days of detention
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全国人大常委会) (2025, revised). Law of the People's Republic of China on Penalties for Administration of Public Security, 治安管理处罚法（第五十条第一款第五项、第二款）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (reprinted by the Supreme People's Procuratorate)
- Notes: There is no research behind the belief that 「pursuing somebody to the end will move them」, but the detention and the no-contact order are in black and white. The record left behind can also affect sitting the civil service examination, sitting a public institution post, political review, and the professional qualifications required for some jobs. The line between pursuing somebody and harassing them does not depend on what you think, it depends on whether the other person has clearly said no.
### 3. To judge whether the other person is interested, look at behaviour and not at 「signals」: who starts it, who arranges the next one, who follows up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money, and not much time. The hard part is changing the habit: stop reading the other person's expressions and tone, and start recording facts instead.
- In plain language: 52 pairs of strangers were set talking for about ten minutes and then each judged whether the other person was flirting. The result is that 「the other person was not flirting」 is judged fairly accurately, while 「the other person was flirting」 cannot be picked up. Changing to 261 people watching videos to make the judgement gives the same conclusion. So stop guessing at expressions and tone, and look at behaviour: who starts it, who arranges the next meeting, and whether the other person follows up when you do not push.
- Benefit: Two studies measured how accurately people can identify flirting. The first had 52 pairs of strangers who did not know each other talk in pairs for 10 to 12 minutes. Afterwards each reported whether they themselves had been flirting and whether they thought the other person had been. The result is that 「没有发生调情的互动被判断得更准，发生了调情的互动反而判断得更差」. The second used 26 one-minute videos and 261 observers to make the judgement, and reached the same conclusion. People can tell reasonably reliably that 「the other person was not flirting」, but have a hard time telling that 「the other person was flirting」
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939-958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972> ; Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70-77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727> (disputed side)
- Notes:Disputed. Other research holds that it is not that men overestimate women's sexual interest, but that women underreport their own intentions when filling in a questionnaire. Who is the more accurate judge is still unsettled. But both sides agree on one thing: inferring another person's thoughts backwards from small cues such as expression and tone has a low accuracy rate. Visible behaviour is far more reliable: whether the other person starts things, whether they arrange the next meeting, and whether they follow up when you do not push.
### 4. Whether a relationship is good depends mainly on how you feel in it, not on the other person's circumstances
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money, and not much time. The hard part is changing where you look: put the checklist for choosing a partner away and watch how things go instead.
- In plain language: What says most about whether a relationship is good is your own feeling in it, not the other person's circumstances. Such as whether you feel the other person is serious and whether they appreciate you, and how often the two of you argue. Your own feeling is between two and four times as good a predictor as what your partner says. But whether the relationship gets better or worse later is something no questionnaire can really work out.
- Benefit: A machine learning analysis of 29 laboratories and 43 longitudinal couples datasets, where longitudinal means following the same couples and recording them all the way through. The items that predicted the quality of the relationship best all came from how the people themselves felt about the relationship: feeling the other person's commitment, feeling appreciated, sexual satisfaction, feeling that the other person is satisfied, and conflict. The strongest of the items about the individual were life satisfaction, negative mood, depression, avoidant attachment and anxious attachment. At the start of the study these items about the relationship explained at most 45% of the variance, where variance means the difference in relationship quality between one couple and another, and 45% is the part of it these items can account for. By the end of the study they explained at most 18%. And the predictive power of 「the items people report themselves」 is between two and four times that of 「the items the partner reports」. Adding individual differences and the partner's reports on top of your own reported feelings about the relationship adds no predictive power at all
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061-19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: The same study also found that whether the relationship got better or worse during the study could not really be worked out from any set of self-report measures in the questionnaire. So for judging whether the relationship in front of you is good, asking yourself how you feel is more accurate than checking the other person's list of circumstances. Do not expect to be able to predict how it will turn out later. For the account that ties how a relationship is going to how your health is going, see item 17 in this section (treat how a relationship is going as a health account).

### 5. To close the distance, take turns answering a set of questions that go from light to deep, and do not learn scripts
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. It takes 45 minutes.
- In plain language: Two strangers take turns answering 36 questions that go from light to deep, and after 45 minutes they are much closer than people who only chatted for the same length of time. What does the work is taking turns telling the truth and going a layer at a time deeper, not any script. This only shows that they were closer at the time, not that it can grow into a long-term relationship.
- Benefit: Strangers were paired up and made to take turns answering three sets of questions that get progressively deeper, 36 questions in all. The questions run from 「who would you most like to invite to dinner」 to 「when was the last time you cried in front of somebody else」. Closeness after 45 minutes was significantly higher than in the control group, which chatted for the same length of time. It was also higher than the average rating that students in that study gave for the most intimate relationship they actually have
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363-377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: The experiment was only run once, on university students, and it only measured closeness over a short period. It did not measure whether the relationship can grow into a long-term one. The closeness in the experiment came from two people taking turns and going a layer at a time deeper into their own real situation, not from any script, any routine or one side performing. So when you talk, you can 「say something true yourself, and ask one question just as deep」, taking turns on both sides.
### 6. Long distance is not worse than living in the same city, and the real hurdle is the first three months after moving in together
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: A fair amount of money. You have to pay for travel, and pay for the time on the way there and back. You meet less often than people in the same city. The hard part is coping for a long time with not seeing each other.
- In plain language: A long-distance relationship is in fact steadier while you are apart, because you see each other less and it is easy to think of the other person as better than they really are. The real hurdle comes after you have moved in together. In the tracking data about half of long-distance couples reached the point of being in the same city, and the other half ended while they were still apart. Of the half that reunited, one third broke up within 3 months.
- Benefit: Two studies reach the same conclusion. A long-distance relationship is steadier than one in the same city while the two are apart. The reason is that the degree to which a long-distance couple imagines the other person to be better than they really are is markedly higher, and the studies measured idealisation distortion, romantic love, relationship memories and perceived agreement. And the less you meet, the better the picture. But that steadiness only holds while the two are still apart, and once they are in the same city, long-distance couples break up more easily. The proportion given by the study that tracked couples moving from long distance to the same city is this: about half of long-distance couples reached the point of moving in together, and the other half ended while they were apart. Of the half that reunited, one third broke up within 3 months of the reunion. The longer the gap between meetings while they were apart, and the stronger the idealisation, the less steady they are after the reunion
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901-919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472> ; Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37-54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578> ; Dargie E et al. (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181-202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367> (the long-distance study referred to in the notes)
- Notes: The people in these studies were dating relationships among university students in the United States, not marriages, and the studies are fairly old, so do not apply the absolute proportions directly. Another study surveyed 717 people long distance and 425 people in the same city. The difference in the quality of the two kinds of relationship was very small, and whether a relationship is good depends mainly on the two people's own circumstances and on the relationship itself. So when deciding whether to go long distance, do not use 「long distance always falls apart」 as your reason. What needs preparing in advance is the few months after the reunion, when there is less room to do things independently, more is known about each other, and conflict and jealousy both increase.

### 7. Read the registration data before you listen to your elders: what the marriage rate and the divorce rate each measure
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Read the marriage registration section of the Ministry of Civil Affairs' annual bulletin, which takes a few minutes.
- In plain language: In 2024 there were 6,106,000 marriage registrations nationwide, about one fifth fewer than the year before, and 3,513,000 divorces. The claim online that the divorce rate is over half takes these two figures and divides one by the other, which comes to about 57.5%. But the people divorcing and the people marrying are not the same people, so it cannot be read as half of marriages ending in divorce. The marriage rate of 4.3‰ and the divorce rate of 2.5‰ in the bulletin are worked out by dividing by the national population.
- Benefit: In 2024 there were 6,106,000 marriages registered nationwide in accordance with the law, 20.5% fewer than the year before, and a marriage rate of 4.3 per thousand. In the same year there were 3,513,000 divorces completed in accordance with the law, of which 2,622,000 were divorces registered with the civil affairs departments and 891,000 were divorces granted by a court through judgment or mediation, and a divorce rate of 2.5 per thousand. The bulletin states that the marriage (divorce) rate = the number of marriages (divorces) in the year / the average total population in the year × 1,000‰. This is a 「crude rate」: the denominator is the whole population, not the married population
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs (民政部) (2025). 2024 年民政事业发展统计公报（五（二）1 婚姻登记服务、注释 5），网页版与 PDF 版. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html> ; <https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>
- Notes: The 「divorce rate over 50%」 you often see online is the number of divorces in the year divided by the number of marriages in the year, 351.3 / 610.6 ≈ 57.5%. This is called the 「divorce-to-marriage ratio」. The two figures being divided are not about the same group of people, and the bulletin has no such indicator, so it cannot be read as 「half of marriages end in divorce」. To work out 「the probability that a particular marriage ends in divorce」, you have to follow the same group of married people all the way through, and this section found no such official data.
### 8. Count the health benefit, but discount it because the data is observational
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money, and no time. You only have to remember one thing: these figures say that married people have a lower mortality rate, which is two things happening at once, not that marriage makes you live longer.
- In plain language: The chance of dying in the same period is about 12% lower for married people than for the unmarried, and about 24% higher for people who have never married than for married people. But this is only two things happening at once, not an effect of getting married. People who are healthy, earn more and have more social contact are both more likely to marry and more likely to live longer. These figures come almost entirely from Europe and North America, and for use in China they are a sense of scale at best.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis on older people, which pools several studies and recalculates them, covered 53 comparisons and more than 250,000 people in all. The all-cause mortality risk ratio for married people relative to unmarried people was RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.85 to 0.91, where RR is the risk ratio and CI is the credible range, so the chance of dying in the same period is about 12% lower. In the same analysis, widowed was 1.11, divorced or separated 1.16 and never married 1.11, which are about 11%, 16% and 11% higher respectively. There is no difference between men and women. The most conservative estimate is RR 0.94, about 6% lower. A second meta-analysis compared never married with married, using 95 articles and more than 500 million people. The result was HR 1.24 (1.19 to 1.30), so the chance of dying in the same period is about 24% higher. And the older the age group, the smaller the gap. A further meta-analysis split by sex, covering 21 studies and more than 7,890,000 individuals, found that for both men and women being unmarried went together with all-cause mortality, cancer mortality and cardiovascular mortality. On the men's side the gap in cardiovascular mortality and in all-cause mortality is larger. Unmarried men, relative to unmarried women, had 31% more stroke deaths and 9% more all-cause deaths
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Soc Sci Med 64:77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031> ; Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. Am J Epidemiol 174(4):379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111> ; Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Glob Health Res Policy 5:4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes:Disputed. What the dispute is about is that married people were selected from the start: people who are healthy, earn more and have more social contact are both more likely to marry and more likely to live longer. All three meta-analyses use observational data on existing groups, that is, records that were followed but not split into a control and a treatment group. The authors themselves write about publication bias and methodological limits. Roelfs also found that the more rigorous the study, the smaller the gap that comes out. These figures are mostly about European and North American populations, and for China they are a sense of scale at best.
### 9. Work out the time account from unpaid work, and settle the division of tasks before you register
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Spend an hour or two before the wedding turning the unpaid work, such as housework, looking after the old and the young, and whose job will give way, into a clear division of tasks, and then work to it. The hard part is laying it out and talking it through rather than letting it drag.
- In plain language: In the national time use survey, unpaid work such as housework and looking after family takes men 1 hour 52 minutes a day on average and women 3 hours 29 minutes. The survey does not break the figures down by whether people are married, so there is no official figure for 「how much more housework there is after marriage」, and this gender gap can only be used as an upper limit. The division of tasks can be negotiated into a different shape before you register.
- Benefit: The 2024 National Time Use Survey. Unpaid work means things like housework and looking after family that bring no pay. Among the people who did this kind of work on the day, it took 2 hours 45 minutes a day on average, of which men accounted for 1 hour 52 minutes and women 3 hours 29 minutes. The share of people who did this kind of work during the day is 67.5% for men and 83.9% for women. Housework among residents averages 1 hour 17 minutes a day, and 1 hour 59 minutes counting only the people who did it, which is 28 minutes less than in 2018. Looking after and caring for family averages 30 minutes a day, and 1 hour 46 minutes counting only the people who did it. The 2018 figures are: housework 45 minutes for men and 2 hours 6 minutes for women, and looking after and caring for children 17 minutes for men and 53 minutes for women
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics (国家统计局) (2024). 第三次全国时间利用调查公报（第二号、第三号）. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html> ; <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html> ; National Bureau of Statistics (国家统计局) (2019). 2018 年全国时间利用调查公报. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: Both surveys only break the figures down by sex, by urban or rural area and by age, and not by whether people are married. So there is no official figure for 「how much more housework there is after marriage」, and the gender gap can only be used as an upper limit. The figures are national averages, and the two of you can quite well negotiate a different division of tasks.
### 10. Start with the default rules in the law for the money, then decide whether to put them in writing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Read the articles on property in the marriage and family book of the Civil Code. If you need to, the two of you sign a written property agreement; the law only requires it to be in writing, and there is no need to have it notarised. The hard part is saying the money things out in advance.
- In plain language: Without a written agreement, the law treats the wages and bonuses earned after the wedding, income from running a business and from investment, and property inherited or received as a gift, as jointly owned by the two of you. Property owned before the wedding, and property that a will or a gift contract says goes to one of you alone, is personal property. The two of you can agree in writing that everything stays separate, and once you have signed it binds you. The party who put in more work bringing up children and looking after the elderly can ask for compensation on divorce.
- Benefit: The default rules where nothing else is agreed are these: wages, bonuses and remuneration for labour earned after the wedding, income from production, business operations and investment, income from intellectual property, and property inherited or received as a gift, belong jointly to the couple (Article one thousand and sixty-two). Property owned before the wedding, compensation for personal injury, property that a will or a gift contract makes clear goes to one of them alone, and articles for personal use are personal property (Article one thousand and sixty-three). The two of them may agree in writing that property before and after the wedding belongs to each of them separately, belongs to both jointly, or is partly separate and partly joint, and that agreement has legal force for both (Article one thousand and sixty-five). The party who has taken on more obligations by bringing up children, looking after the elderly or helping the other party with their work has the right to ask for compensation on divorce (Article one thousand and eighty-eight)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (全国人民代表大会) (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, 中华人民共和国民法典（2021 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第一千零六十二、一千零六十三、一千零六十五、一千零八十八条）, National Database of Laws and Regulations. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf> ; full text of the articles reprinted in the Supreme People's Court Gazette. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>
- Notes: There are no official figures for bride price, for the wedding or for the marital home, so this item gives no numbers. How much the compensation under Article one thousand and eighty-eight comes to is first for the two of you to talk about; if that fails, the court decides it, and there is no fixed formula. A written property agreement works for both sides: the higher earner has a use for it, and so does the party who does a lot of the unpaid work of housework and care.
### 11. When your parents put money towards a flat, write down at the point of the transfer whether it is a loan or a gift
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. On the day the money goes out, write a single page saying whether it is a gift to you or a loan to you, and have both the parents who put the money in and the children sign it. If it says a loan, have both of you sign it too, and write the same sentence in the transfer reference.
- In plain language: Money from parents before the wedding is by default for their own child. Money put in after the wedding with no agreement counts as joint marital property. From 2025-2, where one party's parents paid the whole amount after the wedding and did not state that it was only for their own child, the court can award the flat to the child who provided the funding on divorce and then decide whether the other side is to be compensated. If it is to be a loan, write it down on the day and have both sides sign.
- Benefit: Where parents contributed towards a home for the couple before the wedding, it is treated as a gift to their own child alone. Where the parents have clearly stated that the gift is to both, that is excluded. Money put in after the wedding is dealt with by agreement. Where there is no agreement, or the agreement is unclear, it is dealt with on the principle of joint marital property. There is one more rule from 2025-2-1. Where during the marriage one party's parents paid the whole amount and the gift contract does not say it is only for their own child, then on the division of property at divorce the court may award the flat to the child of the person who provided the funding. Once it has been awarded, several factors are weighed together to decide whether the other side is to be compensated and how much. Those factors are whether the couple lived together and had children together, the fault that led to the divorce, the size of each party's contribution to the family, and the market price of the flat at the time of the divorce. Where one party's parents put in part of the money, or where both sets of parents put money in, the flat is awarded to one of the parties on the basis of the source and the proportion of the funding, with reasonable compensation (nationwide)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court (2020). ### 12. A large sum your spouse borrowed alone does not become your debt if you neither signed nor afterwards approved it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Do not sign as a 「joint borrower」 or 「guarantor」 on your spouse's IOU or guarantee. And do not write things like 「we will repay this money」 in a chat, because that can be read as your having afterwards approved the debt. The hard part is saying nothing when you are being pressed.
- In plain language: Money your spouse borrowed behind your back does not automatically become your debt, as long as it clearly goes beyond the household's everyday spending and you neither signed nor later accepted it. The person owed the money has to prove that it was used for life together or for a business run together. But money spent on a flat, where that really did become a home you live in together or share, is a level the other side can often prove.
- Benefit: Debts incurred in the following situations are joint marital debts. First, where both spouses signed. Second, where one spouse afterwards approved it, that is, accepted the debt afterwards, so that both of you acknowledge it. Third, where during the marriage one spouse borrowed in their own name for the everyday needs of the family. Money borrowed in one spouse's own name that clearly goes beyond the household's everyday spending is not a joint marital debt. The exception is where the creditor, the party owed the money, can prove that the money was used for life together or for joint production and business operations, or where both of you acknowledge the debt. Money spent on a flat clearly goes beyond the everyday needs of the household, and the burden of proof lies with the party claiming the debt, so the person owed the money has to produce the evidence. But if that money really did become a home the couple lives in together or shares, the creditor can often reach the level of 「used for life together」. So the step that does more good is the one in item 11 in this section (writing down whether the money from parents is a loan or a gift) (nationwide, in force from 2021)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (全国人大) (2020). Civil Code, 民法典（第一千零六十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> ; Supreme People's Court (2020, second amendment). - Notes: When property is divided at a divorce and the other side's parents suddenly produce a loan agreement signed only by your spouse, there are two steps. First the size of the loan, how the money was handed over, and the two parties' financial positions, and then how you normally do transactions, whether there is a matching change in the property, and what the witnesses say. Where the other side can produce nothing but a transfer receipt, it is still for them to prove that the loan exists. The second step, once the loan exists, is to ask whether it is a joint debt. ### 13. To register you only need your identity card and a signed statement; you do not need the household register, you pay nothing, and the certificate is issued on the spot

### 15. Work out the cost of getting out: a divorce by agreement has a 30-day cooling-off period, and a divorce by litigation has statutory conditions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Before the wedding, spend some time reading: if you do have to get out, how long it takes and what procedure it follows. The hard part is thinking about splitting up before you have married.
- In plain language: A divorce by agreement is not finished once it is signed. Within 30 days of submitting the application, either party may withdraw it. At the end of that period you also have to collect the certificate in person within 30 days, and not doing so counts as withdrawing. If the other side does not agree, you can only sue. The court tries mediation first, and where there is domestic violence, bigamy, or two years of living apart, and mediation fails, it grants the divorce.
- Benefit: A divorce by agreement requires a written divorce agreement, and both parties must apply in person at the marriage registration authority (Article one thousand and seventy-six). Within 30 days from the day the registration authority receives the application, either party may withdraw it. Within 30 days after that period ends, both parties must collect the divorce certificate in person, and not collecting it is treated as withdrawal (Article one thousand and seventy-seven). Where one side does not agree, the route is litigation, and the court should try mediation first. Where there is bigamy or cohabiting with another person, domestic violence or abuse and abandonment, repeated gambling or drug use that has not been corrected, or two years of living apart because of a breakdown in the relationship, and mediation has no effect, the divorce is to be granted. Where a divorce was refused and the two have since lived apart for another year, a fresh suit is to be granted (Article one thousand and seventy-nine). In 2024 there were 3,513,000 divorces nationwide, of which 891,000 went through a court judgment or mediation

### 16. Keep a separate account for 「marrying because of the older generation」, and do not mix it with your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: No money. Draw two columns on a piece of paper. One column is which benefits in this marriage are your own, which the earlier items in this section can work out. The other is which benefits belong to the older generation: peace of mind, face, and the pressure to marry stopping. The hard part is keeping the older generation's column separate and not merging it into your own.
- In plain language: The benefits that go to the older generation, that is peace of mind, face and no more being nagged, are real. But they do not automatically become your health benefit or your relationship quality. The time account, the money account and the cost of getting out, on the other hand, are all borne by you. Draw two columns on paper and write out which benefits are yours and which belong to the older generation, and whether the deal is worth it is obvious at a glance.
- Benefit: The benefit to the older generation is real. But it does not automatically turn into your health benefit (item 8) or into relationship quality (item 4, whether a relationship is good depends on how you feel in it). And the time account (item 9), the money account (items 10 to 12) and the cost of getting out (item 15) are all borne by the parties themselves. Once the books are kept separately, whether 「marrying to complete a task」 is good value is something you can see for yourself

### 17. Treat how a relationship is going as a health account: in an argument, argue about the thing and not the person, and do not insult or sneer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money. In an argument, stick to the thing in front of you, do not insult, do not sneer and do not bring up old grievances. The hard part is keeping your mouth shut while you are angry.
- In plain language: Couples with a good relationship are somewhat healthier and have a somewhat lower risk of death. The association is small, on a par with the association between diet and health. In one experiment, couples who stayed hostile throughout their fights healed the small wounds the experiment produced at only six tenths the rate of couples who stayed calm.
- Benefit: Pooling 126 studies and more than 72,000 people, which is what a meta-analysis does. The better the quality of the marriage, the better the health, with a correlation coefficient r between .07 and .21. A correlation coefficient of 0 is no relationship at all and 1 is changing in perfect step. The correlation with a lower risk of death is r = .11. A smaller cardiovascular reaction during conflict is r = -.13. The authors say that all these effects are small, on a par with the association between health habits such as diet and health. In another crossover experiment, 42 healthy couples each stayed in hospital twice, for 24 hours each time. Once they had a mutually supportive conversation, and once they discussed a subject on which they disagreed. After the argument, the blister wounds the experiment produced healed more slowly. For the couples who showed more hostility on both occasions, the wounds healed at only 60% the rate of the couples with low hostility. On the morning of the day after the argument, their blood markers of inflammation (IL-6, tumour necrosis factor) had also risen more.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Robles TF, Slatcher RB, Trombello JM, McGinn MM (2014). Marital quality and health: a meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031859> ; Kiecolt-Glaser JK et al. (2005). Hostile marital interactions, proinflammatory cytokine production, and wound healing. Archives of General Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.62.12.1377>
- Notes: The reason for grade B is that most of the pooled studies are only records that were followed, with no control group. So it cannot be told whether a bad relationship makes you ill or poor health makes the relationship worse. In some studies the association is still there after age and income are taken out. Robles also states in the original paper that some of the results are subject to publication bias. The studies that compared men and women explicitly found basically no difference between the sexes. This section found no pooled analysis directly comparing the mortality of a 「marriage with a bad relationship」 with not marrying at all, so 「a bad marriage does more harm than not marrying」 can only be an inference. The wound experiment has only 42 couples in it. 「Argue about the thing and not the person, do not insult and do not sneer」 is a practice the author worked out from the 「hostile behaviour」 in the experiment, and no study compares it directly. Where the arguments are fierce and cannot be changed, the two of you can go together to couple therapy, see item 20 in this section (couple therapy). Where it has come to hitting, or to long-term verbal abuse and threats, that is no longer a matter of communication but of domestic violence: see item 43 in section 8 (if you are being abused at home). For deciding whether to go through with it, see item 15 in this section (the cost of getting out).



### 18. Same-sex partners: while you are both clear-headed, get the power of attorney, the voluntary guardianship and the will done. In law you are not close relatives, and without these documents you have no right to sign and no right to inherit

### 19. Do not marry while concealing your sexual orientation, and do not treat a marriage in name only as the easy way out: in law it is a real marriage, and the only way out is a divorce
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. The hard part is standing up to the pressure from the family to marry, which may take years to bear.
- In plain language: Marrying while concealing your sexual orientation is not void in law and cannot be annulled, so the only way out is a divorce. A marriage in name only is a real marriage as well, and the money earned and the debts incurred after it count as the couple's. The side that was kept in the dark can, on divorce, ask for a larger share of the property and for compensation for mental harm, but whether the court agrees is not settled.
- Benefit: Article one thousand and fifty-one of the Civil Code says a marriage is void in only three situations: bigamy, a relationship of close relation that is forbidden for marriage, and not having reached the legal marriageable age. Article seventeen of Interpretation (One) on the Marriage and Family Book of the Civil Code provides that where a request is made to confirm a marriage void on any ground other than those three, the court rules to reject it. So the only way out is a divorce. A marriage in name only is a lawful marriage once registered. Article one thousand and sixty-two provides that wages, bonuses and remuneration for labour after the wedding are joint marital property. Article one thousand and sixty-four provides that a debt signed for jointly by the two, and a debt one of them took on for the everyday needs of the household, are joint marital debts. Where the agreement says that compensation is given up, a later suit will not be supported. The person who benefits first is the spouse who was kept in the dark, and second is yourself: once the concealment is found out, the divorce, the division of property and the custody of any children all arrive together.

### 20. When a relationship is stuck in repeated arguments and cold shoulders, the two of you go to couple therapy together: waiting on your own will not get better on its own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: It costs money. Each session is charged separately, the prices differ from one hospital to another, and this item gives no figures. In the studies it is generally once a week, and one trial ran for about 8 months from start to finish. The hard part is that both of you have to go, and that you have to keep going to the end.
- In plain language: Couples stuck in a bad patch who finish couple therapy together are markedly more satisfied with the relationship, and about seven in ten improve. Couples who only sit on the waiting list change very little. But the effect fades: followed to the fifth year, about half are still clearly better and about a quarter have already separated or divorced.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis in 2020 pooled 58 studies and 2092 couples. After couple therapy, the change in relationship satisfaction was g = 1.12 (credible range 0.92–1.31), which the authors call a large effect. g is an effect size, see the glossary in the README. For the couples allocated to the waiting list, over the same period g = 0.12 (−0.04 to 0.29), with no clear improvement. The worse the relationship was at the start, the more it improved. A review in 2012 puts it at 70% of the couples who took the treatment improving. Another randomised trial took 134 couples with long-standing and serious discord and gave them about 8 months of one of two behavioural couple therapies. At the end the change in satisfaction was d = 0.90 and 0.71. By year 5, 50.0% and 45.9% of the couples respectively were still clearly better. What the authors mean by clearly better, they call 「an improvement of clinical significance」. At the same time 25.7% and 27.9% had already separated or divorced
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)

- Notes: Disputed. The dispute is over how long the effect lasts. In another meta-analysis that included only randomised trials, at the end g = 0.60 and after six months 0.44. At 12 months only the behavioural couple therapy still has data, g = 0.06, and almost nothing has been kept. Another review pooling 20 meta-analyses says an effect of clinical significance appears in 40% to 50% of people. 「Seven in ten improve」 and 「four to five in ten clearly improve」 use different criteria, so do not compare them. The five-year trial had no untreated control group, so it cannot be said whether divorces went up or down. The 2020 synthesis covered only opposite-sex couples, and this item found no randomised trial in China either, so discount it before you apply it to yourself. Where to go in China: the Mental Health Law requires psychotherapy to be done inside a medical institution, and a psychological counsellor may not carry out psychotherapy. You can ask the psychiatry department or the psychology department of a hospital whether they do couples therapy. When you look for an organisation outside a hospital, first ask what qualifications the people have and what training they have had. If there is domestic violence, do not go together first. The 2016 meta-analysis states that its conclusion applies only to mild to moderate violence between the two of you arising out of conflict. Violence characterised by controlling the other person or by serious harm is not covered. People in the field also worry about revenge after the sessions. If you have been hit, see item 43 in section 8 (if you are being abused at home) first. For how to talk during an argument see item 17 in this section (argue about the thing and not the person), and for deciding whether to go through with it see item 15 in this section (the cost of getting out). The people who benefit are you and your spouse.
